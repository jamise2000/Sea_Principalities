# Merrick

The son of an admiral and one of the finest scouts and navigators in the Monmurg fleet — a quiet, dutiful man of the sea who reads coastline and current like others read a page. Lord Jamis's trusted eyes on the water, and, though Jamis would never say it aloud, the man he considers the best of the Principality.

## Physical Description

Merrick is strong and capable without being a brute — a lean, weathered man of the coast rather than a bruiser, built for endurance and quickness over raw force. He is very dexterous and deadly with a bow, and he moves with a stealth that seems almost unnatural: sent ahead through moonlit terrain, he melts into the shadows, reading the contours of a shoreline like a man who has walked it a thousand times, silent even over open ground.

His gifts are less visible than physical. He carries the keen senses of a lifelong hunter and sailor — sharp perception, an instinct for danger, an unhurried watchfulness. Where his cousin Owen is all easy smiles and loose talk, Merrick is the serious, brooding one, quiet and observant, the man in any room most likely to already know something the others don't. He looks, in short, exactly like what he is: a disciplined navy scout who misses very little.

## History

Merrick was born in Monmurg, that city built on the spoils of piracy and trade, into a family of seasoned sailors. His father, Captain Nado, was a respected navigator — and an admiral — in the Monmurgian navy, a true man of the sea with salt in his veins and a deep, abiding hatred of the sahaugin, the shark-men who once terrorized the Sea Principalities. Nado filled his son's childhood with tales of the battles fought against those creatures, and in doing so planted in Merrick a lasting vigilance and a fierce desire to protect his homeland from whatever might rise out of the deep.

He grew up a child of the coast, roaming the rocky shores and tide pools, while his father taught him the ways of both fisherman and soldier — tracking, survival, combat, and above all the reading of the signs of land and sea. He became an expert in the treacherous waters around Monmurg and the Outer Islands, his keen senses and deep understanding of the natural world making him an invaluable asset to the navy, where he quickly made a name for himself. His life turned on a single night: on a routine patrol chasing rogue privateers, his captain tried to thread the deadly waters near "The Scimitars" in the dark and found the task beyond him. Only Merrick's quick thinking and intimate knowledge of the coastline brought the ship and crew through alive. The feat won him the respect of his peers and, more importantly, the attention of Lord Jamis, commodore of the Monmurg fleet.

Under Jamis's mentorship his career flourished. He was given command of a small detachment patrolling the waters around Helm Island, the strategic linchpin of the Strait of Monmurg, where his father's teachings and his own gifts kept the sea lanes safe for the traders and travelers passing through — he served garrison duty there under Jamis's command, and came to know its barracks, its colored lighthouses, and its roads well, though he never set foot inside the fortress itself. Through all of it, one question never left him: the shark-men had been beaten long ago, but were they truly gone, or only waiting? On the Helm Island expedition that question is answered in the worst way. Merrick watches a full ship overrun by returning sahaugin, finds strange tracks on the southern shore, helps take the beacon tower through a hobgoblin ambush, and shepherds Paul Revero safely through the assault on Fairwind's keep — guarding the prince's nephew, keeping Gouge in line, and, when the mission collapses, building the makeshift raft that carries the survivors home with four barrels of the precious anti-sahaugin powder. He reports it all, plainly and completely, to Lord Jamis.

## Personality Traits

Merrick is steady, disciplined, and dutiful to his core. He is not, by his own reckoning, a master strategist, but he is wise well beyond his years, and both his wisdom and his agility come from instinct rather than calculation — a feel for people, politics, and danger as sure as his feel for the tide. He carries himself with a quiet seriousness that reads to some as brooding, and he is deeply discreet: entrusted with state secrets like the true nature of the powder, he guards them without being asked twice, and he takes visible care never to let slip what he knows to the wrong ears.

He is a careful, shrewd judge of the people around him. Debriefing with Jamis, he weighs each companion honestly — crediting Jude as a brilliant, indispensable asset while recognizing him as hotheaded and self-directed, and reading Gouge as a man to be trusted only so long as one never crosses him. He values understanding the hidden web of loyalties precisely because it gives him an advantage others lack, and he prefers to keep his own allegiances quiet while he learns everyone else's. Yet for all his watchfulness he is fundamentally honorable — Jamis calls his work commendable, even Lord Fairwind respects him — and his loyalty, once given, is not the sort that wavers.

That honor sits easily beside a grounded humility. He defers to Jamis without servility, asks sensible questions, and worries about the men left behind as sitting ducks on the Helm. He is the reliable center of any group he travels with: the one who scouts the safe path, holds the plan together, and gets everyone home.

## Motivations

Merrick's guiding purpose is protection — of Monmurg, of its waters, and of the traders and travelers who depend on the straits being safe. It is a duty he inherited directly from his father, and it shapes everything from his patrols to his instinct to get Paul Revero out alive above all else. Bound up in it is the older, more personal dread his father planted: his lifelong vigilance against the sahaugin, the childhood warning that has now come terribly true. Their return is, for him, both vindication and summons; he even asks Jamis for a small measure of the powder to keep on his own person, so that he is never again caught defenseless against the deep.

Beneath that runs his loyalty to Lord Jamis, the mentor who raised him from a promising scout to a trusted agent, and the quiet weight of his father's legacy. Jamis holds Merrick in the same high regard he once held Captain Nado, seeing in the son the very best of the Principality — and though Jamis will never say so to his face, Merrick clearly feels the pull to be worthy of both the man who taught him and the man who now relies on him.

Finally, he is driven by a need to understand — to read the currents of politics as clearly as the currents of the sea. In a world where powerful, dangerous people surround him, Merrick's edge is knowledge: knowing who serves whom, who can be trusted and how far, and keeping his own counsel while he does. It is how a man who is neither the strongest nor the most cunning in the room nonetheless survives it, and keeps the people he's responsible for alive.
