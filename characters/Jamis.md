# Lord Jamis

Commodore of the fleet of Monmurg, commander of the Harbor District, and — in everything but title — Prince Jeon II's spymaster. The most influential of the prince's advisors and the hidden hand behind much of the party's work, Jamis is the man who moves knives like Gouge and Jude across the board while never being seen in the same room as them twice. An NPC, and one of the central powers of the story.

## Physical Description and Bearing

The sources dwell far more on Jamis's mind than on his face, and that is fitting for a man whose whole craft is to be felt rather than seen. He is a figure of controlled, unhurried authority — the kind of commander who does not raise his voice because he has never needed to. He receives his agents seated behind a desk or across a low table, a glass of wine already poured, and conducts the most dangerous business of the Principality in the measured tones of a man discussing the weather.

He surrounds himself with the trappings of quiet power and quiet caution in equal measure. His offices in the Harbor District and his residence near the Royal Palace are threaded with private stairways and back passages he uses to bring people in and out unseen — he would rather no one witness who comes to speak with him. There is always liquor in the room, from fine Keoland vintages carried in on Oeridian ships down to the rough "red eye" his cutthroats favor, and he is an easy, gracious host with it, though he drinks as a man who is always watching. To sit across from Lord Jamis is to be made comfortable and studied at the same time.

## Station and Responsibilities

Jamis holds one of the most important offices in the Hold of the Sea Princes. Monmurg is the capital of the Hold and its chief naval power, ruled by Prince Jeon II — captain of all fleets, ruler of the Azure Sea, and first among the council of five princes. Jeon governs the city through a council of advisors drawn from its merchants, scholars, and military leaders, and the most influential of them all is Lord Jamis, Commodore of the fleet of Monmurg and the senior military commander beneath the prince himself. Jeon trusts him implicitly and relies on him to coordinate the entire security and defense of the city.

His concrete commands are considerable. He captains a squadron of twelve of Monmurg's fastest ships, and he commands a well-trained battalion of mercenary guardsmen charged with keeping order across both the Harbor District and the notorious Foreign District — the crowded, labyrinthine warren of merchants, mercenaries, cults, and contraband that is the only quarter where non-citizens may live, and of which it is said that if a thing cannot be found there, it does not exist. He runs the Harbor District directly, with its two-mile spit, its guarding forts, and its docks that berth a hundred ships at the height of the trading season, and he is the district's ruling authority in name even where his patrols rarely tread.

Beyond these formal duties lies the role no charter names: Jamis is Prince Jeon's spymaster, the master of an intelligence network that reaches into the Foreign District's underworld, the ranks of the Marines, the outer islands, and the affairs of foreign powers. He deploys assassins, scouts, and informants — Gouge, Jude, Merrick, the half-orc Karmirg, men embedded quietly among ordinary soldiers — and it is he who orchestrated the Helm Island expedition from the shadows, sending his agents to keep Prince Jeon's nephew alive and to determine whether Lord Fairwind was traitor or merely fool.

## Personality and Methods

Jamis is, above all, a man of cold objectivity in a court full of sentiment. Where Prince Jeon is an idealist — blinded by his lifelong friendship with Fairwind, set on ending the Amedio slave trade whatever the political cost — Jamis keeps his own eye "completely objective," and he says plainly that he does not fully trust even the prince's judgment. He had Fairwind on his radar for years, reads the man's jealousy of Monmurg's success clearly, and refuses to let affection or hope color his assessment of a threat. He thinks in webs of motive and leverage, always weighing who benefits and who is being played.

He is a spymaster to his bones: compartmentalized, discreet, and deeply careful. He splits his agents' debriefs so that no one hears more than they must, moves people through hidden stairways, and insists that the Principality's deepest secrets — the true nature of the anti-sahaugin powder, the artifact by which Cain Toli controls turtle dragons, the Scarlet Brotherhood's infiltration of both Keoland and the Hold — never leave the room. He is candid about the nature of his relationships: he tells Jude outright that their arrangement is not trust but patronage, and that his patronage is worth a great deal in desperate times; he warns Merrick that Gouge can be trusted only so long as one never crosses him, and that Jude is "completely amoral," loyal to no one but himself and controlled solely through advancement and favors.

Yet he is not cruel, and not merely a manipulator. He rewards good service generously and precisely — a seaside tower and library access for Jude, a measure of the precious powder for Merrick, a warm word of commendation for Merrick's work. He is a shrewd, fair judge of the people under him, forgiving Paul's hotheadedness as the growing pains of a young prince while noting exactly where it needs tempering. He carries an immense reservoir of history and political knowledge, and dispenses it deliberately — teaching his agents what they need to understand the stakes, and no more. He is patient, playing a long game in which today's maneuver is only a move toward something further off.

## Motivations and the Shadow Over Him

Jamis's stated purpose is the survival of Monmurg and the Sea Principalities through a moment of genuine peril: a Keoland blockade tightening like a noose, the Duke of Gradsul diverting the Hold's trade to Red Shore, Port Torvin burned, famine creeping into the Foreign District, the sahaugin returning to seas that the powder once kept clear, and the Toli — Cain Toli above all — playing a deadly game beneath it all. He labors to hold the city's defenses together, to recover the artifact drowning Monmurg's ships, to understand what has diminished the powder's protection, and to keep Prince Jeon's eyes opening to dangers the prince would rather not see. In service of that end he is willing to be ruthless, secretive, and manipulative, treating loyalty as something to be bought and managed rather than assumed.

But a shadow hangs over all of it, and the story does not resolve it. Lord Fairwind, under interrogation, claims that Jamis is the true traitor — that his wife, known now as Lady Azane, is sister to Lord Zafar Azane, a high Keoland noble loyal to the Duke of Gradsul who runs the sinister Office of Forbidden Magic, and that Zafar has visited Jamis's castle several times in recent months. Lady Jamis herself is whispered to work some influence of her own behind the scenes. Whether these ties make Jamis a Keoland agent betraying Jeon to the enemy blockading the city, or whether Fairwind is simply sowing doubt to save his own skin, is left genuinely uncertain. Even Gouge and Tyrus, hearing the web of connections laid out, feel their suspicions quietly shift toward the spymaster they serve. It is the central ambiguity of Lord Jamis: the man entrusted with the whole security of Monmurg may be its steadiest guardian or its most dangerous enemy — and he is far too careful to let anyone be sure which.

A private dimension of that shadow surfaces only in Jamis's own thoughts (the closing interlude of Ch. 43). Zafar Azane has in fact been his chief, secret counsel this year past — the source who predicted the Toli's moves before they made them, first named the Toli–Gradsul collusion, whispered of the Scarlet Brotherhood as the hand beneath it all, and marked Lord Phranck as a blade that might be turned rather than an enemy to be killed. Nearly the whole shape of the war Jamis carries came from his wife's brother, a man seated in the enemy's own court. After the Helm that reliance has curdled toward dread: for all Zafar foresaw, his long sight never once touched Fairwind's treachery (Jamis caught that himself), and Jamis has begun to wonder whether his brother-in-law has told him everything — or whether, with an Inquisitor's touch once laid upon his thoughts, he can even be sure which of his own certainties are his own. Zafar remains offstage in Book One; this is the seed of him.
