# Gregory

A young thieves'-guild cutpurse of Monmurg's Foreign District, taken alive after a botched robbery of Paul Revero and turned, in the space of a single dungeon interview, into Jude and Paul's first informant. Gregory is nobody's idea of a dangerous man — a hungry street thief of no rank and no name worth speaking — but he is exactly the sort of low, well-placed pair of eyes a spy learns to prize: guild-bred, street-fluent, and frightened enough to be useful. A minor NPC, and the loose thread that first leads the party toward Janus, the Toli coin, and the elf Kalisi.

## Physical Description

Gregory is young — seventeen, by his own count — and looks every day of a life spent hungry. When Paul and Jude come down to him he is curled in the corner of his cell, dirty-haired and hollow-eyed, clutching his filthy shirt like a shield, with the particular worn-thin exhaustion of a man raised on too little food in a quarter that offers little else. Across his back, high on the shoulder, is a faded but unmistakable thieves'-guild tattoo, the one mark that gives the lie to his protests of being no one in particular. He carries a fresh wound besides: the crossbow bolt Paul put through his throat the night before, staunched and bandaged well enough to keep him breathing, so that he speaks through the memory of nearly having died. In fear he has a small tell — a low, wordless muttering under his breath, some private comfort he reaches for before he catches himself — and in speech he is relentlessly deferential, ending nearly every sentence with *sir*.

## History

Gregory was born in the dock district and lives and works the Foreign District now, and he has been in the thieves' guild since he was ten years old — seven years of watching the district's trade from its lowest rung, which he wears as the one thin scrap of pride he has left. He is a cutpurse and second-story man, not a killer and not anyone the guild sends after men of consequence.

His trouble begins with a robbery gone badly wrong. Working with a companion called Jobs, he marked Paul coming out of the Royal District dressed plain, a coin purse jingling at his hip, and took him for nothing more than a rich merchant bound for the Grand Market — never guessing he'd set on the prince's own nephew. The attempt was clumsy and poorly armed, a sling and a pouch of lead shot against a man with a crossbow; Paul dropped Gregory with a bolt to the throat, and Jobs ran and left him for dead. He woke in the palace dungeon, bandaged by the very man who had shot him.

There Jude and Paul turned him. Given water, cheese, and a plain choice between usefulness and the irons, Gregory gave up what he knew — that guild-master Janus, eight months in his seat, had been taking Toli coin these four months past; that four Toli noble assassins, trained from birth in murder and magic both and marked by distinctive rings, had all quit the city together some five days before; and that an elven maid, comely and hard to follow, had been seen more than once near Lord Jamis's own residence. In exchange for coin and food he agreed to become their man: to learn whether Janus is merely paid to look away or bought outright, and to carry word to Ferd's under the signal *the still*. To protect the arrangement he consented to a convincing beating and to being left "dead" on the body pile — a small mercy, since Jobs already believes him a corpse — so that no one in his guild would ever guess the crown had had its hands on him.

## Personality Traits

Gregory is frightened, deferential, and a good deal shrewder than his station suggests. He pleads and agrees quickly, reads a room by instinct — marking at once that it is the silent masked figure in the corner he truly answers to — and bends toward whatever will keep him breathing and fed. He is self-deprecating to the point of fatalism: told he ought to fear the wall, he answers that the wall is for men of higher station and the gutter suits him better. Yet beneath the cringing runs a thread of real pride in his trade and a workable loyalty to Monmurg — "just a Monmurg man, born and bred" — and enough native sense to grasp, once Jude leads him to it, the difference between a thief and a traitor, and where his own interest lies in being reckoned the first and not the second.

## Motivations

Gregory's motives run no deeper than survival and the next meal, which in a besieged city is motive enough. He wants to live, to eat, and to stay clear of both his own guild's notice and the crown's noose, and he has worked out that the surest way to manage all three is to make himself quietly valuable to men more dangerous than himself. He harbors no ambition and no grudge — not even against the man who shot him, to whom he offers something close to gratitude for the care shown him since. He is, in the end, a small survivor of the Foreign District who has stumbled into the edges of a great game, and means only to come out of it alive and a little better fed than he went in.
