# Folsom

A Marine bard from Monmurg — shipwright's son turned navy songsmith, all voice and good cheer and terrible timing. He can charm a hobgoblin or a lord with a word, empty a barroom with a song, and — through the same bardic gift — cure wounds, mending his companions' hurts in the thick of a fight.

## Physical Description

Folsom's gift is not size but presence. He is of medium height and decent build — reasonably strong and hardy, with a sailor's weathered sturdiness rather than a bruiser's bulk — but what people notice is his charisma. He is strikingly handsome, and he knows it, carrying himself with an easy, magnetic charm that made him popular in the ranks and turns heads wherever he performs. Beneath the good looks runs a mischievous, contrarian streak — never quite a troublemaker, but a man who plainly enjoys needling the powerful and saying the thing everyone else is thinking and won't.

He wears the Marine uniform, but his gear is an eclectic assortment that plainly isn't standard issue, and he carries a longbow — an unusual weapon for a man of the sea principalities, and enough on its own to mark him as something other than an ordinary soldier. First impressions of him tend to mislead, as he says himself; the oddness wears off once people get to know him.

## Gear & Items

- **The green song-stone.** A small green stone set at the collar of a worn old cloak, given to Folsom by Gouge in a Harbor-District alley after the barracks scene (Ch. 45). Gouge, who admits he "doesn't use it right" himself, calls it "proper magic" that will make Folsom's songs "a little better" — treat it as a bardic focus amplifying his musical/bardic effects (heartening tunes, healing). It comes with a condition Gouge set on the gift: in a fight, before Folsom "spends" it, he must look to Gouge first — a **nod** means spend it where he pleases; a **flat, deliberate stare** means send whatever he has Gouge's way. (It is *not* related to Paul's homunculus Heartstone/Mindstone, which are also green but a different craft; see `worldbuilding/magic_system.md`.)

## History

Folsom was born in Monmurg to a family of modest means, in a city thick with the smell of salt and spice and loud with the clamor of merchants and sailors. His father was a shipwright who built the very vessels that sailed the Azure Sea; his mother a seamstress whose intricate embroidery adorned the garments of Monmurg's elite. From childhood he was captivated by the city's music — a vibrant mongrel of sounds drawn from across the Flanaess — and his first instrument was a simple fife, a gift from a traveling bard out of Gradsul who had been charmed by the boy's curiosity. That fife became his constant companion, and he learned young to coax melodies from it that could lift spirits and settle nerves.

His talent did not stay hidden. Invited to play at local gatherings and festivals, he performed the traditional music of the Oeridian people and hungered to learn the music of far-off lands, dreaming of adventure and the open sea from the tales sailors carried home. When he came of age he joined the Monmurgian navy — a choice that suited both his love of the city and his taste for structure. His superiors quickly found uses for his gift, employing him to pass signals between ships and to raise the morale of his fellow soldiers, and his jovial, easygoing manner and natural good looks made him a favorite among Marines and officers alike. That he was no real warrior — far more interested in tending the wounded than trading blows — seemed to bother no one. He has served under Lord Jamis a long time, doing navy work for him and acting as something of an advisor to captains on the local waters, more than a mere Marine despite the uniform.

At the story's present, Folsom is folded into Gouge's crew for the Helm Island expedition, where he proves a genuinely useful hand — helping build and sail the makeshift raft, and turning his bardic magic to the mission. He charms his way past an inner-sanctum hobgoblin guard, and later, with Fairwind under a charm spell, it is gentle, guileless Folsom whom the captured lord tries to win over, whispering that Jamis is the true traitor and that "good, honest, loyal men" should not be caught in the schemes of princes. Back in Monmurg he nearly undoes the whole careful cover story with a song, and is promptly folded into a new venture — Owen Black's smuggling runs to Red Shore — as much to get him and his mouth out of the city for a while as anything else.

## Personality Traits

Folsom is an irrepressible performer, warm and funny and utterly in love with his own voice — "Great voice, though" is his defense for nearly everything. He is loyal, good-natured, and eager to please, the kind of man who lifts a room simply by walking into it. His fatal flaw is that he cannot read one. Fresh off the Helm, sworn to secrecy about everything the party saw, he climbs onto a barroom counter in front of patriotic off-duty Marines and performs "The Brewing Storm" — a genuinely well-written, biting satire mocking the princes for letting Monmurg starve. The lyrics are good; the timing is catastrophic, and Gouge has to slide a dagger halfway from its sheath and haul him bodily into the street before it turns ugly. "I can't help it," Folsom admits, and he means it.

For all that, he is not a fool, and resents being treated as one. When Gouge warns him to keep quiet about the barrels and the treason, Folsom pushes back that his mouth stays shut on things that actually matter — "I'm smarter than that" — drawing a real line between his loose tongue for jokes and songs and his discretion with genuine secrets. His humor misfires as often as it lands; Tyrus is forever telling him the jokes aren't working, and forever bringing him along anyway.

In the field he plays a supporter's part rather than a fighter's, an instinct that goes back to his preference for tending the wounded over drawing blood. He accepts Gouge's battlefield pact readily — look to Gouge before loosing a song or a spell — and is comfortable taking his cues from stronger, harder men. Above all he is devoted to Tyrus: "Wherever Tyrus goes, I go" is the whole of his plan for the future, offered without hesitation.

## Motivations

Music is the center of Folsom's life and the engine of nearly everything he does. He performs to move people — to lift their spirits, to tell the tale, to be seen and heard — and the urge is strong enough to override his judgment about when to keep quiet. Every adventure is, to him, partly raw material for the next song.

Threaded through that is a specific, cherished dream: he has never forgotten the traveling bard from Gradsul who gave him his first fife, and he longs one day to journey to Gradsul himself to see, and perhaps to study at, the great College of Bards in that city. It is the horizon he is quietly sailing toward beneath all the barroom clowning.

He is also moved by simpler, steadier loyalties — a real love of Monmurg and of the order and camaraderie he found in its navy, a long-standing service to Lord Jamis, and above all his attachment to his companions, Tyrus most of all. And underneath it lies the healer's temperament noted since his navy days: for all his hunger to entertain, what he most wants in a crisis is to keep the people around him whole and in good cheer.
