# Owen Black

A Redshore-running smuggler, former man of Monmurg's own navy, and Merrick's cousin — the loud, scarred, ruggedly handsome, unmistakably Oeridian face behind half the food and contraband presently finding its way past the blockade into a starving city. Where Merrick reads the sea by duty and instinct, Owen reads it by opportunity, and in a Monmurg sliding toward famine that has made him more useful to the city, and more entangled in its dangers, than he ever set out to be. An NPC.

## Physical Description

Owen is a big, loud, unmistakably Oeridian man — the sort Jude picks out of a crowded tavern before a word passes between them — and he carries a scar across his face prominent enough that the Earl of Redshore needs no fuller description than "scarred, hard to miss" to point a stranger toward him. He moves and talks with the easy confidence of a man used to being noticed and welcomed rather than avoided. He wears the cost of his trade plainly rather than hiding it: back in Monmurg after his run-in with Lord Gloin Baywin — the Plar of Salinmoor, Keoland's appointed overlord of that province — he sports his left arm in a splint, a memento he mentions almost as an afterthought, more a story to be told over a drink than a wound to be nursed. He owns and captains his own small, fast vessels built for outrunning patrols rather than standing in a fight — a scorched sloop out of Redshore, and later a cutter and a skiff, picked up after an earlier ship was lost to trouble he prefers not to explain in full.

## History

Owen came up through Monmurg's own navy and Marine ranks — no dishonorable discharge, by Folsom's account, only bigger ambitions than the service could hold, and a parting amicable enough that half the barracks still greets him like one of their own when he walks in. Merrick sums him up plainly as a cousin, "decent guy, but always drawn to profit," who has spent recent years smuggling for whichever side of the blockade paid better.

He enters the story recommended by the Earl of Redshore, who steers Jude toward him as a man who knows the shipping lanes and the patrols better than anyone alive and owns his own ship — findable, loud and scarred, at a bar in Redshore called the Whale. For a hundred gold, half up front, Owen ferries Jude and Horace out of Redshore and along the coast toward Ensar, running without lights past anchored merchantmen with the unhurried skill of a man who has made the run a hundred times, talking the whole way as men paid to talk tend to do, and asking few questions in return — a trait Jude reads immediately as the single most reliable thing about him: Owen values coin over curiosity. His ship carries old fire damage along the deck, scorched in patches he claims not to understand; he explains it was stolen from him and later returned, and offers nothing further. Jude lets the lie sit unchallenged, one more secret that belongs to somebody else's story.

There is an older, unexplained wound between Owen and Gouge. Six months before the present crisis, by Ferd's account, Gouge beat Owen badly in a fight at Ferd's own bar in Monmurg's foreign district — "didn't like the look of him," is all Ferd can offer by way of reason. Whatever passed between them, it seems to run only one direction: months later, standing a few feet from Gouge without knowing it, Owen has no idea who the man is.

His most recent trouble runs deeper. About two months back, smuggling weapons through Saltmarsh, Owen took scouting work for a job run by Cain Toli, working alongside a gangly Toli cabin boy he thought nothing of at the time — until that same young man turned up in Monmurg weeks later as the pirate known as the Scarlet Rat, and attacked him on sight, having recognized his face. The Saltmarsh job also tied him, through a wizard called Lord Phranck, to the Plar of Salinmoor — Lord Gloin Baywin, the commander known as the "power of Baywin" — a man Owen had crossed before and who had him captured and interrogated in the Appleyard shell-keep, breaking his arm in the process. He escaped only with help — fellow prisoners, bugbears among them, overpowering one of Baywin's guard-beasts together — and fled by skiff down the coast through Saltmarsh and Seton, picking up old friends who helped him back to Monmurg and into a new ship.

He returns to Monmurg on the third of Wealsun, the same day the party comes back from the Helm, with barrels of proper, un-watered cider and a hold of food for the barracks, and is folded quickly back into the party's orbit — trading news and old jokes with Tyrus, worrying over Folsom after the disastrous tavern performance, catching up with Merrick, and angling, not at all subtly, to recruit Gouge onto his crew for the right cut. He lost a man or two to a sahaugin attack a mile out from Redshore on the crossing home, one more sign, alongside what the party saw at the Helm, that the creatures are stirring again after years of quiet. What began as a passage-for-hire has, by the time the debriefs are done, become the cover story Gouge and the others settle on for their next move: reconnaissance into Redshore, run under Owen's name, gathering intelligence for Jamis that Owen himself is never meant to know is anything more than a hired hand's errand.

## Personality Traits

Owen is gregarious where Merrick is serious — friendlier, freer, quick with a smile, a joke, and a punch on the arm for anyone he's glad to see, and it makes him well liked by people who barely know him. He talks easily and often, especially with a drink or a purse in front of him, and he tells even the roughest chapters of his own life — capture, interrogation, a broken arm, a knife-close escape — as a tall tale for the bar rather than a confession, wearing danger lightly in company even when it plainly cost him something. That looseness has limits: pressed on exactly where he's been or what he's carrying, he deflects smoothly ("not a good idea to ask me that question directly") and gives up only what he decides is safe to give, a smuggler's instinct for compartmentalizing that sits just beneath the friendliness.

He is frankly, unsentimentally motivated by coin, and makes no effort to dress that up — but he pairs it with a real, if self-serving, notion of civic duty, framing his profiteering as service to the city that bred him: Monmurg was built on smuggling and piracy in the first place, so keeping the trade moving is keeping Monmurg alive, and he counts himself one of her children with an obligation to see her through. He is politically cynical and says so freely — the princes are fools more interested in what lines their pockets than how they look doing it, in his estimation, and he has no patience for a fleet sitting idle in dock while the blockade strangles the city he's trying to feed. Underneath the recruiter's pitch — and he is always, a little, recruiting, angling for Tyrus, for Folsom, for Gouge — runs a genuine warmth for the people he keeps around him; he buys rounds, checks in on friends after a bad night, and worries, unprompted, about Gouge doing something rash.

## Motivations

Owen's clearest driver is profit, and Jamis, who has taken his measure, calls it plainly: a free agent, morally flexible, loyal to no prince in particular, willing to work whichever side of a border pays — except a slaver's trade, which he won't touch, and a rebel's cause, which holds no appeal for him either. He smuggled into Keoland territory before the blockade to dodge Gradsul's taxes, and now smuggles the same goods back the other way, food and cider out of Saltmarsh and Redshore into a starving Monmurg, because that is where the money and the need both currently point.

Beneath the profit motive sits something closer to identity than sentiment: a belief that Monmurg's smuggling, piracy, and profiteering past is not a shameful thing to outgrow but the very thing that makes the city worth saving, and that keeping goods moving through a blockade is itself a form of loyalty. Family matters to him in the same practical, unshowy register — his bond with Merrick extends easily to Merrick's friends, and his recruitment of Tyrus, Folsom, and Gouge reads as equal parts opportunity and genuine fondness. And running under all of it now is a newer thread of self-preservation: having drawn the attention of the Scarlet Rat, a Toli-linked wizard, and a Keolandish commander willing to break his arm over him, Owen appears to be quietly building alliances — with Tyrus, with Gouge, with Jamis at a careful remove — as insurance against enemies he has only just discovered he has.
