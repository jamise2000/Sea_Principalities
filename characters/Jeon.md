# Prince Jeon II

Prince of Monmurg, captain of all fleets and Ruler of the Azure Sea — first among the five princes of the Sea Principalities and the Hold's official leader. A good and widely loved ruler, uncle and protector of Paul Rivera, and an idealist whose loyalties and convictions may be the very seams the Toli intend to pull apart. An NPC, and the crown at the center of the story's crisis.

## Physical Description and Bearing

Jeon is a strapping Oeridian in his prime — his nephew Paul is built in the same mold, a younger, not-yet-filled-out echo of the same frame, which tells you the prince is the fuller, more finished version of that build. Like every ruler of his lineage he is a swashbuckler of real repute, a man of the sword as much as the council chamber, and he carries himself with the easy physical confidence of one.

In the moments the party sees him, though, it is not swagger but concern that defines him. He receives Paul behind the desk of his palace office, poring over maps of the outer islands and the bays, brow furrowed, trading tense and pregnant pauses with Lord Jamis. He is a ruler under strain — gracious and composed on the surface, warm to his nephew, but plainly a man carrying the weight of a city with a noose around its neck. When he speaks it is with the measured authority of someone accustomed to being obeyed and to being loved, which in Monmurg he is.

## Station and Responsibilities

Jeon II rules Monmurg, the gilded capital of the Hold of the Sea Princes and its chief naval power — a city of onion domes and precious metals raised on the spoils of a piratical past, home to some twenty-five thousand natives and ten thousand foreigners, and the beating heart of Azure Sea trade. As Prince of Monmurg he is captain of all fleets and Ruler of the Azure Sea, considered first among equals on the council of five princes that governs the Hold. He is not the absolute ruler of the nation — each prince holds an equal vote, advised by the Plars of Hokar and Westkeep — but he is recognized as its official leader and, in time of war, as supreme commander of the navy, wielding considerable power well beyond Monmurg's own walls.

Within the city he governs through a council of advisors drawn from its merchants, scholars, and military leaders, chief among them Lord Jamis, his commodore and the commander of his security and defense, whom he trusts implicitly. He holds the Strait of Monmurg — the ten-mile channel through which all the Hold's trade must pass — as a strategic asset, keeping warships on constant patrol, and he maintains the fortified linchpin of Helm Island at the strait's center, the traditional seat where the princes and commodores of the Hold plan their naval campaigns. It is the loss of that island, and the strangling Keoland blockade behind it, that drives the present emergency. Rumor even holds that it is Jeon who pays the yearly tribute of treasure to the eldest dragon turtle of the Dragon Isles to keep Monmurg's shipping safe — a bargain that now appears to be unraveling in Toli hands.

## Personality

Jeon is, by the reckoning of his own people, a good man and a fair ruler, widely loved across the Hold. That reputation is borne out in how he governs: he wants to hear the truth firsthand and in private, insisting on debriefing his nephew Paul himself rather than through intermediaries, unwilling in these dangerous times to simply take everyone's word secondhand. He is engaged and attentive to detail, and he treats those around him — even Jamis's hired killers and wizards — with courtesy.

His defining trait, for better and worse, is idealism. Where the other princes of the outer isles are hard-nosed pragmatists, Jeon holds genuine moral convictions — above all a deep distaste for the Amedio slave trade, which he would end in Monmurg entirely if he could. It is a stance that does him credit and endangers him at once: his campaign against slavery is precisely what the Duke of Berghof and the Toli are using as leverage, restricting the anti-sahaugin powder and pressing him toward ruin. Jamis, who serves him faithfully, privately judges this idealism a luxury the Hold cannot presently afford, and worries that the prince's convictions cloud his strategic sight.

That same warmth of heart is his great vulnerability. Jeon and Lord Fairwind are childhood friends who have known each other their whole lives, and the prince simply cannot imagine his friend capable of betrayal — a blind spot so pronounced that it was part of why agents had to be sent to the Helm in the first place, and why the accusation had to come from Paul, whom he trusts, to have any hope of changing his mind. He is a man whose loyalties run genuine and deep, and who is therefore slow to believe those loyalties misplaced. Even Jamis does not fully trust the prince's judgment, fearing his emotions will blind him twice over.

## Motivations

Jeon's overriding purpose is the survival and flourishing of Monmurg and the Hold through the gathering storm — breaking the blockade, feeding a city sliding toward famine, holding the strait and reclaiming the Helm, and keeping the fragile union of princes from splintering under Toli pressure. He is a ruler who genuinely loves his city and is loved by it in return, and everything he does bends toward keeping it whole.

Bound up with that is a quieter, more personal set of cares. He is devoted to his nephew Paul, the son of his late sister Lady Alaria — protective to the point of not wanting the young man exposed to direct combat, and eager to see him grow into something more than a hidden, scarred figure of the court. To that end he nurtures a real fascination with magic and a wish to see arcane knowledge take root in his own family, personally steering Paul toward the path of the wizard and insisting, over Jamis's objections, that Jude tutor him. In this he blends dynastic ambition with genuine affection: he wants his bloodline strengthened, and he wants the boy to matter.

And underneath the good ruler's steadiness runs the peril the story keeps circling — a prince "caught in a web of intrigue and betrayal," whose finest qualities of loyalty, mercy, and idealism are exactly the levers his enemies mean to use against him. His deepest motivation may simply be to remain the honest center of a nation full of schemers, at the very moment when honesty has become the most dangerous stance of all.
