# Chapter Five — The First Cantrip

The following afternoon, in a quiet corner of Lord Jamis's residence, the two of them sat down properly to begin.

"So," Jude said, studying him across the table, "where do you see yourself? You've met Gouge — if you go on entirely as you have been, that's the road you're walking. I like the man, worked with him a long while, and I'll keep on working with him. But he's a killer, plain and simple, nothing more."

"Is that what you see for yourself," Paul asked, "or what you want?"

"Not exactly. I think I'd like to be something of a mage as well."

"What draws you to it, as far as you understand it? Reading scrolls, or something more?"

"Every new initiate to the arcane comes in carrying their own baggage, if I'm honest."

"So what do you actually want — to compete with others who wield real influence? Power?"

"I like that word. Influence."

"Power for what, though?"

Jude considered the question longer than Paul expected. "The last time I stood against those rogue mages, I wasn't nearly as useful as I'd have liked to be. Nothing I could do to counter their spells directly."

"So you felt ineffective."

"There's a limit to how far cutting a man's throat gets you. Gouge is fine in a pinch, but against someone who truly commands the arcane, it's an entirely different contest."

"Any natural talent for it, that you know of?"

"I think so," Jude said. "I made that little lizard, after all."

"The homunculus — you made it yourself, truly?"

"Truly."

Paul studied him with fresh interest. He knew enough of the craft, from his own reading, to know that most who claimed the title were charlatans selling little more than painted trinkets to credulous nobles — real homunculi were rarer, and harder-won, than the popular fashion for them suggested. "Tell me how you did it, then. Don't spare me the details — I'll know if you're spinning me a tale."

"I've the actual papers on it, if you want proof," Jude said, and described the working plainly enough — blood, clay, a working drawn from an old tome he'd found among a stranger's effects years before, one he kept close and shared with no one. He didn't offer the book itself, nor the name written inside its cover, and Paul, sensing the boundary, didn't press for it.

"Can you shape it safely, the way you want?" Paul asked instead.

"You're good," Jude said. "Yes."

The conversation wandered, as such conversations do, into stranger corners — why a man raised in comfortable circumstances would describe himself as having been "locked up" for twenty years, what lay behind the elf's careful reserve, how much of what Jude said of himself was even true. Jude turned the questions aside without much heat, more amused than offended, and pressed in turn for whatever hidden currents ran beneath Paul's own composed exterior.

"What are your hang-ups?" he asked eventually. "Every man carries something from before he started down this road."

"I want to prove myself," Paul admitted, after a moment's thought. "Earn my place. Be valuable."

"I understand wanting that." Jude leaned forward. "Now — how does that very desire get you killed, in this line of work? Take it further. You don't want to fail. What does failure cost you here, specifically, studying the arcane?"

"It could kill me."

"Worse than that. If, in your hunger to prove yourself, you reach for things you can't yet control, believing you can — you might unleash something on the world that neither of us can put back. Summon what you can't command, thinking you're ready when you're not. I want to be valuable too, Paul. I want power, same as you. But if you're dead, none of that matters, because you're dead. You'll have to check yourself constantly, especially once I can't watch over every step you take, and you're teaching yourself instead. Keep that in your bones: don't overreach. If you forget it, you will overreach, and the cost may run far beyond your own life."

"I understand," Paul said, and meant it.

They turned, at last, to the practical matter of what Paul might actually learn first. He described his own instincts — disguise, misdirection, moving unseen through a crowd rather than cutting a path through it — and Jude nodded slowly, recognizing it for what it was.

"That maps to illusion, in the arts. Being unseen." He considered the six spells owed to a new initiate, and the three cantrips besides. "Mending and Light, to start. You already know how to kill a man — these will teach you the rest of what the job actually requires."

"Mending," Paul said, unconvinced. "How does that help me, exactly?"

"Break a window getting into a place, mend it again on your way out, and cover your tracks entirely."

"That, I can use."

"And Light isn't only illumination — cast it on the inside of a man's visor, and you've blinded him outright. Or use it to check a dark room for hidden traps before you ever set foot inside." Jude ran through the rest — Sleep, to spare a guard rather than kill him; Protection from Good and Evil; Detect Magic, invaluable against warded traps; and, when the conversation strayed toward an old black iron mask Paul had once worn and quickly discarded, Identify as well. "Better safe. And I'll throw in Shield — quick enough to raise in the instant a blow lands, the only spell within your reach that turns aside an incoming strike outright. Stops a magic missile cold, and buys you real protection for the moment you cast it."

They spent the rest of the afternoon and well into evening on the practical work of it — parchment in place of a proper spellbook, ink smudged and re-inked a dozen times over, a broken chair mended and broken again before it finally held. Jude proved an exacting teacher, quick with a thrown bowl or a sharp word whenever Paul's concentration slipped, and Paul gave as good as he got, needling him right back at every opportunity — about the mask, about the mysterious tower in the Aldorian Wood Jude claimed as a childhood home and which, by his own later admission, didn't actually exist. By the time full dark had settled over the palace, Paul had Mending down cold, bruised knuckles and bruised pride both, and Light still eluding him more often than not.

"First cantrip's easy," Jude told him, not unkindly, as they finally called an end to it. "The second one's always rougher."

"I got bashed a lot in the meantime."

"Stop taking it personally. Keep some objectivity." Jude studied him a moment, something almost fond beneath the dryness. "Remember that part of you that wanted to kill every Toli on the dock at Jerly Bay? Learn to hold that same fire steady, and you'll get there."
