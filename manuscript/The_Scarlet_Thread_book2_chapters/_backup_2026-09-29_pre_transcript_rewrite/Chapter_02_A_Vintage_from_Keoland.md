# Chapter Two — A Vintage from Keoland

Jude, meanwhile, had been led by a different route — not up toward the palace, but down a back stairway he'd never walked before, into one of Jamis's private offices in the Harbor District. He noted the path carefully as he went, aware it was likely the man's own private route between residence and office, and aware too, from the care taken to keep him unseen, that Jamis did not wish this visit known.

He'd pocketed a portion of the yellow powder himself before leaving the island, meaning to study it at his own leisure, and it hadn't escaped him that Fairwind now knew him for one of Jamis's agents — reason enough to keep half an eye on every shadowed doorway between the docks and this office, in case some rival chose tonight of all nights to settle a score.

Jamis was waiting when he entered, and rose to pour the wine himself rather than send for it. "Jude — please, sit. I need a drink myself. I assume you're not partial to Gouge's red-eye?"

Jude's expression answered for him.

"I have something better, then — wine, unless you'd prefer something harder."

"Just wine."

Jamis brought the glass over himself. "A vintage from Keoland — I hope you like it."

"Wait," Jude said, studying the bottle. "Was that carried on an Oeridian ship?"

"An Oeridian ship, yes — far beyond what Keoland itself could produce."

"Have you spoken with Paul yet?"

"I won't be — the prince will brief him directly. I may speak with him later, but I wanted you first, before Gouge or Merrick's reports reach me. Start from the beginning, if you would. I already have a fair picture of what happened out there — I had observers embedded among the Marines as well."

"I met some of the ones with Gouge, at least."

"Yes — and at least two of them answer to other people. I don't know if you're one of those."

"I don't need to know who they answer to," Jude said. "On Gouge's crew, they answer to me."

Jamis studied him a moment. "To my understanding, Merrick's one of my agents. Gouge is one of my agents."

"What of the other two? The bard?"

"Where's he from?"

"One of the men in Gouge's crew."

"I can't recall his name offhand, which irritates me. I'll assume the rest were simply regular Marines with you, not my own, though I'll look into it."

"Interesting," Jude said. "I know the bard and Merrick both sent word to have corsairs meet us — that we'd be leaving by skiff."

"Likely men Merrick trusts personally. He did ask to bring along some of his own. I'll speak to him, update what I know of them." Jamis settled back. "What is it you'd like to share first?"

Jude gave his account in careful order — the homunculus returning agitated, the fight with the two Toli guarding the approach, the powerful counterspell or resistance they'd carried alongside their enchanted blades, Fairwind found bound to a chair once the fighting ended. "In the conversation that followed, trying to get him off the island and back to Monmurg for proper interrogation, he admitted to siphoning off the barrels for months. I saw the storage room myself — entirely empty, save the twenty-five we recovered, minus the four we brought home. The rest had already gone, loaded onto Cain Toli's ship, the Sea Ghost.

"So it sounds as if the Toli took a share, and were negotiating with him — and he played it off as business as usual, the kind of jockeying that happens between princes all the time." He caught himself. "He did say — sorry, I may be mixing something in there that only Paul would have heard directly."

"That's fine," Jamis said. "You wouldn't know that part — that was specific to Paul's account."

"But I did hear him admit to siphoning the barrels, and he wasn't surprised the Toli were there. Feigning composure, or genuinely unbothered — either way, they were there. It sounds like Fairwind's out for his own benefit — believes that siding with the Toli against Jeon gains him leverage, more power, more control. A political play, purely."

"I think it was too," Jamis said, "and that's part of why I was trying to bring him back to you — figuring you might still negotiate with him, or send him to his own people, whatever made sense."

"My sense of the Toli, from the short time I've been back after a year abroad, is that if they're involved in this, they're playing for Keoland's benefit. That worries me. But I'm glad to hear he admitted, in full honesty, that he was playing politics for leverage."

"I believe so," Jamis said. "You should know — Fairwind and Jeon are childhood friends, known each other their whole lives. Jeon's mind will change now, I think, especially since it was Paul who brought the accusation. I'm glad it happened that way, because Jeon could never have imagined his own friend capable of this — that's part of why you were sent in the first place."

"He admitted, plainly, to siphoning the barrels — and may hold the largest surviving stash of any of them, which is treasonous to the principalities, if not directly to Jeon himself. Talking with the Toli alone wouldn't ordinarily be improper, but removing barrels of that substance is treason outright. Even knowledge of it, outside the admiralty, is treasonous. The Toli agents shouldn't have been allowed into that area at all."

"So they'd have come up from below as well?"

"Right — we hadn't penetrated the main citadel through the front. We climbed one of the outer walls near the coastline — I levitated us up, Gouge managed on his own — under cover of darkness. His troops weren't aware of us. A sizable portion of his garrison had retreated to the barracks, apparently under cover of that fog a few nights past — that's when the hobgoblins came, dressed in Marine-style gear, though not any color scheme I recognized as belonging to any prince. Interesting, that.

"His garrison commander had no understanding of what had actually happened, beyond the hobgoblins holding the inner citadel. He'd staged a show of force that gave us our diversion to scale the outer wall. But when Fairwind led us down, from the inner hall—"

"The sanctum," Jamis offered.

"Yes, straight down. That's where we fought the Toli, who likely came up from the Sea Ghost."

"So they know not only of the barrels," Jamis said slowly, "but of the inner sanctum's layout too."

"Fairwind said he didn't want to see his men killed — made sure they were disarmed and locked away elsewhere before we arrived. I meant to stay behind with Paul, but Gouge and Merrick judged it wiser to pull back and call for help. That's why they signaled the corsairs Jeon had already sent." Jude turned the rest of it over. "I'm not certain whether the hobgoblins came in under cover of the fog alongside the sahaugin — there were sahaugin there too, which I assume is exactly what the barrels were meant to prevent. We lost the whole complement of two hundred Marines who'd come ashore with us — I saved twenty who were with me directly, retreated into the garrison beside Fairwind's own men.

"To my knowledge, his outer garrison wasn't aware of his duplicity — I never spoke with anyone from the inner sanctum, but it didn't sound as though there'd been a massacre of his own troops. My feeling is he was trying to maintain a ruse — his theft of the barrels going unnoticed, the blame falling on the Toli instead."

"That would make sense," Jamis said, and something in his voice shifted, grew more careful. "There are a couple of things you should know now, and I'll tell you plainly — don't speak of this to anyone. Not Gouge. Not Paul."

"Not even Paul."

"No. Jeon's affection for Fairwind blinded him once, and I'm afraid his emotions could blind him again in this. When we act, we have to do it without those blinders, which is why what I tell you now stays between us."

"Of course."

"This isn't because I trust you completely, Jude," Jamis said, something almost wry in it. "But I understand what patronage means in desperate times, and mine will be worth a great deal to you."

"Do you have another magic ring in that drawer?" Jude asked, only half joking.

Jamis paused. "What did you say?"

"You got another magic ring in there?"

Jamis considered it a long moment, then something crossed his face that suggested he'd landed on something better. "Actually, I have something you'd appreciate more. Real estate's quite cheap in Monmurg right now, and I know where you live — you may have your reasons for staying there, various nefarious ones, I don't judge. I understand you're keeping a thumb on the pulse of the foreign district, and that has its uses. But there's a tower overlooking the sea that would suit you far better. And, of course, access to the Royal Library — not the Archives of the Arcane Order, that's a separate matter, but a fine collection all the same. Better than what you've had, in that half-ruined tower you're keeping now. We'll arrange it, so long as you're willing to act as my agent in this."

"Of course."

"I don't think Fairwind fully understands what the Toli are truly up to," Jamis said. "I don't think he understands the Scarlet Brotherhood at all, or the danger it poses."

"He doesn't."

"I believe he thinks he's simply playing politics—"

"Playing politics with people who are playing for keeps," Jude said.

"Exactly — and he believes the Toli ultimately want the sea principalities united as one nation, that their negotiations with Jeon are purely over the slave trade — the Duke of Berghof, the power of Westkeep, the interior principalities all bound up in it. Fairwind thinks Jeon's objection to that trade is the whole of the Toli's interest. It isn't — and unfortunately, I don't believe Jeon himself fully grasps what they're truly doing either. I have information that the Scarlet Brotherhood isn't only infiltrating the sea principalities — they're infiltrating Keoland too, and the current conflict between the two nations is being stoked, to their own benefit."

"Remember when I spoke with that baron from Redshore?" Jude said. "He seemed surprised when I told him Jeon opposed slavery — as though he'd been led to believe otherwise."

"That belief comes straight from the Duke of Gradsul," Jamis said. "He's always claimed the sea principalities morally corrupt for the slave trade — which existed in Berghof long before we ever came into being, back to the fall of the Suel Empire itself. We had nothing to do with its origin, though yes, slaves are traded in our own marketplaces, and Jeon would end the practice if he could. It's something that predates our nation entirely."
