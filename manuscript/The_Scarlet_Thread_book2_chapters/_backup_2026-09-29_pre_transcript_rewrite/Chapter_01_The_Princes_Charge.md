## PART ONE: DEBRIEFING IN MONMURG

# Chapter One — The Prince's Charge

Paul, meanwhile, had been led up a private stairway past several guards, into the palace that was as much his own home as it was Jeon's seat of power, and brought to his uncle's office. Jeon stood there already, bent over a map of the outer islands, Songmor Bay marked to the north, Jerly Bay to the south, and something drawn and troubled in the set of his shoulders that Paul had rarely seen in him before.

"Paul," Jeon said, without looking up at first. "Sit down, please."

Paul sat. "I heard there was some trouble on Helm Island."

"I've had reports the ship we sent you on was overwhelmed."

"Three hundred Marines on that ship," Paul said, the weight of it settling into his voice as he said it aloud for the first time. "All gone. Maybe a hundred made it off."

"And it was the sahaugin?"

"Yes — and hobgoblins had taken the island besides."

"Tell me more of that."

Paul drew a breath. "Fairwind is a traitor."

Jeon's eyes came up sharply from the map. "How do you know this?"

He was alone with his uncle, Paul realized as he glanced about the room — no Jamis present, no witness beyond the two of them, which told him plainly enough that Jeon wanted his account first, unshaded by anyone else's version of events. Paul laid it out in full: the landing at the southern beacon, the ship overrun by sahaugin behind them even as they fought ashore, the barracks and the Marines still holding there who knew nothing of what had become of their lord. The plan to scale the wall while a diversion drew every eye elsewhere. The inner sanctum, and what he'd seen and heard there through his creature's borrowed senses.

"You said Fairwind was a traitor," Jeon said again, when Paul had laid the ground of it.

"Here's where it gets interesting," Paul said. "His own garrison didn't know how the hobgoblins had gotten in, or what had become of him. They'd just been holed up, holding the line, under siege themselves. We linked with them, made our plan, and snuck around to where we figured he'd be."

"The inner sanctum."

"Yes. I scouted ahead — could see and hear him. Dealing."

"Dealing with whom?"

"The Toli."

Jeon's face went very still. "The Toli — there were Toli on the Helm, at that hour?"

"I saw them myself. We captured one."

"How do you know they were Toli?"

"I saw them," Paul said, "and we took one alive."

"Go on, then. You witnessed Lord Fairwind speaking with them, walking the same room, making deals. What were they discussing?"

"Removing the powder from the Helm," Paul said. "The substance that keeps the sahaugin away. The Toli said something like — 'We noticed you took most of it for yourself.' I think Fairwind was splitting off a share for himself, that was his angle. He'd already removed most of it from the island."

"And that's how the sahaugin were able to swarm it."

"Right. They said he had four barrels of the stuff. Once we'd taken him, I placed him under arrest — he made a great fuss, claimed innocence throughout. We took one Toli alive as well." Paul recounted the rest — the secret passage down through the cave system, the fight on the ledge, the dead Toli thrown into the water, Fairwind's own fall — pushed, or falling, in the same chaos that had nearly taken Paul with him. "Don't know what happened to him. Could be dead. Could be alive. We fought our way down, killed the hobgoblins there, built a raft, sailed clear. Took four barrels with us, enough to keep the sahaugin off for now."

"How many barrels were there, originally?"

"Twenty-five, by our count. We could only take four — the rest stayed behind."

"Who else witnessed Fairwind speaking with the Toli agents?"

"Unfortunately — nobody else. Just me."

Jeon studied him a long moment, something weighing behind his eyes. "So you're the only one with a direct, definitive account of this betrayal. Your companions — how much have you told them of it?"

"That he's a traitor, that he was dealing with the Toli. That's the whole of it — I didn't go into specifics. But they believed me over him."

"It doesn't entirely make sense," Jeon said slowly. "What he apparently told all of them was simply that he'd had his own men give up their arms and be held, cells or otherwise."

"We never found his Marines," Paul admitted. "But they'd basically let the hobgoblins walk right in."

"That doesn't sound very fair to him," Jeon said, "but it is your opinion that Fairwind allowed the hobgoblins to take the Helm."

"The hobgoblins were working with the Toli, we believe."

"Strange, even given the Duke of Berghof's known dealings with hobgoblin mercenaries — so there may be some involvement from him as well." Jeon's brow furrowed. "These hobgoblins — were they red-skinned?"

"I couldn't say — never seen one before."

"I have. They live in the Crystalmist Mountains, which the Duke's duchy backs against, so he has dealings with them. Easier to deal with than most humanoids, in truth — but disconcerting, that they'd be used here."

They went over the rest of it together — the signal from the southern beacon that had warned the fleet off, the strange ship glimpsed through the fog, the Sea Ghost and Cain Toli's supposed command of it. "No doubt that's where the hobgoblins came from," Jeon said, "or the likeliest explanation, at least — we'd seen ships moving back and forth to the caves before. That's where they were storing the powder?"

"Loading it, yes."

"I'll have Jamis send a flotilla to Helm Island," Jeon said, "and we'll retake the caves, recover whatever remains. The area's blockaded for now." He turned then to a gentler line of questioning — the men Paul had traveled with, Merrick and Gouge and Jude among them, whether any beyond that small circle knew of Fairwind's treachery.

"Almost all the surviving Marines in the barracks witnessed parts of it too," Paul admitted.

"How many were with you, specifically?"

"Five. Merrick, Gouge, Jude, Folsom, and Tyrus."

"They'll need debriefing too, quietly — I'll leave that to Jamis, though they answer to him regardless." Jeon considered a moment. "Merrick could be commended for this, I think. Honorably."

"What do you mean?"

"He's obviously loyal to me. Did you choose him?"

"Jamis recommended him."

"They're loyal — I don't think they'd cause trouble. What about Gouge? I worry about him."

"Gouge is fine," Paul said. "Makes quick decisions under pressure. Not much of a threat unless he's threatened himself. Professional, I'd say."

"And this Jude?"

"A good wizard. The job you gave him was to protect me, and he did that well enough. They're all very capable."

"Good." Jeon's tone shifted, settling into something more deliberate, more carefully weighed. "Now, Paul — there's something I want to speak to you about, given what you've told me of your own investigation into your father's history. I've been thinking on this a while. Take it as a suggestion for your own path forward, and how you might best serve your family in these difficult times.

"I've noticed you've taken to something like the ledger domain — spycraft, in a way — and I think that useful. But I knew your father, Grayson, when we were young men, and I was very impressed by his intelligence, and your mother's besides. Both brilliant people. He spoke often of the yellow substance, and how our state would only be truly free once we could produce it ourselves. Are you aware the compound was given to us by the Duke of Berghof, and that he's since stopped supplying it? We've been running low for decades. What remained on Helm Island, even before it was taken, would only have lasted another three years. We've been desperate for it, and now that it's gone, we've neither the time nor the means to produce it ourselves — we simply lack the knowledge. Your father recognized this two decades ago, and meant to change it. Unfortunately, the greatest alchemists all belong to Keoland—"

"Or the Duke of Berghof," Paul finished.

"Exactly. What I'm asking, Paul, is whether you'd dedicate yourself to solving this. I believe you're capable of it. Would you study the making of this substance — pursue it as your own quest, and find a way to produce it?"

Paul turned the question over, feeling the weight of what it would mean. "This would divert me from my present path entirely — I'd have to delve properly into the arcane arts. Is that what you're asking?"

"Most of Oeridian stock are reluctant to go there," Jeon said, "but I see now our neglect of the magical arts has brought us to this point. We need wizards of our own."

"We do."

"Which brings me to my second point. Do you feel Jude is someone you'd be willing to study under?"

"He's a good wizard," Paul said, "very powerful. He could teach me."

"Excellent — I've already asked Lord Jamis to propose the arrangement to him. Jude's a transactional man, from what I understand, but we have things he wants right now. If he could share his knowledge and teach you the arts, it would prove very advantageous to our family — and, honestly, having a new prince of Monmurg with a wizard's abilities would be advantageous in itself.

"So I'm charging you: look into the arcane arts, as your father and mother did before you. I'll give you full leeway in it — do as you wish, so long as it brings no harm to the city or our family's name. There are magics illegal in Keoland and in Berghof both — necromancy comes to mind first — but so long as no one else learns of it, I won't restrict your knowledge, or where you seek it. You'll have full use of the Royal Library, and whatever esoteric components you need, I'll help you acquire. The Grand Market still draws wizards from around the world, even in this difficult year, and I expect we can find most of what you'll require.

"I'll have Jamis speak to Jude about tutoring you, and I'd like to see that begin within the fortnight. I'm charging the two of you together with unraveling this yellow powder — how to make it, where it comes from, everything we need to know. We have months before our supply runs out entirely. There may be other stores to look into — if Fairwind did indeed betray us, he likely holds a large amount of it somewhere on his own island. But I do not wish to go openly to war with him over this."

"Why?" Paul said. "He's a traitor."

"He's a prince of the principalities, with a large force of men and ships."

"So do we."

"True — but we're at war with Gradsul right now. Showing him our internal weakness, our division, would be disastrous."

"So we're just going to let Fairwind get away with it?"

"I did not say that," Jeon said. "The first thing I'd like you and Jude to do is find where he's stored his stockpile, and then we strike. He can play his games; we'll play ours. But there's a clock running on us regardless — it's only a matter of time before our own supply runs dry, and Monmurg's more exposed to the sahaugin than any other principality's holding."

"That makes sense," Paul said. "If the sahaugin could destroy this city, it'd only be a matter of time before everything collapses." He paused, turning something over. "How long does one barrel last, once it's in the water?"

"Not even a week, from what I understand," Jeon said. "The salting ships go out every couple of days with a fresh barrel, though a small one covers a wide range, miles across — the entire Monmurgian Strait, from a single barrel at a time, dumped up near the channel and let the current carry it back this way. We've done it this way for a hundred and fifty years."

"I'll start looking into it."

"There's a political dimension too — I don't want word of Fairwind's treason spreading to the other princes just yet. I'm hoping we can negotiate with him instead, find a way to recover the supply, or at least some part of it."

"You might want to have those Marines stay quiet, then," Paul said.

"I will — I'll leave that to Jamis." Jeon's face eased fractionally. "If I can avoid a war with Fairwind, I very much would like to. I don't want Keoland to see weakness among us — we already look weak enough to that country as it is. Any sign of division among the princes invites direct attack, and I won't risk that.

"There's another matter too — we've looked very weak at sea, and I believe it's tied to some magical talisman Cain Toli holds. We've lost ships we shouldn't have, and until we know what's behind it, I don't want to risk an outright strike on the Keoland fleet. Jamis has ideas on what's causing it; I'll let him share them with you in time. Your own arcane abilities, once developed, may help us counter whatever advantage the Toli hold there. Any knowledge you gain will serve us greatly, and I want you to be my interpreter into that world. I don't trust anyone else with it."

"Not even Jude?"

"Has he given you reason not to trust him?"

"Of course not."

"But he's not family. I've no reason not to use him as an advisor — I would simply rather you be the expert in this."

"That would probably be most effective," Paul admitted. "A wizard-spy."

"Yes — that's what these times require. Good commanders and good fighters aren't quite enough anymore." Jeon studied him a moment longer. "Is there anything you wish to discuss with me?"

"Let me keep these Marines under my own command," Paul said. "My own small group."

"I'll consider it. If you wish to stay in contact with them, I understand — though Merrick holds a fairly high rank, and Jamis has plans for him; he can be drawn on when needed. Gouge is another matter — allocated by Jamis as needed, but if you've a mission requiring his particular skills, I'll see he's made available. Come to me when you've an actual mission, and those four are yours."

"Absolutely."

"Good. In the meantime, continue the work you and Jude were already pursuing — research into the powder, and where Fairwind may have taken his share. That will likely mean sending spies to his island, hiring informants. You've the funds you need for it."

"Got it."

"A good master knows how to delegate — learn that skill too. Let your agents bring information to you; cultivate a network."

"I don't really have agents yet."

"You can get them. Jamis tells me Jude's already quite skilled at it."

"We have one informant," Paul said, "but that was three days ago."

"Find more. Jamis says Jude's particularly gifted at bending people to his will, through various means — learn that from him too, and use him as your cutout, your front man. Tell me what he does, what agents you recruit, and report back what you learn."

"I will."

"Excellent. Go get some rest now — we're done here. You can meet with Jude tomorrow and lay your plans."

Paul rose, was led back out the way he'd come, and found himself, once alone in his own rooms at last, glad simply to be there — peeling off clothes still damp from the shark-infested chasm, singed at the edges from the tower that had gone up in flame behind him, and glad, above all else, to be alive to feel the weight of everything his uncle had just asked of him.
