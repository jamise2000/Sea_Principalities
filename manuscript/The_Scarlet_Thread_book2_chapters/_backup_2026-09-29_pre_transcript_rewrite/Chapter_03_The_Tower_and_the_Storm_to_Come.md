# Chapter Three — The Tower and the Storm to Come

"What might also help me, going forward," Jude said, turning the wine glass slowly in his hand, "is knowing whether you can give me a kill-on-sight list. Would have been helpful to know one existed before I acted on my own account, bringing Fairwind in as I did."

"Fairwind is not a kill-on-sight, Jude," Jamis said, firm. "I do not want him assassinated. He isn't fully aware of the political situation — which means, in the future, he could still come around. Realize where his true loyalty lies, that the Prince of Toli is more dangerous to him than Jeon ever was, and that the game he's playing could destroy him along with everyone else. I don't think any of the princes survive what the Toli mean to bring, if it comes to that."

"I agree with that." Jude turned to the city's more immediate troubles. "How does Monmurg stand, with the embargo on food? Are we suffering mass desertions? Holding together at all?"

"Our stores are going down greatly," Jamis admitted. "We get some food from smugglers running to Redshore — you've seen this yourself, and brought me word of it. My own spies have confirmed what you reported. It's interesting, someone from Redshore approaching you directly — I may have more to say on that in the future. There may be an opening there; I may send you as an envoy to speak with him again.

"But I believe Fairwind and Jeon aren't the only ones being played by the Toli. I have a feeling the sea principalities, and Keoland itself, are being played by other, more dangerous forces still. Do you know what's happening in Keoland right now? A civil war may be imminent there."

"Between whom?"

"The Duke of Gradsul, who by rights should have been crowned king some twelve years past, and was denied it — has been chasing the throne ever since. The man appointed instead, an obscure Oeridian from some back province, has recently died. There is no king currently sitting the throne."

"Any suspicion he was assassinated?"

"The royal alchemist died a month before the king did," Jamis said. "Very suspicious, what's happening there."

"Is the Duke the greater problem for the sea princes, or for Jeon specifically?"

"When I say 'the sea princes,' I mean Jeon specifically, right now — we're on the verge of civil war with the Toli ourselves, depending on where everyone falls. It's already begun, if I'm honest with you. When you speak with Jeon, let him know — Fairwind made a comment, taking the island back from the hobgoblins being a necessity for Monmurg, and Jeon answered that he thought Monmurg would fall without the Helm. So he understands the gravity of it, at least, exactly — and I think, again, he's playing a very dangerous game, for reasons beyond me. He's always been jealous of Monmurg's prosperity, and outspoken that the negotiations over slavery with the Toli and Berghof are unnecessary, idealistic, an attempt to end something older than our own country. Minor things, in the end. Why he's gone to such lengths now is beyond me. Very dangerous — and something I'd like you to try to ascertain, if you can.

"There's a second matter too. The salting ships did their work before the Helm was overrun — why didn't it take effect? Was some magic cast to keep the substance from working as it should? Could someone have been replacing the barrels as they were taken, with something ineffective? I need to know. If someone swapped the barrels for a substitute, that's even more treasonous — more evidence of what's been done. Or is the substance simply growing less effective on its own, the sahaugin adapting, some counter-agent overcoming it? I want to know, and I think you're the one to find out."

"Is the tower equipped with anything," Jude asked, "or is it simply housing?"

"Whatever you want brought there, I'll see done — and of course you may take books to and from for your own research." Jamis paused, something more delicate in what came next. "There is another matter, and I confess I'm hesitant to raise it, but Prince Jeon has insisted. He believes Paul Revero will make an excellent wizard, and wants you to tutor him. He means to push him in that direction."

"I can see he's got a natural talent for it," Jude said. "Made that homunculus entirely on his own."

"He did?" Something like genuine surprise crossed Jamis's face.

"Yes."

Jamis studied him a long moment before continuing. "So — yes, you're fully cleared to research the substance, and I'd like you to research what possibly negated it at the Helm as well."

"Definitely. And I'll bring Paul in on this."

"That's what Jeon wants — he'd like someone in his own family with knowledge of the arcane, and this seems the way to it. The yellow substance was never created by any reigning prince — the Duke of Berghof supplied it to us."

"So you need me to reverse-engineer it."

"Essentially, yes — we've no idea how to produce it ourselves. The Duke began supplying it some twenty, twenty-five years back. The largest stores — the only real stores — were kept on the Helm."

"Do you have any here, in the city?"

"A few barrels in Monmurg. The Helm was built as a storage container, historically — that's where it was kept. But there's another store as well, a significant one, in Selmer Bay, between here and Saltmarsh."

"Still secure, to your knowledge? Does the Toli have any claim on it?"

"It's rotated in possession over the years — the princes trade custody of these stores among themselves." Jamis's expression sobered. "But before we look into that, we need to understand how the Toli are destroying our ships. That's the other thing I want you to investigate — the artifact Gouge tricked Lord Phranck into revealing. Did you hear of this?"

"No."

"An agent from Keoland came to see me last month, around the fifteenth — spoke with an alchemist in the foreign district, then wished to speak with me directly, and was attacked by Toli agents for his trouble. Gouge was hired by one of Phranck's men to guard him; I'd warned them the Toli were watching. What Phranck is, it seems, is what's called an Inquisitor — have you heard the term? A secret society, in truth a government body in Keoland, called the Office of Forbidden Magic. Not widely known, but a kind of magic-policing organization, seeing that wizards don't practice what's forbidden — necromancy, certain conjurations—"

"Demonic conjuration," Jude said.

"Exactly — probably all the things the Scarlet Brotherhood excels at."

"From what I understand, yes."

"Phranck's part of that organization — my own agents confirm it — and he was here trying to learn what the Toli had actually taken. The agents who attacked him, unbeknownst to them, were up against Gouge and Karmirg, my own men acting as his guards. They were defeated before they could finish him, and Phranck tore something free of their minds in the aftermath: the Toli have taken possession of a very powerful, ancient artifact — one I believe grants control over turtle dragons. Our ships vanishing at sea trace back to this, and it's the reason we haven't been able to fight the Keoland fleet effectively."

"Can we still maneuver to Redshore, or to Jerly Bay, without being molested by them? Is it only open-sea shipping that's at risk?"

"The Dragon Isles sit close to both Redshore and Monmurg, so anything north of here carries the risk of Cain Toli and his dragons. Jerly Bay's untouched, but only because the Toli control it themselves — their own thoroughfare to the jungle, unaffected in the least."

"Do you believe the Toli capable of a direct assault on Monmurg?"

"Absolutely not. Our defenses are impenetrable to their armies — there's no way even Keoland's forces, significant as they are, could take this place by storm."

"With the sahaugin s help, if we lack the material to hold them off?"

"There's no true alliance there," Jamis said. "The sahaugin simply go where they can feed — sharks being chummed, more than anything, adding to the chaos rather than serving any real plan. The Toli mean to disrupt us, nothing more, though you can be sure they've no wish to see the sahaugin win outright either — and yes, they do have the means to produce the yellow substance themselves, unlike us.

"So: research the substance, discover whether it's been diminished or thwarted, and why. Try to learn where Fairwind stored his own barrels, if you can. Help Paul in all of this, since that's the charge Jeon means to give him — and take his measure, see what rudiments of the arcane arts he might already grasp."

"Yes, exactly," Jude said, and hesitated a moment before adding, almost idly, "Few people know who Paul's father truly was. I've been mildly curious — though if it's a state secret—"

"I don't think his father lacked for intelligence," Jamis said, choosing his words with visible care. "From what Jeon's told me, and my own dealings with the lad, he's plainly capable himself. His father's own mother was remarkably intelligent — and magical besides."

"Did he suffer some disfiguring injury," Jude asked, "or is he simply shy? I don't need the details — only curious, since I'll always be dealing with him. Should I even ask?"

"You should," Jamis said. "And to be honest, it has nothing to do with disease. He was burned terribly as a young child, in one of his father's great magical experiments gone wrong."

Jude sat with that a moment. "The hell. That's... quite a thing to carry."

"So — the artifact," Jamis said, gathering the threads back together. "Continue researching its nature, and how they're using it against the dragons. The yellow substance. Where Fairwind's hidden his stores. Help Paul develop in all this work, see what he's capable of, and report back to me. Do not let Prince Jeon's loyalties blind you in any of it."

"His loyalty to Fairwind, you mean?"

"His loyalty to everyone. These are dangerous times, and I don't fully trust his judgment, if I'm honest with you."

"Suspect everyone," Jude said.

"Exactly. That ship would never have been lost to the sahaugin if I'd had my way with it — there'd have been a barrel of our own aboard. Jeon's eyes are opening, slowly, but for us to survive, they need to open a great deal faster than they are. Don't let on to Paul about any of this."

"Of course." Jude turned one more matter over before rising to go. "I have in mind — Paul commanded the contingent of twenty Marines, the only ones to survive that ship. Could you give me their names? If they're reassigned back to Monmurg, or held at the Helm — in the sense of the network you mentioned — they'd know both myself and Paul, and carry some sense of solidarity from having survived together."

"I can give you the name of their commander," Jamis said, "and he can speak to the men — though I'd have Paul speak to them himself."

"If you want him to develop in that work, yes — absolutely. Helping men who survived the sahaugin attack is solid ground to build on. And there's money for bribes, if it comes to that."

"That's probably the only thing we have in abundance right now."

"Those men, if they have families," Jude added, "would likely appreciate simply being able to feed them. Unfortunately, food's something none of us have much of either."

An attendant slipped in a moment later to inform Jamis that Gouge had arrived and was waiting to be seen.

"I'm sure you have things to attend to," Jamis said, rising.

"Absolutely," Jude said, and rose as well.

"My attendant will take you back the way you came. Use my residence to collect your things from the foreign district, if you like."

"Will there be keys to the tower made available?"

"Tomorrow — come back to my residence tomorrow, and I'll see you have them."

"Thank you for the wine," Jude said, and let himself be led out, turning over as he went the full weight of everything he'd just been told — a tower, a library, a mission tangled in dragons and forbidden magic and a boy who'd been burned as a child by his own father's ambition, and a city that was, by every account he'd just heard, standing a good deal closer to the edge than anyone within it yet fully understood.
