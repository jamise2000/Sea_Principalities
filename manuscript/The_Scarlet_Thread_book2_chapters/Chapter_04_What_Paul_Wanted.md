## PART TWO: THE FOURTH DAY

# Chapter Four — What Paul Wanted

Some days after the return from Helm Island, Jude and Paul found a quiet hour to sit together and turn over everything each of them had learned, and everything still left undone.

"I'm not sure we should have killed Lord Fairwind," Jude said, turning a cup slowly between his fingers. "Though it may be moot now. I don't know that Jeon and Fairwind could have mended things any other way."

"Did you want him dead?" Paul asked. "I wanted him in custody."

"When we lost him, I wasn't sure what we'd even have done with him beyond arrest — a traitor, guilty of treason plainly enough, but we hadn't the time to sort any of that properly while fighting our way through the castle." Jude studied him a moment. "What exactly did you hear him say, that night? I never asked you outright."

"You already know most of it — he was speaking with the Toli, about disposing of evidence, or selling something off. The Toli answered him, more or less, 'We noticed you took most of it anyway.'"

"Of the powder."

"Yes. He admitted as much himself, once we had him bound."

They picked apart the tangled last hour of the fight together — who had said what to whom on that narrow bridge, whether Fairwind had truly meant to strike Gouge or only stumbled into him in the chaos, what he'd whispered to Folsom in that last moment before breaking free and shouting his warning to the guards below.

"Have you ever actually toured his holdings?" Jude asked. "His own island?"

"Attended a feast there once, at the main port. Never seen the rest of it in any depth."

"So if we need to find where he's stashed things, you'd have no idea where to start."

"Pretty much none."

Jude weighed what kind of man they were hunting. "Is he the sort who'd keep his valuables close at hand, or scattered across separate islands, far from anything obvious?" He turned the question over in his own experience of pirate lords and outer-island princes, and answered it himself before Paul could venture a guess. "Old-school, through and through — pirate tradition to the bone. Men like that live for secret treasures, hidden coves, smuggling routes. If he's true to type, he'll keep several caches, never all his eggs in one basket — one well-defended, obvious decoy, and a handful of genuinely secret stores besides."

"There must be hundreds of little islands out there," Paul said. "Impossible to search them all, even within a day's sail of his main holdings."

"Not infinite," Jude allowed, "but enough to make any search without better information a fool's errand. We'll need shipping logs, records of where he's sailed — official business or otherwise."

"I'll check the library."

"I'll check too, though you'll need to bring me along for proper access." Jude paused. "Do you know what Jamis actually wants from us now? First and plainest — find the powder, recover what we can, recreate it if there's any way to. And he wants me to teach you wizardry properly."

"He said that outright?"

"He did."

"I'm fine with it," Paul said, something settling in him at the admission. "It closes a real gap in the family — we've no spellcasters otherwise."

"He's addressing the weakness directly, then." Jude glanced sideways at him. "What's your intelligence, out of curiosity?"

"I don't actually know, offhand."

"We're probably about equal."

"Doubtful," Paul said, dry.

"Whatever, then — you're not exactly making homunculi out of blood and clay in your spare time."

"I didn't even know that was how it was done."

"You already knew I was teasing you about it."

"That's not an eighteen-intelligence trick, surely."

"What's yours, then?"

"Twenty, apparently."

Jude gave a short, unbothered laugh. "Fine — give me a year or two, I'll catch up. You've got a real propensity for it, at any rate, and the curiosity to match, which matters more."

They turned, more soberly, to the question of timing — fifty days, perhaps three months at the outside, before the last of the powder ran dry, longer still if the twenty-five barrels left behind on Helm Island held secure. Whether they'd be called back to help clear the last of the hobgoblins, or left to their research here in the city; whether the gills Jude had cut from the dead sahaugin might yet hold some trace of a counter-agent, some clue as to why the powder had failed when it should have held.

"There's an alchemist," Paul offered, "just off the square in the foreign district. I've been trying to build a rapport with him — good reason to visit now, between the gills and whatever else we might need."

"Lead the way, then," Jude said, "before these robes start smelling like the docks. I haven't even had the sense to wrap the things properly."

"They have no idea what they're in for," Paul said, shaking his head, "working with you."

Before they parted for the alchemist's door, Paul offered one piece of advice unprompted. "The twenty men who survived the Helm with us — if we hadn't been there, they'd be dead on that ship. I'm not saying hold it over their heads. But find their sergeant, see he's not hoarding what little coin comes his way, and let him pass some of it down to feed the men's families. In times like these, that buys real loyalty."

Jude turned the thought over and found it sound. "And the thief from the guild — the one we didn't put to the poker."

"He already knows who I am."

"Then someone will eventually pay him well to put a knife in a member of the royal family. Could be you. But if he wants to rise faster than a whole life of waiting would get him, that could also be you, if you play it right." Jude considered the whole shape of what Paul had just offered him, unprompted, and found it better counsel than he'd expected. "Good instincts. Start with the soldiers — find out how many of that twenty are still on the island, whether Fairwind's own three hundred are being handled fairly or simply left to rot in whatever hold he penned them in. Then keep cultivating your thief. Even a man who once tried to kill you can be made an asset, with the right patience."

"To be fair," Paul said, "it wasn't a particularly skilled attempt."

"No — I put a bolt clean through his companion's neck for the trouble."

"And then you saved him."

"Out the other side, more or less. We're even, in a way."
