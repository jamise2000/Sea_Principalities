# The Dragon Isles

A reference document, not an in-world artifact — this is the continuity ledger for the Dragon Isles, the two islands north of Jetsom that are home to the turtle dragons, and for the dragons themselves: their nature, the eldest of them (Oceanum Perfide) and the tribute that buys Monmurg's peace, and the way that peace has been turned into a weapon through Cain Toli's artifact. Compiled from what has already been established on the page (the gazetteer section of `The_Sea_Principalities.md`; manuscript Book One Ch. 2–3, 13–14, 21, 25, 31, 33; Book Two Ch. 1–3; `worldbuilding/Helm_Island.md`, `worldbuilding/The_Sahaugin.md`, `worldbuilding/magic_system.md`, `The_Scarlet_Thread_book2_chapters/CONTINUITY.md`). Its purpose is to keep later chapters consistent with what's been shown, and to give a place to check a proposed beat against the isles' established shape before drafting it.

A note on what is known and what is not: the turtle dragons are one of the setting's oldest powers and one of its deepest live mysteries. Almost everything a reader has been told about them arrives second- or third-hand — a smuggler's yarn, a survivor's account through the fog, a spymaster's guess. The creature itself has never been clearly seen on the page. Keep it that way unless a deliberate reveal is intended; the dragons work best as a shape behind weather, not a monster on a slab. Flagged throughout: **[on page]** for what has been shown or stated in the manuscript, **[folk belief]** for in-world rumor treated as rumor, and **[dragon-turtle basis]** for the D&D creature template this species is built on, to be dramatized only as far as canon allows.

## What the Dragon Isles Are — Position & Strategic Role

- **Two fairly large islands, four leagues north of Jetsom Island**, in the Azure Sea (`The_Sea_Principalities.md`, "The Dragon Isles"). A league is roughly three miles, so call it a dozen miles off Jetsom's northern tip — a short sail, near enough that the threat sits athwart Monmurg's own sea lanes rather than out in the deep.
- **Cold northern waters.** The isles lie in the cooler seas north of the outer islands — "well clear of the cold northern waters where the turtle dragons are said to nest" is how the Helm's position is fixed against them (Ch. 17; `Helm_Island.md`). The gazetteer's climate note agrees: north of the islands the water turns cooler and sea-grass replaces the southern corals (`The_Sea_Principalities.md`, "Climate"). This matters — it is why the dragons keep to the north and why an "unnatural fog … in a sea too shallow to breed one honestly" reads as wrong to any sailor who knows the water (Ch. 2).
- **Good harbors, well placed for a forward naval base — and yet unclaimed.** Both isles have decent natural harbors and would make excellent forward stations in the Azure Sea, but neither the Sea Princes nor Keoland has ever tried to claim them, because of the dragons (`The_Sea_Principalities.md`). This is the single most important strategic fact about them: a prize position no power dares hold.
- **They sit astride the northern approaches.** "The Dragon Isles sit close to both Redshore and Monmurg, so anything north of here carries the risk of Cain Toli and his dragons" (Book 2 Ch. 3). Open-sea shipping and the northern runs are the exposed routes; Jerlea Bay stays safe only because the Toli control it themselves as their own thoroughfare to the Amedio (Book 2 Ch. 3). **Spelling:** use **Jerlea Bay** (gazetteer canonical spelling); Book 2 Ch. 3's "Jerly Bay" is a variant to reconcile on a later pass.
- **Ships give them a wide berth** as a matter of course; going too close invites attack (`The_Sea_Principalities.md`). Owen Black has "sailed past them" and speaks of them the way sailors speak of a place you note and leave alone (Ch. 2).

## The Turtle Dragons

**[dragon-turtle basis]** The species is built on the classic dragon turtle: an immense, ancient, aquatic reptile — a dragon in a shell — that lives in deep or coastal water, sinks ships, hoards treasure on the seabed or in its nesting caves, breathes a gout of scalding steam, and is deeply, jealously territorial. Old ones are enormous and cunning, and speak. Use these as the substrate the manuscript's turtle dragons are made from; dramatize each trait only as far as the page has earned it.

What the manuscript has actually established:

- **They nest on the Dragon Isles** and treat the surrounding water as theirs; ships that intrude are attacked (`The_Sea_Principalities.md`). The isles are nesting grounds, not merely a haunt — a detail that implies more than one dragon and successive generations.
- **They are vast.** The one glimpse a reader gets of the thing that comes out of the fog is ship-scaled — "something vast coming out of the mist — like one of the Toli slaving ships, three hundred feet of hull crashing through a corsair before vanishing without a trace" (Ch. 13, survivors' accounts through Jamis). **[on page]** *Handle with care:* this description is deliberately ambiguous between the dragon itself and Cain Toli's ship the *Sea Ghost* (also a ~300-foot slaving hull; `Helm_Island.md`). The confusion is the point — see Open Threads. Do not "clarify" it into a clean sighting without meaning to.
- **They kill through weather, and the weather is theirs.** Every time Monmurg's fleet sallies in force, "a storm rises that has no business existing in these waters," and the fleet limps home or does not come home; three ships have been lost running the blockade (Ch. 3). The manifestations are unnatural fog and storm — "a mist once, thick as a hurricane, in a sea too shallow to breed one honestly" (Ch. 2); "storms that broke, with suspicious punctuality, the moment the fleet cleared the strait" (Ch. 2). **Canon (author decision): the storm-raising is the dragon's own nature, not the artifact's doing.** A turtle dragon of Oceanum Perfide's age can call true fog and storm at sea as a matter of nature; the Orb of the Dragon Turtle only *calls and incites* the creature (and can raise a lesser, artificial fog of its own — see `The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`). So the weather that actually drowns the fleet is the living leviathan loosed, not the relic. Keep it a haze, literally and narratively.
- **[folk belief] They prey on sahaugin as they do on sharks.** This is the popular explanation tied to the century-old warding that kept the shark-folk clear of the Principalities' waters; the Helm's garrison captain wonders aloud whether the sahaugin merely inherited the sharks' instinct to avoid warded water (Ch. 25; `Helm_Island.md`, `The_Sahaugin.md`). Treat as in-world speculation, consistent with the setting's mechanism-with-a-mystery register — do not harden it into fact without a deliberate reveal.
- **They are intelligent enough to bargain.** The tribute arrangement (below) only makes sense if the eldest dragon can hold to a deal, weigh treasure, and choose not to sink a ship it could sink. This is the dragon-turtle's cunning and greed, shown through the arrangement rather than through dialogue — the dragons have not yet spoken on the page.

**Dragon-scale armor.** A set of "dragon-scale armor" exists in the story — Gouge's, taken into Jamis's keeping so his Toli disguise will be convincing (Ch. 13). The text does not state the scale came from a turtle dragon. Plausible and evocative, but **not confirmed**; if you want it to be turtle-dragon scale, that is a small reveal to place deliberately, not to assume.

## Oceanum Perfide and the Tribute

- **The eldest and greatest of the turtle dragons is Oceanum Perfide** (`The_Sea_Principalities.md`). Prince Jeon II pays it a yearly tribute of treasure to keep it from attacking shipping to and from Monmurg. In Jamis's words it is "the greatest of them — the very one we've paid our tribute to for a hundred and fifty years" (Ch. 3).
- **The tribute is a standing, dynastic arrangement, not Jeon's personal innovation.** The gazetteer frames it around Prince Jeon II because he is the reigning payer, but Ch. 3 fixes the practice at roughly **150 years** — generations of Monmurg's princes buying the greatest dragon's forbearance. Jeon inherited the bargain and keeps it.
- **A convergence worth noting (author inference, not yet hardened on the page):** three things date to about the same 150-year mark — the tribute to Oceanum Perfide (Ch. 3), the old fort "built a hundred and fifty years back against the sahaugin" now in Toli hands (Ch. 2), and the warding that has "held for over a century" against the shark-folk (Ch. 25; `The_Sahaugin.md`). The last great sahaugin war, the raising of the warding, and the first tribute to the eldest dragon appear to belong to one founding era of the northern frontier. This alignment is available to be made explicit — it would tie the turtle dragons, the sahaugin, and the powder into a single backstory — but treat it as a thread to pull, not established fact.
- **The name.** "Oceanum Perfide" reads as dog-Latin for *the treacherous / faithless sea* — an apt name for a power you pay so it will not turn on you. Note that the name appears **only in the gazetteer**; no character has spoken it on the page yet. Its first spoken use is a beat still available.

## The Cain Toli / Artifact Thread — the peace turned into a weapon

This is the plot engine the isles drive, and the piece that must stay a mystery. Established:

- **Cain Toli**, a Toli adventurer and princeling, has come into possession of "some rare and terrible artifact" — learned when the evoker Lord Phranck tore it from the dying mind of a Toli assassin (Ch. 3; Book 2 Ch. 3).
- **The artifact is the Orb of the Dragon Turtle, and it commands one dragon at a time.** Full ledger in `The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`. Jamis names it "an artifact that we believe controls one of the turtle dragons out of the Dragon Isles. The greatest of them" (Ch. 3). It commands a single dragon turtle at a time, and Cain Toli has bound it to the largest and most powerful — **Oceanum Perfide**, the eldest, the very dragon Monmurg has paid tribute to for a century and a half. This resolves the earlier wording tension: Book One's "the greatest of them" (Ch. 3) is exact; Book Two's looser "grants control over turtle dragons" / "Cain Toli and his dragons" (Book 2 Ch. 3) is vernacular for the one dragon he wields. The orb is also **mobile** — its own fog is centered on the orb and the dragon must be near to be incited — so the threat travels with Cain (most likely aboard the *Sea Ghost*), rather than hanging as a fixed curse over the isles.
- **What it does to Monmurg.** The bought peace is inverted: instead of sparing Monmurg's ships, the dragon is turned against them. The unnatural storms and fog that wreck sallying fleets, the ships lost running Keoland's blockade, "the reason we haven't been able to fight the Keoland fleet effectively" — all trace back to the artifact (Ch. 3; Book 2 Ch. 3). The Dragon Isles are thus the hidden hinge of the whole blockade: Keoland holds the line at sea only because the Toli have stolen Monmurg's oldest insurance. **The scheme is unified with the powder plot:** Cain uses the dragon to isolate Monmurg *and* to seal off its remaining anti-sahaugin powder stores, so the city, cut off, is left defenseless against the returning sahaugin (`The_Anti-Sahaugin_Powder.md`, `The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`).
- **In-story it is still a mystery.** No one in Monmurg yet knows the artifact is an Orb of Dragonkind; Jamis has set Jude and Paul to research "its nature, and how they're using it against the dragons" (Book 2 Ch. 3; `CONTINUITY.md`). Author-side the nature is now fixed (the Orb of the Dragon Turtle); on the page keep rendering it by effect and pace the reveal deliberately.
- **Cain Toli's island.** Owen Black and a Monmurg tanner both point out a fortified island on the northern approach — an old fort "built a hundred and fifty years back against the sahaugin," now held by "a Toli princeling," from which ships come and go freely by day and around which the fleet-stopping storms rise (Ch. 2). Jamis names it "Cain Toli's island" and notes Jude sailed past it coming in with Owen (Ch. 3). The Toli carry the stolen anti-sahaugin powder "home to Cain Toli's island" (Ch. 31). Treat this fortress-island as Cain Toli's base of operations at or among the Dragon Isles. **Now settled (author ruling):** the fort is **Pocra Sententia** — an ancient Suel fortification and one of the original staging grounds of the anti-sahaugin powder in the sahaugin wars ~150 years ago (`The_Anti-Sahaugin_Powder.md`) — a **separate fortified island near the Dragon Isles, not one of the two isles proper.** It is Cain Toli's fortress and base of operations.
- **The *Sea Ghost*.** Cain Toli's own ship — a ~300-foot Toli slaving hull, crewed by hundreds of hobgoblins and his hand-picked guard — is "tied to the turtle dragons that drown Monmurg's fleet" and prowled the bay during the Helm crisis (Ch. 33, Ch. 41; Book 2 Ch. 1; `Helm_Island.md`). It is still at large going into Book Two (`CONTINUITY.md`). Its relationship to the isles (home port? the artifact's mobile platform?) is unstated.

## How the Dragons Have Been Shown (register guide)

For anyone drafting a scene near the isles or the fog:

- **The reader never sees the dragon plainly.** Its presence is weather (fog "thick as a hurricane," storms with "suspicious punctuality"), a vast half-seen shape, ships that leave and do not return. Survivors speak of "something vast coming out of the mist"; no one describes eyes, shell, or head. Preserve this. A clear on-page sighting is a major card — spend it on purpose.
- **Everything is hearsay, and the hearsay disagrees.** Owen calls them "sea turtles"; the gazetteer and Jamis call them turtle dragons; folk belief makes them sahaugin-eaters; Jamis only "believes" the artifact controls them. Let characters stay uncertain. The mismatch between the tribute (a bargain with a thinking creature) and the storms (a beast loosed as a weapon) is dramatic fuel, not an error to smooth over.
- **No dice, no bare monster-manual name.** Per house style, render the dragon through effect — the wrong fog, the sea gone strange, the hull vanishing — never through exposed mechanics or the words "dragon turtle" as a stat block. "Turtle dragon" is the in-world term and is fine.
