# Albashon's Knowledge Box

A reference document, not an in-world artifact. This is the continuity ledger for the contents of the stone "knowledge box" that Albashon kept in a compartment under his bed. Leslie opened it with his master's password at about 3:30 a.m. on the 5th of Wealsun, 576 CY (`Jude_Paul_and_Leslie_collect_Alchemy_tomes_5th_Wealsun`). The transcript gives the contents as **three books, about eight scrolls, and a cache of small potions**. Jude carried them on his floating disk to Paul's hidden laboratory under the palace.

**Craft rule.** Per `magic_system.md` (*Craft Note*) and CLAUDE.md, the game names, levels and numbers below are author-side scaffolding. On the page, render each spell by its effect, or by Albashon's own title for it (given in italics after each entry), and never by the game name.

---

## Item 1 — Albashon's Spellbook (the master book)

### The book itself

- **What it is.** Albashon's own working grimoire, the book he cast from for decades. It is **not** the spellbook he gave Leslie (`characters/Leslie.md`), which was a smaller apprentice's book copied out for a student. This is the master copy.
- **Language.** Ancient Suel throughout, in Albashon's cramped, practiced hand. Leslie reads it fluently. Jude reads Suel more slowly, and Paul cannot read it yet.
- **Physical description.** A thick quarto bound in dark, oiled sharkskin over wooden boards, with brass corners and a brass clasp. The pages are a mix of vellum and newer rag paper sewn in over the years. Margins are crowded with alchemical sigils, dosage notes and dates. Several pages have chemical burns or old stains. Survived the fire because it was inside the stone box.
- **Organization.** Arranged by school, not by level, which is the habit of a specialist: transmutation first, then the wards (abjuration), then the "lower arts" (necromancy) at the back. A few necromantic pages are written in a deliberately plainer hand with no marginal notes, as if Albashon wanted them to be easy to tear out.

### The caster it implies

The book is scaled to a **10th-level wizard of the School of Transmutation**, which fits Albashon as a "master alchemist and transmuter" (`characters/Minor_characters.md`).

| | |
|---|---|
| **Spell slots** | 1st: 4 · 2nd: 3 · 3rd: 3 · 4th: 3 · 5th: 2 |
| **Highest spell level** | 5th |
| **Spells prepared per day** | Int modifier + 10 (about 15 of the 20 in the book) |
| **Assumed Intelligence** | 20 (+5): **spell save DC 17**, **spell attack +9** |
| **Transmuter features** | Minor Alchemy (2nd), Transmuter's Stone (6th), Shapechanger (10th) |

The book holds **20 spells**, five more than he could prepare on any one day. Cantrips are not recorded in a wizard's book and are left out.

**Distribution.** Spells are weighted toward the lower levels, roughly in proportion to his slots. Transmutation 7, Necromancy 7, Abjuration 6.

| Level | Count | Transmutation | Necromancy | Abjuration |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1st | 5 | 1 | 2 | 2 |
| 2nd | 4 | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| 3rd | 4 | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| 4th | 4 | 2 | 1 | 1 |
| 5th | 3 | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| **Total** | **20** | **7** | **7** | **6** |

**Scaling note.** Where a spell improves when cast with a higher slot, the entry gives its strength at Albashon's highest slot (5th). He has only two 5th-level slots, so he would raise a lower spell that high only when it really mattered.

### 1st-Level Spells (5)

**1. Alarm** — Abjuration (ritual)
1 minute · 30 ft · 8 hours. Wards a door, window or area up to a 20-ft cube. If an uninvited creature enters, it sets off an audible bell or a silent mental alert. Material: a tiny bell and a piece of fine silver wire.
*In-world:* Albashon's "Silver Thread." Probably set on the shop door every night, so he had warning of the raid but not enough time.

**2. Absorb Elements** — Abjuration
Reaction · self · 1 round. When he takes acid, cold, fire, lightning or thunder damage, he gains resistance to that type, and his next melee hit deals extra damage of the same type. **At 5th level: +5d6.**
*In-world:* "Drinking the Spill." It is an alchemist's ward against his own failed reactions. The margin note reads "for when the retort cracks."

**3. False Life** — Necromancy
1 action · self · 1 hour. Gives 1d4 + 4 temporary hit points. **At 5th level: 1d4 + 20.** Material: a small amount of alcohol or distilled spirits.
*In-world:* "The Borrowed Hour." The old man probably used it on long nights at the bench, which may explain how he stayed on his feet at his age.

**4. Ray of Sickness** — Necromancy
1 action · 60 ft · instant. A ranged spell attack dealing 2d8 poison damage, and the target must make a Constitution save or be poisoned. **At 5th level: 6d8.**
*In-world:* "Green Breath." It is one of his few offensive spells, and the margin is mostly about which antidotes counter it.

**5. Feather Fall** — Transmutation
Reaction · 60 ft · 1 minute. Up to five falling creatures drift down at 60 ft per round and land unharmed.
*In-world:* "The Slow Descent." It sits beside a sketch of the shop's spiral stair down to the sewers.

### 2nd-Level Spells (4)

**6. Arcane Lock** — Abjuration
1 action · touch · until dispelled. Magically locks a door, chest or container. A password set by the caster suppresses the lock for 1 minute. Material: gold dust worth 25 gp, consumed.
*In-world:* "The Sealed Word." **This is the spell on the knowledge box itself.** The "magic word" Leslie spoke to open it was the password Albashon set. That Leslie knew it is strong evidence of how far Albashon trusted him.

**7. Gentle Repose** — Necromancy (ritual)
1 hour (ritual) · touch · 10 days. Protects a corpse from decay and from being raised as undead. Material: a pinch of salt and a copper piece on each eye.
*In-world:* "Salt and Copper." An alchemist's preservation working, used for specimens and for bodies he wanted to examine. Paul recovered Albashon's corpse, so this page matters (see Story Hooks).

**8. Enlarge/Reduce** — Transmutation
1 action · 30 ft · concentration, 1 minute. Doubles or halves a creature's or object's size. Unwilling targets make a Constitution save.
*In-world:* "The Greater and the Lesser." Most of his notes are about objects, not people: shrinking reagents to carry them, enlarging a crucible.

**9. Alter Self** — Transmutation
1 action · self · concentration, 1 hour. Gives gills and webbed limbs for breathing water, changes his appearance, or grows natural weapons.
*In-world:* "The Mask of Flesh." The gills option is heavily annotated, so he likely spent time in the harbor, possibly collecting samples for the powder research.

### 3rd-Level Spells (4)

**10. Speak with Dead** — Necromancy
1 action · 10 ft · 10 minutes. A corpse with a mouth answers five questions, using only what it knew in life. It cannot be used on the same corpse more than once in 10 days.
*In-world:* "The Last Five Questions." It is one of the plainly written pages. See Story Hooks: the obvious body to use it on is Albashon's own.

**11. Life Transference** — Necromancy
1 action · 30 ft · instant. The caster takes 4d8 necrotic damage, which cannot be reduced, and one creature he can see regains twice that much. **At 5th level: 6d8, healing twice that.**
*In-world:* "The Bleeding Gift." **This is the clearest link to his work among the victims of the Gift of Insamiar.** It is a spell that heals other people by spending the caster's own life. Albashon was taking pain relief to the poisoned (`Jude_and_Paul_interrogation_cont_5th_Wealsun`). This page shows he sometimes paid for it out of his own body. The margins carry a tally of marks, which may be a count of patients.

**12. Dispel Magic** — Abjuration
1 action · 120 ft · instant. Ends spells of 3rd level or lower on a target. Higher-level spells need an ability check against DC 10 + the spell's level. **At 5th level:** ends spells of 5th level or lower automatically.
*In-world:* "The Unweaving." A transmuter's standard tool for undoing what another transmuter has done.

**13. Haste** — Transmutation
1 action · 30 ft · concentration, 1 minute. Doubles one creature's speed, gives +2 AC and advantage on Dexterity saves, and grants an extra limited action. When it ends, the target loses a turn to exhaustion. Material: a shaving of licorice root.
*In-world:* "The Quickening." A note warns about using it on the elderly, perhaps written from experience.

### 4th-Level Spells (4)

**14. Polymorph** — Transmutation
1 action · 60 ft · concentration, 1 hour. Turns a creature into a beast whose challenge rating is no higher than the target's. Unwilling targets make a Wisdom save. Material: a caterpillar cocoon.
*In-world:* "The Changing of the Shape." **This page directly relates to Jude**, who was half-transformed toward a serpent by the Yuan-ti and still has the snake eyes. It also relates to the Yuan-ti themselves (`The_Yuan-ti.md`). The margins compare ordinary polymorph with a "willed and lasting" change. That could be Albashon studying what the Scarlet Brotherhood does to its people (see Story Hooks). His 10th-level Shapechanger feature also lets him take a beast form himself without spending a slot.

**15. Stone Shape** — Transmutation
1 action · touch · instant. Reshapes a stone object of Medium size, or a section up to 5 ft in any dimension, into any form.
*In-world:* "The Mason's Hand." **This is how the hidden stone compartment under his bed was made**: there are no seams because there were never any joints.

**16. Blight** — Necromancy
1 action · 30 ft · instant. Drains moisture and vitality: 8d8 necrotic damage, half on a successful Constitution save. Plants take maximum damage, and it does nothing to the undead or constructs. **At 5th level: 9d8.**
*In-world:* "The Withering." His notes treat it as a way to dry and render reagents, not as a weapon. Even so, this is the most dangerous page in the book.

**17. Mordenkainen's Private Sanctum** — Abjuration
10 minutes · 120 ft · 24 hours. Wards an area up to a 100-ft cube against any or all of the following: sound passing out, vision from outside, divination sensors, and planar travel in or out. Cast on the same spot every day for a year, it becomes permanent. Material: a sheet of lead, glass, wool, a broomstraw and chrysolite powder.
*In-world:* "The Closed House." A note suggests the workshop and bedroom were made permanent years ago. That would explain why no one could scry what he was working on, so the Scarlet Brotherhood had to come in person, through the front door. The ward does nothing against a knife through a door slit.

### 5th-Level Spells (3)

**18. Telekinesis** — Transmutation
1 action · 60 ft · concentration, 10 minutes. Moves a creature or an object of up to 1,000 lb. A creature resists with a contested check.
*In-world:* "The Unseen Hands." A careful alchemist's way of handling what should not be touched: hot vessels, volatile mixtures, raw powders that kill if breathed (`The_Anti-Sahaugin_Powder.md`).

**19. Enervation** — Necromancy
1 action · 60 ft · concentration, 1 minute. A tendril of dark energy deals 4d8 necrotic damage (Dexterity save for half, which ends the spell). On later turns the caster can repeat the damage automatically, and he regains half the necrotic damage dealt as hit points.
*In-world:* "The Drawing Off." This page is on different, older vellum and the ink has faded, as if it were copied from someone else's book long ago. The margin is the only one where Albashon wrote a single word: "No."

**20. Planar Binding** — Abjuration
1 hour · 60 ft · 24 hours (at 5th level). Binds a celestial, elemental, fey or fiend that stays within range for the whole casting to the caster's service, unless it succeeds on a Charisma save. Material: a jewel worth at least 1,000 gp, consumed.
*In-world:* "The Binding of the Willing and Unwilling." It is close kin to the Suel soul-gem and lithomancy tradition (`magic_system.md`, *Lithomancy and soul gems*). The jewel requirement fits the gem-craft lineage of Zafar Azane and the Arcane Order.

### What the book does *not* contain (continuity guard)

- **No powder recipe.** Per `magic_system.md`, the anti-sahuagin powder is alchemy, not magic. Its formula, if it is anywhere in the box, belongs in the other two books or the scrolls, not among these spells.
- **No cure for the Gift.** Albashon's prisoner says nothing can stop the Gift of Insamiar. Albashon could only ease its effects (`Jude_and_Paul_interrogation_cont_5th_Wealsun`). The spellbook holds Life Transference and a few wards, not a cure.
- **No evocation and no illusion.** He never needed fire or tricks of the eye. This keeps the book useful to Jude (an evoker) and Paul (an illusionist in training) without overlapping their home schools.

### Story hooks

1. **Speaking with the master.** Gentle Repose (spell 7) and Speak with Dead (spell 10) are both here, and Paul has Albashon's body. Someone strong enough could ask the old man five questions. Leslie cannot cast 3rd-level spells yet, which gives the party a reason to want it and a cost to bear in getting it.
2. **Jude's eyes.** The Polymorph margins about a "willed and lasting" change could be the first written clue to what was done to Jude, and to whether Dispel Magic can undo any of it.
3. **The tally marks.** The marks beside Life Transference show how many Gift victims Albashon treated. They could also lead to the people he treated.
4. **The page marked "No."** Enervation, copied from an unknown older book, suggests that Albashon once had a different teacher or source. This connects to the *Magnum Opus* and its "bread and wine" (`magic_system.md`, *The Office of Forbidden Magic*).
5. **Inheritance.** Leslie claims the box as "my inheritance." This book is what Paul's offer of a lab and royal backing is actually bargaining over, even though Paul only wants the recipe.

## Item 2 — Albashon's Annotated *Magnum Opus*

### What it is

Albashon's personal working copy of the ***Magnum Opus* of Ujor Udias**, the foundational treatise on transmutation, written in Ancient Suel (`Name_Normalization_Key.md`; `The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`). This is the book Albashon taught Leslie from (`characters/Leslie.md`). It is not the original but a copy of the **edited text**, the version the Office of Forbidden Magic produced after Paracelsus (`The_Homunculi.md`). **It is probably as old as Albashon himself.** He has owned and worked in it for most of his life and turned it into a record of his own work.

**The book is not magical.** It holds no spells, enchantments or wards. Its value is entirely in what it teaches.

### Physical description

- **Binding.** A large, heavy folio that Albashon has **spliced and rebound himself many times**. The current covers are scuffed calfskin over boards, the spine has been repaired with linen tape, and the leather is worn pale where a thumb has opened it thousands of times. Brass bosses protect the corners, and one is missing. The sewing is visibly uneven from one gathering to the next, a record of different rebindings.
- **Interleaved and spliced.** Albashon **rebound it with a blank leaf between every printed leaf**, the way a scholar prepares a book he means to argue with. On almost every blank leaf he wrote commentary opposite the passage it explains. Over the years he spliced in more: extra gatherings where a chapter needed more room, folded sheets of diagrams, and replacement leaves where old ones wore through. The paper varies from one section to the next, and so does the book's thickness.
- **Two layers of text.**
  - *Ujor's text* is in a formal Ancient Suel book hand. It is deliberately obscure, built on allegory, metaphor and coded terms such as the green lion, the king's bath and the "bread and wine." That obscurity is why the *Magnum Opus* has a reputation for being nearly unreadable.
  - *Albashon's notes* are mostly in **Common**, the plain trade tongue, with Suel technical terms kept and glossed. He wrote them so that a student, almost certainly Leslie, could follow them. They put Ujor's allegories into practical terms: what "the dragon devours its tail" means at the bench, at what temperature, in which vessel and for how long.
- **Layers of ink.** The notes span decades, and the ink changes with them: iron-gall gone brown in the oldest notes, then black, then a sharp blue-black in the most recent. A reader can see Albashon's thinking change over time. Some early notes are crossed out with a later note beside them ("Wrong. See leaf 212.").
- **Experiment records.** Throughout the book Albashon recorded his own trials with the date, the materials, the quantities, the result and a mark for success or failure. Failures outnumber successes, and he recorded both. Some entries have small diagrams of apparatus: retorts, an athanor (furnace), a pelican vessel.
- **The Office's edits, marked.** Albashon clearly knew the text had been censored. The Office's cuts are crude: passages that stop mid-argument, cross-references to sections that no longer exist, and a change of copying hand where material was patched over. Albashon marks each of these places in the margin, for example "Cut here." or "Something was removed. The argument does not join." He notes the gaps but does not claim to know what was taken out. In particular, **he does not reconstruct the homunculus passage** (Selrotin's "bread and wine" process).
- **Wear and damage.** Chemical stains, wax drips, a scorch along one fore-edge and pages soft with handling. It survived the fire because it was inside the stone box. It smells of sulfur and old smoke.

### How it is organized

Ujor's treatise follows the four classical stages of the Great Work. Albashon's notes are thickest in the chapters he understood best, and that is what limits the book's teaching value (see *Mechanical effect*).

| Stage | Ujor's subject | Albashon's notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Nigredo** (the blackening) | Breaking matter down by putrefaction and dissolution. In-world legend ties Insamiar to this stage (`Jude_and_Paul_interrogation_cont_5th_Wealsun`), but Albashon's notes here keep to bench practice. | Dense and confident. His experiments are complete and repeated. |
| **Albedo** (the whitening) | Purifying and washing what has been broken down. | Dense and confident. |
| **Citrinitas** (the yellowing) | Awakening the solar principle in matter. | Thorough but more cautious. Many entries are marked "repeat to confirm." |
| **Rubedo** (the reddening) | The Great Work completed: the red stone. | **Thin.** Mostly questions and struck-out guesses. |

### The last leaves: the Gift of Insamiar

After the rubedo chapter, on the final blank leaves at the **back of the book**, Albashon kept a few short notes on the **Gift of Insamiar**, which he was studying but had **made little progress** on. They are dated late, in the blue-black ink of his last years, and **very brief**: a handful of lines and observations with nothing worked out and no experiment carried through. They are not keyed to any passage of Ujor's text, and they add nothing to the book's teaching value.

The last of them is underlined twice:

> "I do not yet understand what he means by the willing gift."

**Author ruling:** this line refers to the **Gift of Insamiar**, and "he" is Ujor Udias. **It will be explained in the third book (Item 3).** Do not resolve it from this book.

### Mechanical effect

**Benefit.** A **wizard of the School of Transmutation** who studies this book during their training **trains at double the normal rate when advancing to wizard levels 5, 6, 7 and 8**. Whatever training time or downtime the table requires for gaining a level is **halved** for those four levels. If training has a daily upkeep cost, that cost is halved too, because there are half as many days.

**Requirements.**
- **Must be a transmuter.** Albashon's notes assume a transmuter's habits of mind and his specific methods. A wizard of another school (Jude the evoker, Paul the illusionist in training) can read it with interest and learn from it, but gets no mechanical benefit.
- **Must read Ancient Suel.** The notes are in Common, but each one is keyed to a passage of Ujor's text and makes no sense without it. Leslie can read Suel. Jude can, more slowly. Paul cannot yet.
- **Must have access to the book during training.** The benefit comes from working through it, not from having read it once.

**Why only levels 5–8.**
- **Below 5th:** Albashon wrote for an apprentice who already knew the basics. The book takes the fundamentals for granted, so a wizard still learning them gets nothing extra from it.
- **5th–6th:** The nigredo and albedo chapters, where his notes are strongest.
- **7th–8th:** The citrinitas chapter. The notes are still good enough to teach from.
- **9th and above:** The rubedo notes are only questions. Albashon reached his own 9th and 10th levels by work he never recorded here, and a reader who gets this far is past the edge of his written understanding and back to Ujor's riddles alone.

**What it does not do.**
- It does not teach or contain any spell, and it cannot be used as a spellbook.
- It does not speed up copying spells into a spellbook (the transmuter's existing savant discount still applies as normal).
- It does not stack with any other training aid that halves time.
- **It does not contain the anti-sahuagin powder recipe.** The powder is a Duke of Berghof invention, not Ujor's work (`The_Anti-Sahaugin_Powder.md`). Any notes Albashon made on it are not in this book unless the author decides otherwise.

### Who benefits

- **Leslie**, a **2nd-level transmuter** (`characters/Leslie.md`), is the obvious reader: it is his master's teaching in his master's voice, and he already reads Suel. At 2nd level the book is a promise for later, not a present advantage. Its benefit begins when he advances to 5th level.
- **Jude** gets no mechanical benefit (he is an evoker), but he has read Ujor's work before (Ch. 12) and will recognize what Albashon's notes do to that text.
- **Paul** gets no benefit, and he is training as an illusionist.

### Danger and value

- **Keoland would want it destroyed.** The Office of Forbidden Magic edited the *Magnum Opus* after Paracelsus was burned, and Zafar Azane runs that office now (`magic_system.md`, `The_Homunculi.md`). A copy whose margins plainly decode Ujor's euphemisms is exactly what the Office exists to suppress.
- **It is worth a fortune to the right buyer**, such as the Arcane Order of Port Toli, any serious transmuter, or the Scarlet Brotherhood, who killed the man who wrote in it.
- **Leslie will not part with it.** Of everything in the box, this is the item he means when he says "my inheritance."

### Author rulings (settled)

1. **The edited text.** Albashon's copy is **not** older than the Office of Forbidden Magic's edit, so it lacks the homunculus passage. This matches Jude's copy (Ch. 12). Albashon knew the text had been censored, from its strange omissions and crude patching, and marked those places, but he did not restore what was removed.
2. **Only brief notes on the Gift.** Albashon was studying the Gift of Insamiar but made little progress, so this book holds only a few brief notes on it, on the last leaves at the back. They end with the underlined "willing gift" line, which the third book will explain. His treatment work for its victims (`Jude_and_Paul_interrogation_cont_5th_Wealsun`) is not recorded here.
3. **Age.** It is Albashon's personal copy, spliced and rebound by him many times, and **probably as old as he is.** This avoids the open date question for Ujor's original text in `Name_Normalization_Key.md`, which is left for the author.
4. **Not the shop copy.** The *Opus* Albashon kept on the counter and showed Jude on the 2nd of Wealsun (`Jude_2nd_Wealsun`; Book One Ch. 4) is a **second, unannotated copy he bought in Port Toli some twenty years ago**. This annotated copy is the one he kept hidden, and it is the one in the box.

## Item 3 — Albashon's Journal

Albashon's personal journal and daybook, kept for years. Most of it is a working ledger: experiments, reagent costs, stores used, potions made and sold. Beginning about a year before the raid, the entries record his observations of the Warren: torpor, then irrationality, then violence. They track his slow realization that the cause is the **Gift of Insamiar**, and his meeting with **Lord Phranck** on the **15th of Readying, 576 CY**. The last entry is the morning of the 4th of Wealsun, 576 CY. Author ruling: this book explains what Albashon meant by **"the willing gift"** (see Item 2, *The last leaves*).

**Full text (final):** `Albashons_Journal.md`.

## Item 4 — The Scrolls (eight recipes)

### The scrolls themselves

- **What they are.** Eight rolled recipes, each a fair copy of a formula Albashon had worked out and trusted. They are **not spell scrolls**: no one can cast from them. They are working instructions, written so that someone else could compound each preparation without him.
- **Language.** **Common**, like his notes in the *Magnum Opus*, with Suel technical terms kept and glossed. They were written for Leslie.
- **Physical description.** Long sheets of rag paper, each rolled on a plain wooden rod and tied with a strip of dyed linen. The color of the tie shows the class of preparation: **white** for the treatment, **red** for the healing draught, **green** for the other six. Each scroll gives a title, the ingredients and their cost, the method, the dose, and a line of warnings. The seven potion scrolls are older, in black ink that has begun to brown. The treatment scroll is the newest, in the blue-black ink of his last year.
- **What they do not contain.** **No powder recipe** (see the continuity guard under Item 1) and **no cure for the Gift**.

### Pricing rule (author-side scaffolding)

Ingredient costs follow the 5e crafting rule for consumables in *Xanathar's Guide to Everything*: **half the base cost of the item**. That gives **25 gp of materials for a common potion** and **250 gp for an uncommon one**. Market price is taken as **double the ingredient cost** (50 gp common, 500 gp uncommon). The potion of healing matches the *Player's Handbook* price of 50 gp, which is also what Gedeon paid on the 15th of Readying (`Albashons_Journal.md`).

Gem values use the 5e gemstone tables (moonstone, jasper, carnelian, bloodstone and rose quartz at 50 gp; chrysoberyl, garnet, jet and pearl at 100 gp). Trade goods follow the *Player's Handbook* where it lists them (saffron 15 gp per lb, a glass vial 1 gp).

**Magic in the making.** The **treatment** is pure alchemy and needs no magic, like the anti-sahuagin powder (`magic_system.md`). The **healing draught** can be made by anyone skilled with an herbalist's kit, as in 5e. **The other six need a spellcaster** to work the matching magic into the brew as it cools. Each scroll names that magic by its effect, never by a spell name. This is why Albashon could teach the treatment to anyone, but only a wizard could take over the green scrolls.

### Scroll 1 — The White Draught *(white tie)*

**Treatment for the black sickness (the Gift of Insamiar). Not a cure.**

Dated **28th of Readying, 576 CY**, the day he began giving it in the Warren (`Albashons_Journal.md`). Headed in his hand: *"For Leslie, if I am not here to make it."*

**Ingredients, one jar (one month for one patient). Cost about 30 gp.**

| Ingredient | Quantity | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Milk of poppy (the dried tears of the white poppy, steeped) | 2 oz | 22 gp |
| White willow bark, powdered | 4 oz | 1 gp |
| Chalk, washed and levigated | 6 oz | 5 sp |
| Isinglass (sturgeon's bladder glue): the binder | 1 oz | 4 gp |
| Honey | 1 lb | 5 sp |
| Spirits of wine, twice distilled | 1 pint | 1 gp |
| Stoneware jar with a waxed stopper | 1 | 1 gp |
| **Total** | | **30 gp** |

**Method (as written).** Steep the poppy in the spirits for three days in the dark. Boil the willow separately and strain it. Dissolve the isinglass in a little warm water and never let it boil, or it will not bind. Combine everything over a low fire, adding the chalk last, a spoon at a time, until the liquor turns white and will not take more. Pour it into the jar warm. When it cools it sets to the thickness of cream.

**The binder.** This is the secret the journal calls "a binder of my own." The isinglass holds the poppy and willow in a gel that the gut breaks down slowly, so the relief comes over the whole day instead of all at once. Without it, the same dose sedates for an hour and then lets the pain and rage come back worse.

**Dose.** One spoon morning and night. A full jar keeps one patient for a month, and a quarter jar for a week. Sold at **50 gp** a jar (the price Albashon quoted Lord Phranck). In the Warren it was given free.

**Warnings (as written).** *"It does not touch the poison. It eases the pain and the rage and slows the slide. It is no use to those who no longer know their own names. The poppy is a chain of its own: do not give more than the dose, and do not stop it all at once."*

**Mechanical effect (author-side, canon).** While a victim of the Gift takes the full dose, the sickness **does not advance to its next stage** (torpor, unreason, rage, the body's ruin), and the taker has **advantage on saving throws against the Gift's rage and madness effects**. It has **no effect on someone already in the final stage**, and it **cures nothing**: when the dosing stops, the sickness resumes from where it was. **Dependence:** after four weeks of continuous dosing, missing a day's dose leaves the taker **poisoned** until the next dose, or for 1d4 days if no more is given.

### Continuity notes and author rulings

1. **Shop prices (settled).** The journal ledger for the 7th of Reaping, 572 CY now uses these prices: the divers' water-breathing elixirs sell at 500 gp each, and the mundane restoratives, sleep draughts and ointments at a few silver (`Albashons_Journal.md`, ruling 14).
2. **The White Draught mechanic is canon** as written under Scroll 1.
3. **Who can brew what.** The matching magic for each green scroll has a minimum spell level. A brewer must be able to cast magic of that level and have the spell in their book.

| Scroll | Matching magic (author-side) | Minimum spell level |
|---|---|---|
| 1. The White Draught | none (alchemy) | — |
| 2. The True Draught | none (herbalist's kit) | — |
| 3. The Gecko's Tincture | Spider Climb | 2nd |
| 4. The Diver's Elixir | Water Breathing | 3rd |
| 5. The Greater Measure | Enlarge/Reduce | 2nd |
| 6. Salamander Oil | Absorb Elements | 1st |
| 7. The Eel's Coat | Grease, then Freedom of Movement | 4th |
| 8. The Heart's Philter | Charm Person | 1st |

**Leslie is a 2nd-level wizard of the School of Transmutation** (author ruling; `characters/Leslie.md`). He casts only 1st-level magic, so today he can brew **the White Draught**, **the True Draught**, and **Salamander Oil** once he copies Absorb Elements (*Drinking the Spill*) from Albashon's spellbook (Item 1). He can also brew **the Heart's Philter** if charm magic is in his own book. That book's five spells are not yet recorded. Charm Person is an enchantment and is not in Albashon's book. He reaches 2nd-level spells (the Gecko's Tincture, the Greater Measure) at wizard level 3 and 3rd-level spells (the Diver's Elixir) at level 5. The Eel's Coat waits until level 7.

## Item 5 — The Potion Cache (eight vials)

A small wooden rack of **eight stoppered glass vials**, packed in straw at the bottom of the box (`Jude_Paul_and_Leslie_collect_Alchemy_tomes_5th_Wealsun`: "a cache of these small potions"). Author ruling: **four White Draughts and four True Draughts.** Nothing else.

| Vials | Preparation | Recipe | Contents and effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 | **The White Draught** (black sickness treatment) | Scroll 1 | Each vial holds **a quarter jar: one week's dose for one patient**, the same measure Albashon poured for Lord Phranck on the 15th of Readying (`Albashons_Journal.md`). White, the thickness of cream, sealed with wax. Effect as given under Scroll 1. |
| 4 | **The True Draught** | Scroll 2 | **5e: Potion of Healing** (common): restores 2d4 + 2 hit points. Clear red, glitters when shaken. |

**What the cache adds up to.**
- **White Draughts:** four vials are **one month for one patient, or one week for four**. Albashon priced a quarter jar at about 12½ gp (Phranck paid 15 gp for one). In the Warren he gave it free.
- **True Draughts:** four potions at 50 gp each, **200 gp** at market.

**Story hook (author-side).** The last journal entry, on the morning of the 4th of Wealsun, ends: "Tansy did not come for the bottles this morning." The four White Draughts can be the vials set aside for Mother Tansy's Warren patients, locked away when she failed to come. Whoever holds them now holds a week of relief for four of Albashon's patients, and Leslie knows who they were.

**Continuity notes.**
- **No Transmuter's Stone.** The cache contains only these eight vials. If Albashon had a Transmuter's Stone (he is a 10th-level transmuter, Item 1), it is not in the box.
- Leslie can make more of both (Item 4, *Who can brew what*). Neither needs magic.
