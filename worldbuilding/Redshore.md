# Redshore — the island and the city

A reference document and location description: **Redshore**, the whaling island off the Salinmoor coast and the faded old city upon it, now the chokepoint of Keoland's blockade of Monmurg and the gateway through which smugglers feed the starving city. Compiled from established canon — `worldbuilding/Guide_to_Salinmoor.md` (the parent gazetteer; Redshore and the Lorchester/Secundforth split), `characters/Earl_of_Redshore.md`, and manuscript Ch. 1 (Jude's arrival), Ch. 2, Ch. 43–44, 46–47, and Book 2 Ch. 1–3. Where this file and the gazetteer overlap, the gazetteer is the source of record; this file adds the lived-in description and the story-present state.

## The Island

Redshore is an island roughly **ten miles by twelve, lying some eight miles off the coast of Salinmoor**, with a channel of open water between it and the mainland shore (`Guide_to_Salinmoor.md`; Ch. 1). It is the **oldest settlement in the Province of Salinmoor** — the seat the first Viscount chose — and, for all its faded glory, still the largest and most cosmopolitan place in that backwater province.

The land is more than a fishing rock. Its **interior hills** carry timber and some mineral wealth — not enough to export, but enough to keep Redshore largely self-sufficient — and they give the island relief from the coastal heat that the low mainland marshes never get. The **coasts are rocky**, protection enough against a hostile landing (though less formidable than the cliffs of Angler Island to the west). But the island's fortune has always come from the water: Redshore sits **on the migratory path of several whale species**, and whaling has been its lifeblood since its founding — the trade that made its ruling family, the **Lorchesters**, the richest house in all Salinmoor.

## The City & Waterfront

Seen from an incoming deck at evening, Redshore is **not the fishing hamlet its reputation promises** (Ch. 1). It is a city — "a faded sprawl of stone climbing the island's hills behind a working waterfront of rendering houses and oil sheds," grown rich on whales that ran these waters before Monmurg was a power. Its best days are behind it and it shows, but it remains grander and more worldly than anything else in the province.

The waterfront is a **whaler's waterfront**, and that fact governs everything now. Redshore was built to butcher and boil whales, not to berth a merchant fleet: it has a **whaler's wharf and a beach**, a handful of working quays, and the sheds and try-works of the oil trade crowding the shore. The air near the water carries the rendering-houses — smoke and the thick reek of boiling blubber. Above the harbor the town climbs the hills in weathered stone, old grandeur gone thin.

The one structure that could be called a fortification is the **Earl's castle** — and only charitably (`characters/Earl_of_Redshore.md`). It is a small stone fortress-keep built for a siege from the sea and none at all from the land, reached by a drawbridge rickety enough that a warhorse must cross it with care; the whole of it would fit inside a single wing of Prince Jeon's palace in Monmurg. A worn, once-proud Suel crest hangs above the mantel of its one hall grand enough to receive a guest. Its household is threadbare — the cook lately dead, meat and bread the best the kitchen can manage.

Dockside, the tavern to know is **the Whale** — loud, low, the sort of place a scarred smuggler like Owen Black holds court (`characters/Owen_Black.md`; Ch. 1–2).

## Rulers — the Lorchesters and the Earl

Redshore is held by the **Lorchester line**, whose head in the story present carries the title **Earl of Redshore** (also called, loosely, a baron or lord in passing; Book 2 Ch. 2–3). The Lorchesters were once **Viscounts of all Salinmoor**, until the collapse of Keoland's southern empire brought the **Secundforths** in as Viscounts, seated inland at Burle, and pared the Lorchesters back to Redshore (`Guide_to_Salinmoor.md`). They remain the richest family in the province on the strength of the whale trade, and on the coastal islands their word, not the Secundforths', is what carries.

The **island-versus-mainland split** is worth keeping active in any Redshore scene: the island communities are wealthier, prouder, and more dynamic, behaving as near-independent holdings that pay the Secundforths only lip service; the mainland is poor and static; each sees itself as the "true" Salinmoor, and the two largely ignore one another (`Guide_to_Salinmoor.md`).

The current Earl is a proud, brittle, part-Flannish Suel lord past fifty — soldier once, lonely now, a moral pragmatist who enforces the Duke of Gradsul's war while all but admitting the anti-slavery cause is a pretext for an old grudge. Full character in `characters/Earl_of_Redshore.md`.

## The Blockade & the Stranded Harbor (story present)

This is Redshore's role in the plot, and its current character. Under the **Keoland embargo of Monmurg**, all shipping bound for the city is **diverted to Redshore for inspection** (Ch. 1). The **Earl of Redshore is the Duke of Gradsul's commander of the blockade flotilla here**: his **two men-of-war**, flying the red-and-black lion of Keoland over the Duke's standard, intercept Monmurg-bound traders and funnel them under escort into his overcrowded channel (`characters/Earl_of_Redshore.md`; Ch. 1).

The result is a harbor drowning in ships it was never built to hold:

- **Forty-odd merchantmen lie at anchor** in the channel between the island and the mainland — more hulls than Redshore could unload in a season (Ch. 1; `characters/Earl_of_Redshore.md`). *(Owen later rounds it to "fifty merchant ships," Ch. 44 — his loose count; the narration's "forty-odd" is the figure of record.)*
- **The beach has become a raw, improvised market** — kegs, stalls, canvas awnings — merchants hawking to men who have nowhere else to spend and nothing to do but wait out a war, "half-mad to sell at any price."
- **The trade is rotting in place:** no moorage for this volume, a month or a season to empty the holds if they ever find the room (Ch. 1). Fortunes are quietly dying at anchor.

Into that gap runs the **smuggling economy** that makes Redshore matter to Monmurg's survival: men like **Owen Black** buy cheap off the desperate — food, oil, cider — and run it past the patrols into the starving city (Ch. 44, 46; Book 2 Ch. 3). Redshore's own defense is thin for how much now depends on it: Owen counts only about **three warships here, two more at Seaton, one at Saltmarsh**, captains who plainly wish they were elsewhere — "one good strike and the whole blockade's ash" (Ch. 44). And the sea itself is turning dangerous again: the **sahaugin are stirring**, taking men within a mile of the island (Ch. 44).

## People & Culture

Redshore's folk are **whalers and oil-renderers** at root, an islander population that is (like all Salinmoor) a near-even melting pot of Suel and Oeridian blood, ruled by a Suel-flavored aristocracy that leans on old courtesies (the paired wine-goblets, the crest on the wall) to prop up a claim time has worn thin. The blockade has swollen the town with **stranded merchant crews and idle sailors**, and given the beach over to a hand-to-mouth market economy that suits smugglers and cutpurses better than honest traders. Off the island's coasts, as along all of Salinmoor, dwell **aquatic (sea) elves**, generally keeping to themselves, with halflings as their go-betweens (`Guide_to_Salinmoor.md`) — regional color available if a scene wants it.

## Strategic & Story Role

Redshore is a **nexus of blockade, smuggling, and espionage**, and that is its real value at the table and on the page:

- It is the **valve on Monmurg's lifeline** — everything bound for the city passes through here, legally into the Earl's inspection or illegally past his patrols.
- It is where **Jamis's reconnaissance runs**: Gouge, Merrick, and Tyrus take Owen's smuggling run as cover for spying on Redshore for the crown, Owen never to know (Ch. 46–47).
- The **Earl is himself a Gradsul intelligencer**, fishing for word of Jamis and Jeon's intentions — and he has already made contact with Monmurg's side through Jude, an opening Jamis has noted and may reopen by sending Jude back as an envoy (Book 2 Ch. 3). Redshore is thus a place where **both spymasters are reaching across the same water.**
- The **Dragon Isles sit close by** (Book 2 Ch. 3), which is why the northern runs out of Redshore carry the risk of Cain Toli and his dragons — and why Owen's reef-hidden cove, the Molt, lies within a smuggler's reach of these lanes (`worldbuilding/The_Molt.md`).

## Canon Notes & Continuity

- **Ship count.** "Forty-odd merchantmen" (Ch. 1; Earl file) is the figure of record; Owen's "fifty" (Ch. 44) is a round number in a bar-tale.
- **The Earl's title.** Canonical: **Earl of Redshore**, of the **Lorchester** line (`Guide_to_Salinmoor.md` canon note). "Baron"/"lord"/"that baron from Redshore" appear loosely in Book 2 dialogue; keep **Earl** in narration. He is unnamed beyond his title in every scene so far.
- **Warship count.** The Earl commands **two men-of-war** as the blockade flotilla (Ch. 1; Earl file); Owen counts **~three warships at Redshore** (Ch. 44). Treat as compatible (Owen's rounding, or a third hull), not a contradiction — but pick a firm number before a scene turns on it.
- **Spelling.** **Lorchester** (the gazetteer has a stray "Lorchersters" typo at one point); **Secundforth** (with a *d*); **Baywin** (with an *a*) for the Plar of Salinmoor. **Redshore**, one word.
- **Cross-references.** `worldbuilding/Guide_to_Salinmoor.md` (parent gazetteer), `characters/Earl_of_Redshore.md`, `characters/Owen_Black.md`, `worldbuilding/The_Molt.md`, `worldbuilding/The_Dragon_Isles.md`.
