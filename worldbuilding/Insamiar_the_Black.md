# Insamiar, the Black

A reference document, not an in-world artifact — this is the continuity ledger for **Insamiar, the Black**: the ancient black dragon of the Hool Marshes, the faith built around it (the primitive kobold religion and the human **Followers of Insamiar**), its thrall-kobolds and their **All-Watcher** priesthood, and the addictive drug-rite at the heart of it all. Compiled from author-supplied canon and cross-checked against `The_Sea_Principalities.md` (the gazetteer — "Swampton," "Riding the Dragon"), `The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`, `magic_system.md`, and `The_Dragon_Isles.md`.

**Status on the page.** As of the current manuscript, none of this has been dramatized in a scene — the cult exists so far only as gazetteer background and as the counter-power behind the Orb of the Dragon Turtle. This file is a resource for future chapters, not a record of shown events. Where the setting's register applies (polytheistic world — "Gods," not "God"; magic as discipline-with-a-mystery), it is noted below.

## Insamiar the Black (the dragon)

Insamiar is an **ancient black dragon** bound to the Hool Marshes — a swamp-dwelling breed, and "the Black" both names its color and answers to the black-dragon Orb of Dragonkind (`The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`). It is old enough that Ujor Udias built the Orb of the Dragon Turtle against it nearly six hundred years ago, which places its menace in the marsh back at least that far. **Insamiar is the true name** — the same name the cult venerates it under (not a cult corruption of some other name).

**A deliberate theological ambiguity (keep it a mystery).** Two accounts of what Insamiar *is* coexist in-world, and both should stay live rather than being collapsed:

- **A real, individual dragon** — an actual ancient black dragon of the marsh, the being Ujor's orb was made to counter and the thing cult members claim to "ride" in their visions.
- **A degenerate aspect of Tiamat** — outside scholars (in Raloro's naturalist tradition, below) read the whole religion as a **perversion of Tiamat-worship**, common among kobolds, narrowed down to her **black-dragon head** alone.

The setting can hold both: a real ancient dragon that its worshippers identify with — or have elevated into — Tiamat's black aspect. Whether Insamiar is literally that divine head, a distinct dragon the cult conflates with it, or a real dragon that has made itself a god to the marsh, is an open question worth preserving (see Open Threads). Tiamat is established elsewhere as a genuine dragon deity, beyond the reach of the Orbs of Dragonkind (`The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`, mechanics appendix).

**Present state — open.** Dead, dormant, or still lurking in the deep marsh is not fixed. The living, active cult and the ongoing drug-rite argue it is at least a present spiritual power; whether a physical dragon still lairs in the Hool is a card left in the deck.

## The Kobold Religion — Origin

- The **kobold worship of Insamiar** is the root; the human cult is downstream of it. It was first recorded by the **arch-druid and naturalist Raloro** in **CY 100**, and has existed since — almost certainly older than that first notice.
- Scholars hold it to be a **perversion of the worship of Tiamat** (widespread among kobolds), fixated on her **black-dragon head** to the exclusion of the rest.
- Its adherents among the marsh kobolds are the **Black Kobolds — Insamiar's thralls** (the same kobold tribes the gazetteer places in the Hool Marsh around Westkeep, who brew the drug and run the fertility rites; `The_Sea_Principalities.md`, "Swampton"). *Naming note:* "Black Kobolds" is the name of record for the Insamiar-worshipping marsh kobolds established earlier in this project; the new lore calls them simply the marsh kobolds. Treated here as one and the same.

## The All-Watchers (kobold elders and priests)

- An **All-Watcher** is the **eldest kobold** of a family — its gerontocratic ruler, and **often a powerful shaman**; a clan is governed by its families' All-Watchers sitting as a **council** (`Guide_to_the_Hool_Marshes.md`). Leadership and priesthood are fused in the eldest: they are not a separate caste but the same elders who **translate Insamiar's word** to the faithful and **lead every one of the cult's rituals** — including the human Followers' participation.
- This inverts the usual order and is what makes the whole faith an anomaly: in the rare joint human–humanoid religions known elsewhere, the *humans* dominate the rites and doctrine. Here the **kobolds lead and the humans follow** — a point worth keeping sharp in any scene, because it is genuinely unheard-of and unsettling to outsiders.

## The Human Cult — the Followers of Insamiar

- The **Followers of Insamiar** are the cult's **human** adherents — a small, secret body who take part in the *kobolds'* rituals rather than running their own. Reptile-based religions are many in the Hool Marshes, but this one is peculiar precisely for its human–kobold partnership under kobold leadership.
- **Discovery and suppression.** The human cult was discovered **~100 years ago, in CY 470, by the Plar of Westkeep**, who — finding its dealings with the "primitive" kobolds abhorrent — **outlawed it and imprisoned its leaders** to choke off new membership. Since then **every Plar of Westkeep has led an annual military expedition into the marsh to eradicate the kobold tribes** of the area. (This is the standing enmity behind the gazetteer's line that the lord of Westkeep "will execute anyone found to be a member"; `The_Sea_Principalities.md`. It also gives the militant character of Westkeep a recurring seasonal campaign — a hook for scenes.)
- **Why it survives the suppression.** The **Blessing of Insamiar** — the ritual potion — is **powerfully addictive**, which is the standing in-world explanation for why the cult keeps its numbers despite a century of Plars hunting it (`The_Sea_Principalities.md`).

## Areas of Influence

- The **kobold religion is marsh-wide**: wherever the marsh kobolds settle, **effigies of Insamiar's head** appear.
- The **human Followers cluster in and around Westkeep and the marshlands to the northeast**, always near large kobold populations — necessarily, since the rites are kobold-run. The kobolds **rarely permit cult members to live among them.** (Consistent with the gazetteer placing the Followers at Swampton, the Flan shanty settlement by Westkeep.)

## Symbols & Rituals

- **The one symbol** of the faith is the **head and neck of a black dragon.** The Followers' renderings are more sophisticated and varied than the kobolds' crude effigies — **ornate jewelry and tapestries of Insamiar** are common, and are **prized by the kobolds**, who covet such refined images. **Tattoos of Insamiar** are common among Followers, placed where they can be easily concealed (a practical necessity under Westkeep's law).
- **The monthly egg-rite (the core ritual):**
  1. Each month the **All-Watchers gather all the new eggs** from a clan's hatchery into a **porous basket of reeds and grass.**
  2. Before the whole clan and any attending Followers, they **pour a thick black liquid — the Blessing of Insamiar — over the eggs.** The excess drains through the basket and is **caught in stone urns.**
  3. The All-Watchers then move among the attendees, letting each **sip from the bowl in exchange for a "gift."** A **more valuable gift buys a larger sip.** The traditional gift is a **small polished stone**; human Followers usually offer **representations of Insamiar in polished stone or silver.**
- **The effect.** The kobolds are said to believe the Blessing **strengthens their offspring.** Those who drink it fall into a **stupor lasting hours**, and the faithful report **visions of Insamiar** within it — the experience the Followers call **"Riding the Dragon."** (This is the origin of the Hold-wide saying of the same name, meaning an act both highly dangerous and highly illegal; `The_Sea_Principalities.md`.) *Continuity flag:* the gazetteer's "Riding the Dragon" entry says the stupor "can last for **days**," while this fuller lore says **hours** — reconcile to one on a later pass (this file uses hours).
- **Addiction and escalation.** The Blessing is addictive, and **Followers give ever more valuable gifts as their tolerance grows** — a slow bleed of wealth (and of increasingly fine Insamiar-images) from the human faithful into kobold hands. **How the All-Watchers brew the potion is unknown** — keep it a mystery, in the setting's alchemy-with-a-hole register.

**Terminology (canon, reconciled).** The **Blessing of Insamiar** is the *potion* — the black liquid poured over the eggs and sipped from the urns. The **"gift"** is the *offering* (a polished stone or silver image) given in exchange for a sip. Earlier files/gazetteer use "Gift of Insamiar" loosely for the potion; the precise usage is Blessing = potion, gift = offering. (`The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md` has been updated to match; the in-world gazetteer's looser phrasing can stand as vernacular or be tightened on a later pass.)

## The War of Faiths in the Marsh (how this connects to the turtle dragons)

The Hool Marshes hold **many reptile religions**, and the deep contest running under them is between two poles:

- **The turtle dragons — the lizard men's living, natural god.** Per established canon (`The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`), the **physical presence** of the great turtle dragons gives the marsh's **lizard men** a real, benign deity of their own to worship, and anchors them against Insamiar. This was the whole point of Ujor Udias's Orb of the Dragon Turtle: to *keep the turtles present* so the marsh's faith held. **This squares with the lizardfolk's nature as Fryhone records it** (`Guide_to_the_Hool_Marshes.md`): their racial god is **Semuanya**, but lizardfolk carry a standing **dragon-affinity — a tribe entering a dragon's territory offers it tribute.** The turtle dragons are the present, physical dragon-power that affinity fastens onto; Semuanya remains the overarching racial deity, the turtle dragons the local dragon-god of tribute and reverence.
- **Insamiar — the dark pull.** Remove the turtles, and that same dragon-hunger has nowhere benign to go: the lizard men are drawn by the **Black Kobolds** toward Insamiar's worship, the marsh's dominant dark faith.

**Keep the two peoples distinct.** The **Followers of Insamiar are human**; the **lizard men are a separate reptile people** with their own turtle-dragon faith. The lizard men are not the human cult — their peril is the *erosion of their turtle-god worship* and a slide toward Insamiar's sway, not enrolment in the human Followers. **Present danger:** with **Cain Toli using the Orb of the Dragon Turtle for war at sea** instead of keeping the turtles present in the marsh, the lizard men are left spiritually undefended — Insamiar's reach into the Hool grows unopposed while the orb serves Toli ambition (`The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`; `The_Dragon_Isles.md`).

## Named Figures & Dates (quick reference)

- **Insamiar, the Black** — ancient black dragon / dark god of the Hool Marshes.
- **The All-Watchers** — a kobold family's eldest (its gerontocratic ruler, often a shaman); as a council they lead the clan, and they translate Insamiar's word and lead the rites.
- **The Black Kobolds** — Insamiar's thrall-tribes of the marsh.
- **Raloro** — arch-druid and naturalist; first recorded the kobold religion in **CY 100**.
- **The Plar of Westkeep** — discovered and outlawed the human cult in **CY 470**; his line has mounted annual eradication expeditions into the marsh ever since.
- **Ujor Udias** — transmuter; made the Orb of the Dragon Turtle to hold the marsh against Insamiar (`The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`, `magic_system.md`).

## Author ruling — "the Gift of Insamiar" is a poison (dragon-origin is a rumor)

- **Canon (author ruling):** The **Gift of Insamiar** is a **poison**, **rumored** to come from the dragon **Insamiar the Black**. The dragon-origin is an **in-world rumor, not confirmed fact** — render it as belief/hearsay, never as authoritative narration.
- This is the field usage, e.g. Owen's **"special draught"** in `Owen_and_Torus_plan_6th_Wealsun` (the poison he caught in the eye when it was meant for Folsom).
- **Reconcile with the terminology above (flag for author):** the ledger reconciles **Blessing = the sipped potion** and **gift = the offering (polished stone or silver image)**. This ruling uses **"Gift of Insamiar" = the poison itself**. Open question: is this poison the same black liquid as the "Blessing" (with "gift" doing double duty), or a distinct substance? Recorded as given; left for the author to settle.
