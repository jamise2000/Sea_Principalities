# Guide to the Hool Marshes

*By Archibald Fryhone, in Common Year 554.*

An in-world tome — a naturalist's compendium of the Hool Marshes, reproduced here as a primary source. Author's-bible **Canon Notes & Continuity** follow the text at the foot of the file. (Light editorial cleanup only: obvious typos corrected, and **"sahaugin"** used for the project's canonical spelling in place of the manuscript's "sahuagin." Content unchanged.)

## Forward

After the initial rush of the Hool River from the high lake and freshets in the Hellfurnaces, it begins to meander across the plains, and most of its length is surrounded by quaking mires and bottomless pools of the Hool Marshes. The Marshes form a natural boundary between the lands of the Yeomanry and the holdings of the Sea Princes to the south. Although the Yeomanry and Keoland have both attempted to pacify the region in the past, the marshes remain wild and untamed. There are many ruins and abandoned keeps scattered throughout the marshes bearing testament to the efforts of these nations, however.

These marshes are home to renegade humans, humanoids, and many types of monsters. The humans living in the marshes are the worst sort; outcasts and criminals from all three of the bordering nations, or worse, residents of the marsh that have never lived among society. Evil cults exist in the marsh, hiding in the shadows. Disturbing tales say that organizations dedicated to demon princes such as Orcus and Dagon have developed there. There are also signs of humans worshiping foul demi-gods banished from the civilized world, some stemming from humanoid superstitions or even insane followers of Acerak the Lich.

Ogre and orc tribes fleeing from the elf hunting parties in the Dreadwood will often hide in the marsh, but are only temporary residents. Most of the permanent humanoid tribes inhabiting the marsh are reptilians or amphibian variants; kobolds, lizardfolk, yuan-ti and even some sahaugin along the coast. These races are well adapted to the conditions of the marsh where others are not; hot, wet weather, rotting vegetation, swarms of insects and rampant disease.

Beasts and monsters also thrive in the marshes; crocodiles, marsh lizards and evil wyverns are found throughout. Near the slow-moving rivers of the Hool and Javan Rivers sightings of giant pike and schools of flesh eating fish are common. In the fetid pools leeches and giant toads wait for prey.

However, despite all the dangers the marshes are a vibrant region containing an extremely varied array of flora and fauna. Druids and sages make pilgrimages to the marshes to study and observe, and the spices, herbs and plants found therein are coveted by alchemists and healers alike.

Near the coastline of the marsh, the plant species diversity is relatively low, since the flora must be tolerant of salt water, in which plants are subjected to complete or partial submersion. The most common coastal marsh plants are glassworts and cordgrass. Their shoots lift the main flow of the tide above the mud surface while their roots spread stabilize the sticky mud. Plants such as sea lavenders, plantains and varied sedges and rushes grow in the coastal regions as well. The largest plants are the mangroves; small bushy trees that thrive in coastal salt or brackish waters. Farther inland the cordgrass and mangroves give way to larger trees, such as the marsh oak and sandpaper fig, taller reeds and grasses.

## Notable Fauna

The Hool Marshes are a tropical wetland, and provide a perfect habitat for amphibious and reptilian forms of life. Consequently, most of the creatures of note that are attracted to the marshes are of this form.

While there are many strange creatures that live in the marsh, this compendium will only attempt to describe the most dangerous and unexpected someone might encounter.

### Giant Crocodiles

The crocodiles of the Hool Marshes have few natural enemies, and can grow to enormous sizes. Some specimens twenty-five feet long have been observed. These monsters dominate the slowly running rivers and channels of the Hool Marshes, feeding on the abundant schools of fish and smaller crocodiles.

Besides their bulk they are stealthy hunters and have a hide that provides natural cover in their habitat. This and their ability to stay motionless underwater for more than half an hour make them particularly difficult to detect, until it is too late.

### Marsh Hydra

The Hydras of the Hool Marshes are without doubt the lords of the region. They have no rivals, except each other, and will even attack and devour the largest of the giant crocodiles if they are hungry enough, which they always are. These reptilian horrors have crocodilian bodies and multiple heads on long serpentine necks. Species in other regions average five heads, but the marsh hydras average at least seven and some with as many as ten have been observed.

Hydras are natural swimmers, dwelling in the rivers, along the lakeshores, in the oceans shallows and in the wetland bogs. The marsh hydras do not have lairs; these rapacious and gluttonous monsters will quickly clear a territory of food, and then be forced to move on to seek its next meal.

A marsh hydra's hunger is so great that if it can't feed, it might turn against itself, its heads attacking each other as the creature eats itself alive. This trait fortunately keeps their numbers small, even in a region as verdant as the Hool Marshes.

### Marsh Wyverns

Most wyverns in the Flanaess inhabit wooded hills or mountains, seeking out deep caverns as their preferred lair. Strangely, there is a wyvern flock located near the middle of the Hool Marshes that prefers the bogs and fetid pools of the great marsh to their natural highland habitat. The reason they prefer the marsh to the densely wooded hills east of the Dreadwood is a mystery, but they have been sighted off the northern coast of the Principalities hunting the dolphins and fish that play and spawn there.

It has been suggested that this flock have developed a taste for sea creatures, and would rather be closer to the sea than the eastern Dreadwood allows.

Whatever the reason for their choice of habitat, these creatures carefully create nests made from trees and rocks, which they apparently uproot and carry from as far away as the Dreadwoods and the Little Hills. This is atypical of wyvern behavior; most wyvern species are satisfied with scattering the bones of their victims on the floors of their caverns.

As strange as their nesting behavior is, however, they are as territorial and ill-tempered as their cousins, and will attack large parties of soldiers, or even ships, that come near their domain.

### Marsh Lizards

The marsh lizards are giant amphibious lizards. They are strong swimmers, and can submerge beneath the waters for up to a quarter of an hour without surfacing. On dry or wet grounds, they are equally mobile, and can sprint at least as fast as a man can run.

The marsh lizards are not particularly aggressive, but will attack if hungry or if they are harassed. Lizardfolk keep them as draft animals and pets.

## Notable Flora

Besides the mundane cordgrass and marsh oaks, several species of herbaceous plants grow throughout the Hool Marsh. Many of these are useful for cooking, healing and even in the creation of potions. Only the most notable are identified here.

### Marsh potato

The marsh potato is a hardy, tuberous plant that will tolerate fluctuating levels of sea water. The stalk of the marsh potato reaches three feet in height and it can be identified by its beautiful, medium green, arrow-shaped leaves. White flowers with yellow centers bloom in mid-summer. The marsh potato grows best in rich, damp lowland soils or in water up to two feet in depth. Waterfowl, muskrats, and even deer favor the tender shoots, tubers, and seeds. It is also used as a basic food source for much of the human and humanoid populations of the marsh.

### Marsh white oak

The marsh white oak is frequently found in heavy mucky soils, usually in the bottomlands, stream margins and the deep marshes. The tree can grow to height of eighty feet, and has a white, rough bark. The wood is close-grained, strong, hard, tough and heavy. Trees do not self-prune and dead branches remain on the trees for many years. This does mean that the wood is usually quite knotty. The wood can be used for construction, furniture, as well as for fencing and fuel.

The seed of the white oak are edible raw or cooked. They have a rather sweet flavor. The seed is quite large, about one inch long and half an inch wide, and unlike most other oaks, is attached to the tree by a long stem. It matures in its first year. The seed can be dried, ground into a powder and used as a thickening in stews or mixed with cereals for making bread. The seed from some trees can contain bitter tannins; these can be leached out by thoroughly washing the seed in running water though many minerals will also be lost. Either the whole seed can be used or the seed can be dried and ground into a powder. It can take several days or even weeks to properly leach whole seeds. One method is to wrap them in a cloth bag and place them in a stream. Leaching the powder is quicker. The traditional method of preparing the seed was to bury it in boggy ground overwinter. The germinating seed was dug up in the spring when it would have lost most of its astringency.

A mulch of the leaves repels slugs, grubs and insects, though fresh leaves should not be used as these can inhibit plant growth.

Oak galls are excrescences that are sometimes produced in great numbers on the tree and are caused by the activity of the larvae of different insects. The insects live inside these galls, obtaining their nutrient therein. When the insect pupates and leaves, the gall can be used as a rich source of tannin that can also be used as a dyestuff. Any galls produced on the tree are strongly astringent and can be used in the treatment of hemorrhages, diarrhea, dysentery and other diseases common to the marsh.

The bark can be boiled with hemlock and soft maple bark, and the liquid used to remove rust. This mixture is also believed to prevent rust.

## The Fey and Undead of the Northern Marshes

Evil creatures roam the Northern marsh, including lycanthropes, wraiths and other undead. Wererats, masquerading as villagers or farmers, are numerous along the edge of the marsh, while wereboars often stalk the marsh as hunters or trappers. Vampires' spawn, though uncommon, have also been encountered. The dark places in the Hool Marshes are many, and provide hiding places for the Fey and Undead.

Many historians think these are children of the Dread March, created by Count Olan and the Lich, Acerak. The Dread March existed many centuries ago and encompassed the lands laying between the northern edges of the Hool Marshes, the southern boundaries of the Dreadwood, the Javan River and the Azure Sea. The lands were ruled by a vampire lord, Count Olan, who was the thrall of the powerful lich Acerak. Acerak dwelled in the Dreadwood, and to this day the echoes of his influence remain in the Dreadwood and the northern tip of the Hool Marsh.

### Dread March Will-o-Wisp

The will-o-wisps of the marsh are almost certainly creations of the Battle of One-hundred banners; a legacy left from the deaths of the evil servants of Olan as the swords of the forces of Keoland purged the Dread March of Acerak's evil.

Will-o-wisps are the souls of evil beings that perished in anguish, misery and defeat; forsaken by their evil masters. They thrive in the marshy bogs of the northern marsh, which seems to draw the souls of Acerak's minions as soldiers are drawn to a muster ground.

These wisps, seen as balls of pale blue light, will lure unwary creatures into quicksand pits and the bottomless bogs of the marsh, feeding on the suffering of their prey and reveling in their death. In all cases, it is best to avoid them.

### Lycanthropes

One of the most ancient and feared of all curses, lycanthropy can transform the most civilized human into a ravening beast. To some who dwell in the northern marshes, lycanthropy is often considered a badge of honor or a rite of passage. Among these afflicted, there is no civilization, only the beast.

In its natural humanoid form, a creature cursed by lycanthropy appears as its normal self. Over time, however, many lycanthropes acquire features suggestive of their animal form. In animal form, a lycanthrope resembles a powerful version of a normal animal. However, on close inspection its eyes show a faint spark of unnatural intelligence.

Normally, evil lycanthropes hide among normal folk, emerging in animal form at night to spread terror and bloodshed, especially under a full moon. The lycanthropes of the marsh are much bolder, forming small enclaves in and around villages and towns and spreading their disease actively. Many are members of the Sons of Olan, an evil cult, and see their affliction as a gift to be bestowed on others.

## Humanoid Tribes

The two main groups of humanoids inhabiting the Hool Marshes are the black marsh kobolds and the various tribal clans of lizardfolk. Although there have been sightings of yuan-ti and sahaugin throughout the years, the infrequency of contact and the reputations of the sources make any mention of these races anecdotal and mundane.

### The kobold clans of the Hool Marshes

The Hool Marshes are home to a large population of a kobold species; the black marsh kobolds. The Hool Marsh kobolds are significant for their numbers, their religion and their unique physical characteristics.

#### Ecology

It is thought among the learned that kobolds are distantly related to dragons, and they are often found serving dragons as minions. Kobolds speak a version of the draconic tongue with a yipping accent, which lends further evidence to the theory of their draconic origins. However, it is unclear if kobolds learned the Draconic language from dragons or if it sprang from a common linguistic root. Whatever their origins, kobolds are clearly reptilian and have been described as having features that are both reptilian and doglike. They are scavengers and omnivores with no scruples about what or whom they eat. They can digest bark, dirt, leather, eggshells, or their own younger siblings if they are desperate enough.

Kobolds are extremely fecund egg-layers, having the highest birth rate, and death rate, of all humanoid species. A female kobold will lay a clutch of hard-shelled eggs two weeks after fertilization. The eggs must be incubated for an additional sixty days before hatching. Kobolds reach maturity by the age of eight or nine and are considered "great wyrms" by the age of 121. They live up to 135 years. While they do bond with one another, they have no concept of monogamy.

Most kobolds have an extreme hatred for gnomes, with whom they compete for the same areas and mining rights, as well as pixies, brownies, and sprites. However, the kobold clans of the Hool Marshes are not generally great miners, probably due to the watery lands they inhabit, and seem to have little reason to compete with gnomes for mines or territory. They have warred with the gnomes and dwarves of the Little Hills but were never interested in keeping conquered territory, always returning to the marsh.

While typical kobold tribes are often at war with goblins, no known goblin tribes exist in or around the Hool Marshes. Because kobold-goblin conflicts usually help keep the populations of both races down to a manageable level, the lack of goblin tribes in the area means that this natural check on the Hool Marsh kobold population does not exist. As a result, the Hool Marshes are infested with marsh kobolds, a state that is expected to only get worse with time.

#### Environment

Most kobolds prefer dark, damp underground lairs and overgrown forests, but the Hool Marsh kobold tribes have adapted to the marshes landscape, and generally make their dwellings in the hollow trunks of rotting trees and logs if nothing else is available. While the Hool Marsh kobolds still burrow, their tunnel complexes are much smaller than their cousins, and are sometimes nothing more than connected trenches and pits. These 'villages' usually occupy the scarce dry and stable land available in the marsh but some dwellings built into the branches of trees have also been found in areas where dry land is scarce. These limitations create smaller but more widely spread kobold tribes; marsh kobolds quickly fill their habitats thanks to their rapid rate of reproduction. Excess marsh kobold populations will quickly migrate to new areas as the living space becomes too cramped.

While most of marsh kobolds live in dispersed groups, large densities have been observed when the terrain permits. This often occurs in or around the numerous ruins found in the Hool Marshes; outposts from the old Suel Empire or keeps erected by Keoland at the height of its power, but since abandoned. The kobolds adapt these ruins to their needs quickly, and will use natural material like mud, trees and rocks to create burrows suitable for mating and egg-laying.

The largest observed kobold population is located sixty miles Northwest of Westkeep, where the Davish River and Hool River join to drain to the Azure Sea. This is the location of an ancient Suel military post abandoned more than one thousand years ago. It is estimated that as many as five-thousand kobolds inhabit the ruins, rivaling the population of Westkeep itself.

While normal kobolds will use boars or giant weasels as guards, marsh kobolds tend to keep marsh lizards and crocodiles as guards instead. It is assumed this practice was learned from the lizardfolk inhabiting the marshes, whom the kobolds have dealings with.

#### Physical characteristics

Typical kobolds vary in height between three and four feet, with scaly, hairless hides, reptilian heads, and tails, and weigh about thirty to thirty-five pounds. Males are about three inches taller and ten pounds heavier than most females. The variant found in the Hool Marshes are typically taller and heavier; most are closer to four feet in height and can weigh nearly forty-five pounds. While most kobold hides are typically a rust-brown or reddish black color with ivory-colored horns, the kobolds that live in the Hool Marshes are significantly darker, often having hides that are black in color with brownish or greenish hues. Their heads can be described as a cross between a dog and a crocodile; short snouts with large mouths and several rows of short, sharp teeth. Their horns are also longer than normal and are also significantly darker, sometimes even black. Like typical kobolds, marsh kobolds' eyes glow red, and they can see accurately even in lightless conditions up to sixty feet away. Marsh kobolds are described as smelling like a damp dog that has gone swimming in stagnant, fetid water. They rarely wear clothing, but they are fond of jewelry and other ornamentation.

Kobolds shed their skin about once a season, in patches, over the course of a week. They keep themselves well-groomed during this period, regularly polishing their horns, claws, and teeth.

#### Society

Marsh kobolds have specialized laborers, yet most kobolds are hunters. The most coveted careers are trap maker, shaman, caretaker, and warrior. Marsh kobolds typically apprentice in their chosen profession at the age of three. A kobold's job may change over the years based on the needs of the family. Kobold families are gerontocracies, ruled by their eldest member, who is known as the All-Watcher. Often this leader is a powerful shaman. Kobold laws may change along with their leaders. Kobolds prefer exile to execution, and in some disputes, kobold families will split to spread their kind over a larger region. Kobold family members often lay eggs in a common nest, with specialized foster parents to watch over the eggs and wyrmlings.

Kobold society is tribal with war bands based on clans. As many as ten families can be part of a normal sized clan, usually with up to forty members in a family. Each clan generally controls a ten-mile radius, but larger concentrations are not unheard of. These clans are usually led by the member families' elder kobolds, the All-Watchers, acting as a council.

The Hool Marsh kobolds are known for their close-knit cooperation. Unlike many kobold species, Hool Marsh kobolds share information among the different clans of the marsh and inter-clan cooperation is great. General plans and goals are common knowledge, and detailed plans are shared with other clans if possible. This allows the clans to work fruitfully for the good of all the Hool Marsh kobold tribes. While all kobolds have a natural hatred of other non-draconic creatures because of mistreatment of their race, marsh kobolds hate other kobold species as well, and will attack other kobolds on sight.

#### Religion

The main deity of the entire kobold race is Kurtulmak, the god of war and mining. The other main god worshipped by kobolds is Gaknulak, the god of protection, stealth, trickery, and traps. A lesser-known kobold demigod is Dakarnok.

The Hool Marsh kobolds recognize none of these gods and instead worship a dragon-god they call "Insamiar, The Black". Insamiar is portrayed as a giant black dragon neck and head, and is believed to be a primitive representation of Tiamat's black head.

While it is not uncommon for individual kobolds or kobold tribes to worship deities of the draconic pantheon (Tiamat and Io often play a significant role in their creation myths), what is strange is that several human cults exist in the Hool Marshes that also recognize Insamiar as a god. It is rumored that the marsh kobolds and the cultists even engage in joint religious rituals.

The effigies of Insamiar's great head are used by the clans to mark kobold territory.

#### Relationships with Hool Marsh lizardfolk

There is a significant population of lizardfolk that inhabit the Hool Marshes with the kobold clans, and competition for food and territory between the kobolds and lizardfolk would logically drive some level of conflict. However, there seems to be an understanding, if not some level of cooperation, between the two races. The kobolds avoid lizardman enclaves, which are more often found in the wetter parts of the marshes or along the coast, and seem to respect the territorial instincts of their larger cousins.

Whether this respect is caused by fear or a sense of kinship is unknown, but conflict between the two races is almost non-existent. Both groups seem to go out of their way to co-exist.

### The lizardfolk of the Hool Marshes

While not as prolific as kobolds, the lizardfolk that inhabit the marshes are quite numerous and can be found throughout the marsh. Lizardfolk are usually primitive reptilian humanoids that use tools and weapons of stone and wood, but many tribes of the Hool Marshes have displayed greater sophistication and will trade for metal items, even if they cannot forge their own.

#### Ecology

In most ways the lizardfolk of the Hool Marshes are like lizardfolk found elsewhere. Like kobolds, lizardfolk speak Draconic, and as with kobolds, this fact has been used to argue that the lizardfolk and dragons are distantly related. Whether the lizardfolk were taught how to speak the language of dragons or if the language has a common linguistic root is unknown, however. What is known is lizardfolk have an affinity to dragons, and if a tribe wanders into the territory of a dragon it will offer it tribute.

Lizardfolk are omnivorous, but they have a taste for humanoid flesh. The marsh lizardfolk are rumored to favor orc flesh, but it is said that they have feasted on humans in the past. Prisoners are often taken back to their villages to become the centerpieces of great feasts and rites involving dancing, storytelling, and ritualistic combat.

Save for the marsh kobolds, the lizardfolk of the marsh rarely deal or trade with other races. Fiercely territorial, they use well-hidden scouts to guard the perimeter of their domain. When they detect unwelcome visitors, a tribe will send a hunting band to harass or drive the trespassers off, often forcing them into the lair of a local predator.

Although they are not skilled artisans, lizardfolk craft tools and ornamental jewelry out of the bones of their kills, and use the hides and shells of dead monsters to create shields.

#### Environment

The Hool Marshes provide the perfect environment for the lizardfolk, and their primitive villages flourish throughout the marsh. The near-tropical wetlands that stretch for miles provide ample hunting grounds for the lizardfolk population, and competition for hunting grounds between the tribes has not been observed.

They tend to avoid the territories of the marsh kobolds, which tend to live farther from the coast in the bogs and sparse dry areas. It is thought that this is more out of territorial respect than preference, however.

#### Physical characteristics

The marsh lizardfolk have skin covered in scales that vary in color from dark green through shades of brown and gray. Taller than humans and powerfully built, lizardfolk are often between 6 and 7 feet tall and weighed between 200 and 250 pounds when fully grown. Lizardfolk have muscular tails that grow to three or four feet in length, and these are used for balance. They have sharp claws and teeth.

Although non-reptilians struggle to tell the difference between males and females, lizardfolk can easily distinguish opposite sex.

#### Society

Lizardfolk have no notion of morality, and they find the concepts of good and evil alien. They are consummate pragmatists and survivors, and will usually make great personal sacrifices for the good of their tribe.

Lizardfolk respect and fear magic with a religious awe and they are usually led by the magic-using shamans. Lizardfolk shamans coordinate the daily functions of the tribe and oversee the rites and ceremonies performed to honor Semuanya, the lizardfolk god.

Besides the station of shaman, there is no real specialization seen in lizardfolk society, or even between the lizardfolk sexes. Most of the members of the tribe hunt, gather, build dwellings and create tools and artifacts like all others.

The average tribe is usually comprised of roughly 50 males, 50 females and 50 hatchlings. There are usually two to four shaman leaders of either sex in a tribe. Groups larger than this will usually separate, with one group moving on to claim a new territory.

Lizardfolk rarely stray beyond their claimed hunting grounds, and respect the territories of other tribes. Any creature that enters their territory is fair game, even other lizardfolk.

Lizardfolk will often tame Marsh Lizards to use as pack animals or guards.

#### Religion

Like most lizardfolk, the marsh lizardfolk worship Semuanya. The god Semuanya focuses solely on the survival and propagation of the lizardfolk species. The religion is maintained by the shamans who bestow Semuanya's blessings on the lizardfolk people and direct her will throughout the lizardfolk society.

Lizardfolk have no shrines or temples but are very proud of their religious traditions and their deity.

#### Relationship with black marsh kobolds

The lizardfolk seem to have an agreement with the marsh kobolds, which instinctively respect the territorial claims of their larger cousins. Conflict between the two races is almost non-existent, and both seem to go out of their way to co-exist.

## Cults of the Marsh

The vast marshes and wetlands of the region give cover to strange religions and dark cults, often derived from ancestor worship, common among the deep marsh dwellers who have never known civilization.

Scholars know of twenty-seven distinct religions based in the Hool Marshes; only the two most interesting will be discussed here.

### Followers of Insamiar

There are many reptile-based religions in the Hool Marshes, but the Followers of Insamiar are peculiar because the members, which are all human, participate in rituals with kobolds. The kobold All-Watchers act as the translators for Insamiar's word and they lead all the cult's rituals. Such a relationship is unheard of; joint human-humanoid religions are very rare, and in all other known cases the humans dominate the religious rites and belief system.

#### Origin

The existence of the religion among the kobold tribes was first noted by the arch-druid and naturalist Raloro in CY 100, and has existed since that time. The kobold religion is thought to be a perversion of the worship of Tiamat (common among kobolds), focusing only on her black dragon head. The beliefs of the Followers of Insamiar are obviously based off the primitive religion of the marsh kobolds.

The human cult was discovered about 100 years ago by the Plar of Westkeep, in CY 470. Not long after making his discovery the Plar, finding the cult's interactions with the primitive kobolds abhorrent, outlawed it and began imprisoning its leaders to discourage new membership. Since then, all the Plars of Westkeep have led military expeditions into the marsh annually to eradicate the kobold tribes in the area.

Today the Followers of Insamiar are banned throughout the Hold of the Sea Princes but, despite efforts by the lords of that land, the religion still exists. It is thought that the "Gift of Insamiar" used in the cult's ritual is a powerfully addictive potion, which may explain why the cult has so many members despite the suppression by the lords of the Hold.

#### Areas of Influence

The kobold religion is spread throughout the marsh and anywhere the marsh kobolds have settled the effigies of Insamiar's head can be seen. The "Followers of Insamiar", as the cult's human adherents are known, are concentrated in and around Westkeep and the marsh lands to the Northeast. They will usually be found near large kobold populations. Rarely will the kobolds allow members of the cult to cohabitate with them.

#### Symbols and Rituals

Consistent with the kobold religion, the only symbol of Insamiar's followers is the head and neck of a black dragon. The portrayal of Insamiar by the Followers is like that of the kobolds but is usually more sophisticated and varied. Ornate jewelry and tapestries of Insamiar are common and highly sought after by the kobolds, who desire such sophisticated representations greatly. Tattoos of Insamiar are common among the Followers, but usually located in places on the body that can be easily concealed.

The main ritual practiced by the Followers of Insamiar occurs during a marsh kobold reproduction rite. The All-Watchers of a clan will collect all the new eggs laid in the hatchery every month and place them in a porous basket, made of reeds and grass. The All-Watchers, with the entire clan and any participating Followers in attendance, will then pour a thick black liquid, called "The Blessing of Insamiar", over the eggs. The excess liquid is then caught in several stone urns.

After the dousing is finished, the All-Watchers then walk among the attendees, allowing each to sip from its bowl for a "gift". The "gift" is traditionally a small polished stone, but the more valuable the gift the larger the sip. The humans attending the ritual usually give representations of Insamiar made of polished stone or silver.

It is rumored that the kobolds believe this "blessing" will strengthen their offspring, but those who consume it are said to enter a stupor that can last for hours. It is said the faithful will have visions of Insamiar during this stupor. Many of the Followers call this ritual "Riding the Dragon".

How the kobold All-Watchers produce this potion is unknown, but it is said to be addictive and Followers will give more and more valuable gifts as their tolerance to the Blessing of Insamiar grows.

### Sons of Olan

The Sons of Olan are a secret military and religious cult dedicated to resisting the rule of Keoland and its king.

They have existed since the destruction of the Dread March, which was crushed by Keoland and the Sons of Heironeous over six hundred years ago.

#### Origin

Olan was one of Acerak's captains, an undead vampire lord that ruled the Dread March for his master. In CY -150 the Kingdom of Keoland, with the aid of the Sons of Heironeous, challenged Olan and his followers inhabiting the Dread March. Olan was defeated in CY -149 at the Battle of One-hundred banners, and the remnants of his army fled to the northern marshes to escape the wrath of Keoland. It is thought that the descendants of Olan's defeated army founded the Sons of Olan after this, more than six hundred years ago.

#### Areas of Influence

The Sons of Olan are concentrated in the northern region of the marsh, north of the Hool River as it meanders its way to the sea. Camps, often called 'nests', of the cult's members are scattered throughout this part of the marsh. These camps move often to avoid detection. However, sometimes a camp will become permanent, especially in the ruins of an old keep or military post buried deep within the marsh.

Since the cult's founding, the northern region of the marsh has become a haven for vampires, lycanthropes and the undead, many of whom belong to or aid the Sons of Olan.

It is thought that the cult maintains a spy network between Saltmarsh and Burle. Many of its members live in the villages and towns near the two cities, and 'nests' of adherents have been found in both. The road along the marsh can be dangerous, especially at night when the Sons of Olan usually hunt.

#### Symbols and Rituals

The cult practices necromancy and engages in blood rituals and sacrifice honoring Olan's memory. Many members are known to be vampires, vampires' spawn and lycanthropes, and the cult engages in the "conversion" of unwilling worshipers by vampiric or lycanthropic infection.

Their main symbol is Count Olan's family crest; a black wyvern rising in front of a full moon.

## Canon Notes & Continuity (author's-bible — not part of the tome)

Reference notes for keeping this tome consistent with the rest of the project. The tome is an **in-world naturalist's compendium (CY 554)** — it reports what a learned outsider of that year believed, which is not always the author-level truth. Where the two differ, both are noted.

- **What this file is.** The first substantial treatment of the Hool Marshes as its own region (previously only the gazetteer's short subsection in `The_Sea_Principalities.md`). Stored as an in-world primary source, like `Guide_to_Salinmoor.md`. Light typo cleanup only; **"sahuagin" normalized to the project's "sahaugin"** throughout.

- **Insamiar — in-world belief vs. author-truth.** The tome states the scholarly, in-world view: the kobolds' Insamiar is a dragon-*god*, "a primitive representation of Tiamat's black head." The author-level truth established elsewhere (`Insamiar_the_Black.md`, `The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`) is that **Insamiar is a real ancient black dragon** of the marshes, against which Ujor Udias made the Orb of the Dragon Turtle ~600 years ago. Both coexist deliberately: a real dragon that its worshippers (and outside scholars) have theologized into Tiamat's black aspect. Do not let a scene collapse the mystery unless by design.

- **The All-Watchers — corrected.** The tome fixes an earlier characterization: an **All-Watcher is the eldest kobold** of a family, its gerontocratic ruler (**often a powerful shaman**); clans are led by a **council of All-Watchers**. They are leaders first, and in the Insamiar religion the same elders **translate Insamiar's word and lead the rites** — leadership and priesthood are fused in the eldest, not a separate priest-caste. (`Insamiar_the_Black.md` updated to match.)

- **Black marsh kobolds = the project's "Black Kobolds."** The tome's **black marsh kobolds** are the same as the **Black Kobolds, Insamiar's thralls** used elsewhere in this project (`Insamiar_the_Black.md`, `Guide_to_Salinmoor.md`), and the "black or swamp kobolds" of the Salinmoor guide. The tome supplies their full ecology, physical description (darker, taller ~4 ft, ~45 lb, black horns, red eyes), gerontocratic society, close inter-clan cooperation, and religion. The largest concentration — **~5,000 kobolds in a >1,000-year-old Suel ruin sixty miles NW of Westkeep, where the Davish and Hool Rivers meet** — is a notable set-piece location (and **Davish River** is a new named waterway to keep).

- **Lizardfolk, Semuanya, and the turtle dragons — reconciled.** The tome says the marsh lizardfolk worship **Semuanya** (their racial survival-god) and have a standing **dragon-affinity: a tribe entering a dragon's territory offers it tribute.** This *supports* rather than contradicts the established idea that the **turtle dragons are the lizard men's living, natural god** (`The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`): Semuanya is the overarching racial deity, while the turtle dragons are the **present, physical dragon-power** the lizardfolk's dragon-affinity draws them to venerate and tribute — the benign counter to Insamiar's pull. Absent the turtles, that same dragon-hunger is what Insamiar (and the Black Kobolds) exploit.

- **Sons of Olan — now fully anchored.** This is the major addition. The cult that Book 1 knew only as "a necromantic cult… the black sickness" (Ch. 3) is here given its origin: the **descendants of Count Olan's army** — Olan a vampire-lord captain of the **lich Acerak**, who ruled the **Dread March** until Keoland and the **Sons of Heironeous** destroyed it (Olan slain CY -149 at the **Battle of One-hundred banners**). They are necromantic, vampiric/lycanthropic, resist Keoland, nest in the northern marsh, and run a **spy network between Saltmarsh and Burle**; crest: **a black wyvern before a full moon.** Direct hooks into current canon:
  - **Ixid** — the necromancer who traded Cain Toli the orb's location — **is a Son of Olan** (`The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`, `Guide_to_Salinmoor.md`).
  - **Lord Phranck** was sent into Salinmoor as an Inquisitor of the Office of Forbidden Magic to hunt exactly this cult and its "black sickness" (Ch. 3; `magic_system.md`).
  - The Saltmarsh–Burle spy corridor overlaps the smuggling country Owen Black works (`characters/Owen_Black.md`, `Guide_to_Salinmoor.md`).
  - A dedicated `Sons_of_Olan.md` author's-bible is the natural next file if you want the cult developed on its own.

- **Marsh threats for future scenes.** Giant crocodiles (to 25 ft), **marsh hydras** (7–10 heads, the marsh's apex, self-devouring when starved), and a **marsh wyvern flock** that nests in the bogs and **hunts off the northern Principalities coast** and will attack ships. Note the wyverns range into the same northern waters as the Dragon Isles turtle dragons but are a **distinct** threat (and the Sons of Olan crest is a black wyvern — motif, not necessarily a link).

- **New named figures.** **Archibald Fryhone** (naturalist, author of this tome, CY 554); **Count Olan** (vampire-lord, founder-namesake); **Acerak** (the lich; the famous D&D lich, placed here in the Dreadwood). **Raloro** (arch-druid/naturalist, CY 100) recurs here consistently with `Insamiar_the_Black.md`.

- **Still to reconcile (offered, not done).** The **gazetteer** (`The_Sea_Principalities.md`) still says the Insamiar stupor "can last for **days**" and calls the potion the "**Gift** of Insamiar"; this tome (the fuller source) says **hours** and names the potion the **Blessing** (with "gift" = the offering). ## Canon Note — the two bearers of the name "Acerak"

Two distinct figures carry the name **Acerak** in this setting, and in-world sources conflate them. Keep them separate author-side:

1. **The original lich Acerak** — the famed lich of the Dreadwood who, with **Count Olan**, raised the **Dread March** many centuries ago (the backstory above). He **vanished hundreds of years before Ujor Udias lived.**
2. **Ujor Udias** — the transmuter who made the **Orb of the Dragon Turtle** ~600 years ago (`The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`). Grown into a lich across an unnaturally long life, he **took (inherited) the vanished Acerak's name.** When Toli sources or the Sons of Olan (e.g., the necromancer **Ixid**) credit the orb to "the Lich Acerak," they mean **Ujor Udias under that borrowed name** — not the Dread March's original Acerak.

So the marsh's ancient Acerak (Count Olan's master) and the orb-maker "Acerak" are two beings separated by centuries who happen to share the name. Author ruling; render the conflation as in-world imprecision, not as fact.
