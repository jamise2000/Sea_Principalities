# Helm Island

A reference document, not an in-world artifact — this is the continuity ledger for Helm Island: its geography, fortifications, signals, strategic role, and the state of it during the crisis at the center of *Journey of the Stone*. Compiled from what has already been established on the page (manuscript Ch. 15–25, `characters/Fairwind.md`, `magic_system.md`). Its purpose is to keep later chapters consistent with what's been shown, and to give a place to check a proposed beat against the island's established shape before drafting it.

A note on names: **"the Helm"** does double duty. It is shorthand for the whole island (**Helm Island**) and also the name of the **keep/citadel** at its crown. Prose uses "the Helm" and "the citadel" interchangeably for that fortress. The two friendly strongpoints below it must stay distinct in the prose (see *Fortifications*): the small **southern guard barracks** by the southern beacon, and the large midway **garrison**.

## What the Helm Is — Strategic Role

Helm Island is the fortified rock at the center of the **Strait of Monmurg**, and the hinge the whole Principality leans its weight against — "the very hinge of the Strait, the stone the whole Principality leaned its weight against" (Ch. 16). Monmurg sits on a finger of land that could be cut off from the mainland; the outer islands, the Helm chief among them, are the shield that keeps the city from being severed at the neck.

Its importance is more than positional. Per `characters/Fairwind.md`, the Helm is:

- **The traditional seat where the princes and commodores plan their naval campaigns.**
- **The chief storehouse of the anti-sahaugin powder** — the Hold's most closely guarded secret and its largest stockpile (see `magic_system.md`, "The anti-sahaugin powder").
- **A rotating command.** Command of the Helm rotates among Monmurg and the three outer isles (Fairwind, Flotsom, Jetsom). At the moment of its fall it was **Prince Fairwind's turn** to hold it — which placed both the secret and the stockpile directly in the hands of the man who betrayed them. Lord Jamis has held the Helm in the past (Merrick served garrison duty there under him, Ch. 16 / `characters/Merrick.md`).

Keep this trio — planning seat, powder storehouse, rotating trust — as the reason the island matters. The theft of the powder and the fall of the Helm are the same crime.

## Position & Approach

- **Distance from Monmurg:** three to five miles, a short crossing rather than the half-day a newcomer might expect (Ch. 17). An hour and a half under sail brought the corsair from the Royal Docks into sight of it.
- **Setting:** a speck of rock and lighthouse fire in the middle of the Monmurgian Straits, between the mainland peninsula and the looming bulk of **Jetsom Island**, well clear of the cold northern waters where the **turtle dragons** are said to nest (Ch. 17).
- **Visible from Monmurg:** the island can be seen from the city's seawall — the unnatural fog that preceded the attack was watched from Monmurg the day before the fleet sailed (Ch. 21).
- **Shape:** a hunched grey shoulder of rock, steep and solitary, its crown crowned by the keep at the island's highest point. Black cliffs worn hollow at the waterline, a scrub of wind-bent green above the tide, and a pale switchback road climbing through it toward the heights (Ch. 17). The interior between the garrison and the Helm is **open ground and light jungle** (Ch. 25).
- **Leeward (sheltered) side:** a natural **breakwater** of jagged black stone shelters the quays and the harbor's low stone buildings, which climb the slope in weathered tiers; the water lies flat and calm in the shadow of the rocks (Ch. 17).

## Fortifications & Layout

Only **one true fort** — the keep — with guard towers by most of the lighthouses and a large barracks midway up the island (Captain Richard's briefing, Ch. 17).

**The keep / the Helm / the citadel** (island's crown):
- Walls stand **fifty feet**; defenders hold the height, archers looking down the length of any approach (Ch. 25).
- The approach is a **ramp and a bridge/drawbridge** — **no moat** — meant for heavy traffic, not for keeping anyone out on its own. Breaching the gate needs someone inside to lower the bridge (Ch. 25).
- **Past the gate, the interior is subdivided:** the **garrison house**, the **tower** (the great signal tower), and the **inner sanctum** are each **separately walled off** — so the gate is only the first of several works (Ch. 25). Fairwind's inner sanctum is the innermost, hard for any force to take outright (Ch. 21).
- A garrison of **~150 men can hold every face of the keep at once** (Ch. 25).
- **Seaward approach:** the cliffs must be scaled first, then a climb by the garrison house and a drop into the courtyard — real skill at the climb required (Ch. 25). This is the route Jude's levitation makes possible (`characters/Jude.md`, `magic_system.md`).

**The garrison** (midway up the island, below the Helm proper):
- A **walled compound behind a wooden palisade**, its own gate and watchtower, roughly a thousand feet from the harbor where Paul's party sheltered (Ch. 17, Ch. 21).
- **Hundred-man garrison.** During the crisis it was left unassaulted and holds intact — "a strange island of order in the wreckage of the day" (Ch. 21).

**The southern spur** (nearest the mainland):
- A small **southern beacon (lighthouse)** with a **southern watchtower** — forty feet of timber, built to cover any landing at the small dock bay below (Ch. 17).
- A small **southern guard barracks** — quarters for forty men at most, "nothing like the great garrison midway up the island" (Ch. 16). Held by hobgoblins during the attack.
- Connected to the island proper by the **southern bridge**; roughly **two miles inland** from the bridge to the garrison (Ch. 20). The spur is the light that lies **nearest the mainland** and the one best placed to throw word back toward Monmurg (Ch. 17).

**The harbor / quays** (leeward, behind the Helm):
- Two stone stairways climb from the waterfront toward the higher ground. The quay wall is set with squat **hard points** — battlements, archers' posts, firing slots cut to rake anyone crossing the open ground below — with a **second tier thirty feet up** (Ch. 17, Ch. 18).

**The three lighthouses — east, west, south:**
- Each is a stone tower of **near eighty feet, several floors** (Ch. 21). Guard towers stand by most of them.
- The **eastern lighthouse** has its own connecting bridge and no proper dock, only mud flats where men might wade ashore; retaking it is hardest, standing in the shadow of the Helm's own cliffs, exposed to fire from above (Ch. 17, Ch. 25).
- The **western beacon** is the easiest of the outlying positions to retake — fifty men marching by night could likely manage it (Ch. 25).

**Far side / anchorage:** there is an **anchorage and a stretch of beach on the island's far side** that could serve to put men ashore; ships can be made to swing wide around the island to avoid the strange vessel in the bay (Ch. 21, Ch. 25).

## The Smugglers' Caves (rumored)

Closely-held Admiralty knowledge, treated in-world as rumor (Ch. 25):
- A **cave system beneath the Helm**, navigable by small boat — an **old smugglers' route from centuries past**.
- Entrance somewhere on the **northern coast** — a well-hidden cave, waters treacherous enough that only a small craft can manage the approach, and **tide-dependent**.
- **Only the Admiralty's pilots know it for certain;** admitting knowledge of it would be treason for a line officer. Even the garrison captain has never set foot down there.
- Suspected as the route the hobgoblins used to come "up from beneath." Jude's live worry: the sahaugin could use any **water-fed tunnel** to reach the interior.

(This is the cave/cavern system Paul later falls into as Fairwind escapes over the side — see manuscript Part Five, "Into the Caverns" / "The Chasm Fight.")

## Signals & Communication

- **Lighthouses signal by changing the color of the light** — **red for an attack** (Ch. 17).
- **Direct word to Monmurg comes from the great tower at the Helm** — several **braziers**, each meaning something different (Ch. 17). Spare braziers and the makings of a proper signal fire are kept at the garrison (Ch. 25).
- **The signal chain:** the eastern light can't be seen from the south and can't reach Monmurg direct; the **west can reach the Helm**, and the **east can relay word on to Flotsom** (Ch. 17). The southern light lies nearest the mainland and is the surest to carry word back to the city.
- **Mirror/light code:** a hinged mirror worked against the sun (or a blinking lamp after dark) in **dots and dashes — the old sea-principality code** that once carried word the length of the Monmurgian coast in minutes (Ch. 22, Ch. 23).
- **Recognition signal — "Karmirg":** a name flashed tower-to-tower to tell friend from enemy. Friendly holders answer; enemy-held towers answer with confusion or silence (Ch. 21–22). Trust-names between the two friendly groups: **Karmirg, Gouge, Jude** (Ch. 21).
- **Beacons are repairable** with oil and polish and a spare brazier — unless the fixture itself is destroyed, as the southern men reported (Ch. 25). A working beacon does double duty: it warns passing ships to steer wide *and* carries word toward the city.

## Water & Supply

**No freshwater source on the island** save rain, caught in **cisterns**. Everything else — water included — is **shipped in** (Ch. 25). A protracted siege therefore turns on supply as much as on walls.

## The Warding Against the sahaugin
The island's deepest strategic secret, and the true crisis when it fails (Ch. 25; cross-reference `magic_system.md`):
- Some **manner of warding has held for over a century**, since the last great war against the sahaugin keeping the shark-folk — and even ordinary sharks — clear of these waters (Ch. 16, Ch. 25).
- **How it is maintained, and by whom, is known only to Lord Fairwind and the Admiralty above him.** No line officer is briefed on it (Ch. 25).
- It is bound up with the **anti-sahaugin powder** stockpiled on the Helm (copper-and-sulfur, an alchemical reaction, not a spell — see `magic_system.md`). Fairwind siphoned the stores off for months aboard Cain Toli's *Sea Ghost*; with the protection gone, the sahaugin return.
- Folk belief ties it to the **turtle dragons**, said to prey on sahaugin as they do on sharks; the garrison captain wonders whether the shark-folk simply inherited the sharks' instinct to avoid the warded water (Ch. 25). Treat this as in-world speculation, consistent with the setting's "mechanism-with-a-mystery" register — don't harden it into fact without a deliberate reveal.
- **The broken warding is the real emergency,** more than the hobgoblins: if the sahaugin can swarm the bay at will, whole relief companies can be lost before they ever make land.

## Command, Garrison & Heraldry (normal state)

- **Rotating command** among Monmurg and the three outer isles; Fairwind held it at the fall; Jamis has held it before (see *Strategic Role*).
- A full **keep garrison of ~150 men** holds the Helm; the midway garrison adds a hundred; the southern beacon its own detachment.
- **War colors** (paint, not heraldry; the armor itself is near-identical across houses — scale mail, standard-issue bows, commanders in breastplates) (Ch. 25):
  - **Blue** — Fairwind's men
  - **Green** — Monmurg
  - **Yellow** — Toli
  - **Brownish-red** — the hobgoblins; belongs to no prince anyone has served under, though their armor is close enough to a Monmurg Marine's to trouble a practiced eye (deliberately fitted to pass at a distance).

## The Crisis (2nd–3rd Wealsun) — Current State

The situation the story turns on:
- **The fog.** The day before the fall, an **unnatural fog** rolled over the island — thick enough to kill a man's sight past five feet. It was watched from Monmurg's seawall the previous day and had lifted before the fleet crossed under a clear sky (Ch. 16, Ch. 21, Ch. 25). Under its cover the attackers almost certainly landed unseen, ferried ashore by small boat to the far-side anchorage (Ch. 25).
- **The attackers, two kinds, working in concert:**
  - **Hobgoblins** — red-faced, inhuman, armored to pass for Monmurg Marines; roughly **a hundred seen this side of the island**, with smaller bands of twenty to thirty elsewhere and an estimated **two hundred to two hundred-fifty (perhaps three hundred) total** (Ch. 18, Ch. 25). Associated with certain nobles out of **Berghof** (Ch. 16). Established in Ch. 43 as **Toli mercenaries** — hired by the shipload from the mountain country behind the **Duchy of Berghof**. They are land soldiers, not sea-creatures; the ancient enemy the powder destroyed 150 years ago was the **sahaugin**, not any hobgoblin kingdom (Ch. 43; `The_Anti-Sahaugin_Powder.md`).
  - **Sahaugin** — the shark-folk, up out of the sea by the hundreds; the greater threat by far, simply because there are more of them, and because they own the water after dark (Ch. 19, Ch. 20, Ch. 25).
- **What has fallen:** the Helm itself is taken; most/all lighthouses are dark; **two ships have been sunk in the bay**, and the corsair that carried the battalion was swarmed and lost with the better part of two hundred Marines aboard (Ch. 20). A **strange vessel** — Cain Toli's *Sea Ghost*, tied to the turtle dragons that drown Monmurg's fleet — prowls the bay (Ch. 21; `characters/Jude.md`, `magic_system.md`).
- **What holds:** **Lord Fairwind** is besieged (no word, believed alive in the inner sanctum). Three friendly strongpoints hold, isolated and unable to see or reach one another — the **midway garrison** (~100), the **southern beacon/tower** (the second squad, ~27 left of 30), and **Paul's landing party** (~20–22 of 30). Somewhere near **a hundred fifty to two hundred Marines** still capable of fighting are scattered across those points (Ch. 23, Ch. 25).
- **The plan taking shape** (Ch. 25): hold the garrison; retake a beacon (the western first) to restore a signal to Monmurg; salvage hobgoblin armor to pass at a distance; and, once Gouge's party arrives, mount a diversion at the gate while a small party climbs the seaward cliffs into the Helm to reach Fairwind and, ultimately, the powder and the warding.

## Quick Reference — Distances & Numbers

- Monmurg → Helm Island: **3–5 miles** (~90 minutes under sail)
- Southern bridge → the garrison: **~2 miles** inland
- Second squad → the main docks: **~1 mile**
- Lighthouses: **~80 ft**, several floors each
- Keep walls: **50 ft**; southern watchtower: **40 ft** of timber; harbor second-tier defenses: **30 ft** up
- Garrison (midway): **100 men**; southern guard barracks: quarters for **40**; keep garrison (normal): **~150**
- Enemy estimate: **~200–250 hobgoblins** (up to ~300), plus **hundreds** of sahaugin
- Friendly holdouts at the low point of the crisis: **~150–200** scattered across three points
