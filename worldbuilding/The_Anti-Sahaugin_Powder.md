# The Anti-Sahaugin Powder

A reference document — the continuity ledger for the substance that is the material heart of the Helm Island plot. It is the "warding" that has kept the sahaugin off Monmurg's waters for a century and a half, the secret Fairwind sold, and the thing the survivors raft home with. Cross-check any scene that touches the powder, the warding, or the barrels against this file (and against `magic_system.md`, where it also appears under "Alchemy & Artifice").

Popular in-world names: **"the powder," "the compound," "the substance,"** and the euphemism the Toli use for it, **"pest control."** In the open, almost no one calls it anything, because almost no one knows it exists.

## What it is

Plain **copper and sulfur**, combined in a way no one now living knows how to reproduce — plus a scatter of trace elements an alchemist can detect but not identify. It is emphatically **not magic**: an alchemical reaction, not a spell, with materials, a procedure, and a failure mode. The mystery is only the *manner of combining* (Book 2 Ch. 8). This is the setting's signature "hard" device — chemistry with a magic-shaped hole where the method should be.

**Appearance:** a fine, **pale yellow powder**, stored in stout barrels marked with **three red X's** apiece (Ch. 6). Loose, it smells faintly of sulfur.

## How it works

**Far more dangerous to water-breathers than to air-breathers — but not harmless to either.** Diluted through the sea as the warding uses it, the treated water is safe for a man or an elf to swim: at roughly one part in a thousand, spread across miles of ocean, the compound is far too thin to harm an air-breather — which is the whole point of the warding, clearing the water of gilled things while leaving it fit for shipping and human use. The **raw, concentrated powder is deadly, and by inhalation above all: breathing in even a minute amount will kill an air-breather.** (Paul's mother warned him of exactly this over the three-marked barrels in her hidden room — "Breathing in even a minute amount of that will kill you, Paul" — Ch. 6, Ch. 31.) Heavy skin contact is caustic besides, staining and burning exposed skin a sickly greenish-yellow, and it is a poison to get in the eyes or to swallow — so out of the barrel it is handled with great care (Ch. 38, where Tyrus douses himself opening one and Paul, recognizing what it is, warns him off breathing the dust and tells him to wash every trace of it away). To anything with **gills**, it is deadlier still — lethal in trace amounts even diluted: roughly **one part in a thousand** of even a token pinch kills a fish outright, entering straight through the gills into the blood (Book 2 Ch. 8). Sprinkled into the sea, it **poisons the water** against the shark-folk (and against ordinary sharks, which is why the warded waters run clear of both). It does not "make them vanish"; it makes the water uninhabitable to them, and they leave or die.

**Potency — the standard figure (keep consistent):** **one barrel protects roughly ten square miles of water for about ten days.** Use this everywhere a character estimates coverage or duration; do not render it as a radius-in-miles or a months-long span. A single barrel is therefore enough to secure the Helm and its bay for a short window — long enough to hold the island or cover a crossing, not long enough to be left and forgotten. Protection must be renewed; the stores are a running cost, not a one-time enchantment.

**Volatility:** *not ordinarily* explosive — inert as sand in the barrel. But under **pressure**, or after **aging in seawater**, the brine reaction gives off heat enough to catch fire (Book 2 Ch. 8). Practical consequence for the plot: you cannot simply burn the Toli's ship to solve the problem — fire near the aged, sea-soaked barrels risks destroying the very thing worth recovering (Ch. 33).

## Origin and history

- Made or discovered by the **Fhoraxians**, a little-known people of the mountains along the **Duke of Berghof's southwestern border**. The Hold bought it **at cost from the Duke of Berghof** (Ch. 33, Book 2 Ch. 8).
- It was the weapon that **turned the wars against the sahaugin roughly a hundred and fifty years ago**, and it has held the shark-folk off ever since (Ch. 33; `Helm_Island.md`).
- The wider world — most of Monmurg included — believes the island's protection is an **"ancient enchantment."** That is a **deliberate cover story**, maintained to hide both the true (mundane, reproducible-in-principle) nature of the defense and its single greatest weakness: the supply.
- The **supply is nearly spent.** The Hold has not been able to acquire more in a long while; a sample recovered on the Helm is about **twenty years old — the last lot Jeon's people managed to obtain** (Book 2 Ch. 8). The method has been lost; the raw materials are cheap (sulfur sells at any market stall), so anyone who *cracked the manner of combining* could make it by the ton — which is exactly why the secret is worth killing over.

## The lost method — attempts to recreate it

- **Grayson Jamis** (Paul's father) pursued the powder's true composition before his death, treating it as his life's work — "recreating the pale yellow powder… the very weapon that once turned the tides against the sahaugin" (Ch. 7). He may have left **formulae or notes** (Book 2 Ch. 8).
- An **old alchemist in Monmurg's foreign district** — the same who sold Jude the analyzed sample — can describe *what* it does but not *how* it is made, and holds notes of his own he believes may bear on the question (Book 2 Ch. 8). Reproducing the powder is an open, load-bearing mystery of Book 2 and beyond; any breakthrough should read as alchemical/procedural (a recovered formula, a technique), never as a spell that fixes the problem.

## Strategic role — and the crime

- The **Helm is the chief storehouse** and the powder is the Hold's most closely guarded secret; only **Fairwind and the Admiralty** know how the warding is maintained (`Helm_Island.md`). This is why the island matters: planning seat, powder storehouse, rotating trust in one place. **The theft of the powder and the fall of the Helm are the same crime.**
- **Fairwind siphoned the stores off for months**, loading them aboard Cain Toli's ship, the ***Sea Ghost***, and quietly moving as much as he could to a cache of his own on **Fairwind Island** (Ch. 33, Book 2 Ch. 2). With the protection gone, the sahaugin returned — and a corsair of Monmurg Marines was overrun in the bay (roughly two hundred dead).
- The party recovers only what remains: of some **twenty-five barrels** still on the island, the survivors raft home with **four** (Book 2 Ch. 2).

## Open threads (established, unresolved — don't silently close)

- **The manner of combining** — the central unsolved question; the throughline connecting Grayson's research, Zafar Azane, the alchemist's notes, and the Duke of Berghof's territories.
- **Why the Toli want it.** They do not obviously suffer the sahaugin themselves; their motive in stripping Monmurg's defense is political, tied to the blockade and the disputes among the principalities (Ch. 33).
- **Folk belief** ties the clear water to the **turtle dragons** (said to prey on sahaugin as on sharks); treat as in-world speculation, not confirmed fact (`Helm_Island.md`).
