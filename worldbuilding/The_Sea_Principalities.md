# The Sea Principalities
The buccaneers of the Azure Sea and Jerlea
Bay grew strong and wealthy when Keoland
was at the height of its power, for the eyes of
Keoland’s leaders were turned northward
toward empire, and the sea raiders were
ignored. These privateers took to calling
themselves Sea Princes, after a particularly
successful captain who was in fact of noble
birth. Sailing unchecked from their island
and coastal strongholds, these raiders were
once the scourge of the coasts from Gradsul
to Scant, on the Pomarj, and even beyond into
the Sea of Gearnat and the Tilva Strait. When
Keoland turned back from imperial
expansion, her navy began to rebuild in order
to check the threat of the Sea Princes, as they
were now commonly known. Their numbers
and strength had become so great, however,
that the Keoish fleet, even with the aid of a
squadron of Ulek warships, could at best
deliver a sharp check to them (Battle of
Jetsom Island, CY 464). This lesson caused their leaders to rethink their policies,
however, and several of the wiser captains
retired to mainland estates, appointing
lieutenants to command their ships, not in
piratical or raiding activities in the Flanaess,
but on expeditions to the Amedio coast and
thence to trade northward with the rare
woods, spices, ivory, and gold which they
wrested from the jungle savages. Eventually
the mainland possessions of the Sea Princes
amounted to more territory than their island
homes, and they practiced little formal
raiding. Today they probably are still the
strongest sea power, but they also have a
small and efficient army and are relatively
peaceful traders.

## Early History

It is thought that Flan tribesmen were the
first humans to wander into the lands east of
the great Crystalmist Mountains, to the
inland regions of what is now considered the
territory of the Sea Princes. They were
nomadic hunters and did little to colonize the
area, finding the verdant Hool Marshes more
suited to the nomadic life that most Flan
tribes were accustomed to. Consequently, the
Flan have very little presence in what is today
the Hold of the Sea Princes and most peoples
of Flannish descent live along the northern
border near Westkeep.
The Suel colonized the area thousands of
years before the fall of the Suel Empire,
establishing permanent settlements along the
Crystalmists Mountains in what is today
known as the Duchy of Berghof. Both the
cities of Hokar and Port Toli are thought to
have been founded during the Suel
colonization period and the construction and
culture of those two cities display significant
Suel influences compared to other major
settlements in the Sea Principalities.
Renegade Suel fleeing the Empire and the
war with the Baklunish are thought to have
avoided this region, migrating primarily
through the Yeomanry into Keoland and
beyond. However, after the devastation of the
Twin Cataclysms, many Suel refugees risked
the perilous migration over the Crystalmist
Mountains and moved into the coastal
territories of what is now the Hold of the Sea
Princes. In the eastern and northern coastal
regions, the migrating Suel came into conflict
with fierce Oeridian tribes that had come
before. Each race was unable to dominate the
other and over the centuries the two peoples
intermingled considerably, especially along
the northern coast from Westkeep to
Monmurg. However, the Oeridian influence
still dominates the island territories while the
interior regions and eastern coastline have
significant Suel influences.
As populations in lands to the north
increased, trade down the Javan River, one of
the longest navigable rivers in the lands,
increased greatly. Four cities and three
island holdings rose to dominance in the
lands that are now known as the Hold of the
Sea Princes. Monmurg and Port Toli were two
of the oldest and most accessible settlements
and consequently are the largest. Monmurg
had grown up from the villages and towns
that the first Oeridian buccaneers had based
their activities out of and had acted as a
meeting place for the Oeridian tribes for
centuries. Port Toli’s growth and wealth
increased as the Oeridian tribes became
interested in the slaves and the wood that
Port Toli’s raiders wrested from the Amedio
jungle and it grew as more and more
Oeridians joined in those activities. Westkeep
grew with the trade that moved up and down
the river to the Yeomanry and further east
becoming a fortified gateway along this
east-west passage. As the population of the
region grew even the ancient and remote city
of Hokar benefited and turned into a center
for mining, agriculture and other industry.
Seven families, four descended from an
original captain of the buccaneers and three
from the ancient Plardoms that had existed
since the days of the Suel colonization, moved
to the forefront of leadership in the region.

## Recent History

Until recently the region was balkanized into
these seven land holdings, city states and
island strongholds, each with their own
distinct cultures, interests and customs. This
began to change in CY 400 when the ruling
family of Port Toli entered into a trading and
mutual protection agreement with the city
state of Monmurg. The merchants of Port Toli
provided access to the riches of the Amedio
Jungle through the extensive number of
outposts and trade routes it controlled. In
exchange the ships from Monmurg quit
attacking Port Toli convoys and provided
protection from the raiders that sailed from
Flotsom, Jetsom and Fairwind islands. As the
fleet of Monmurg increased in size, mainly
from the wealth and resources provided by
Port Toli, the commodores of the outer
islands realized that joining with Monmurg
and Port Toli was more profitable than
opposing them. The economic power of Port
Toli combined with the sea power of
Monmurg and the outer islands created a
potent sea-based economy unrivaled in all
the Flanesse. This, coupled with a naval power
that dominated the Azure Sea, gave this
relatively small region incredible trading
power throughout the Flanesse. Much of this
power was derived from buccaneers sailing
from Monmurg and the outer islands, who
would attack any trading ships that didn’t fly
the flag of Port Toli and eventually, as the
union between the Sea Princes strengthened,
the flag of the Sea Principalities. In CY 447
the region officially unified, creating the Hold
of the Sea Princes or the Sea Principalities as
it is sometimes known. This nation is ruled by
a council of five princes (the Prince of
Monmurg, the Prince of Toli and the Princes
of Flotsom, Jetsom and Fairwind), each with
an equal vote, that are advised by the Plar of
Hokar and the Plar of Westkeep. The Prince of
Monmurg is recognized as the supreme
commander of the navy during times of war
while the Plar of Westkeep is given absolute
control of the nation’s small, but
professional, army.

After unification the buccaneers of the Sea
Princes grew even stronger and wealthier,
raiding the coastlines of the Flanesse. The
merchants of Port Toli and Monmurg held a
monopoly on the trade routes that crossed
the Azure Sea and both cities profited
considerably. In the early days of the new
nation the growth was not limited, as
Keoland, the biggest and dominant power in
the area, was looking northwards towards
the empire and mostly ignored the sea
raiders. By CY 450 Keoland realized its
mistake, and the Duke of Gradsul called for
action against this new rival. At first Keoland
tried to deal with the Sea Princes by attacking
the region through the Hool Marshes, which
resulted in a disastrous defeat at the Siege of
Westkeep in CY 453. This was followed by
several lesser, but equally ill advised,
campaigns over the next 50 years. By CY 500
Keoland finally realized that they could not
defeat the Sea Princes without challenging
them at sea and the naval yards of Gradsul
began to construct a strong fleet to combat
the raiders. But by then the Sea Princes were
quite powerful and long experienced in the naval tradition. When Keoland finally acted
the Sea Princes fought the combined navies of
Keoland and Ulek to a near draw in the Battle
of Jetsom Island in CY 525. Although the Sea
Princes were technically victorious after this
engagement, the cost of the battle, combined
with the increased patrols on the oceans by
Keoland and Ulek, made the leaders of the
Sea Princes rethink their policies. The
captains began to decrease their piratical
practices and turned more to trade or
mercenary work. They soon found that trade
was almost as lucrative as banditry and much
safer. As a precaution the council also began
to fortify the coastal and island holdings,
making the main cities and major outposts of
the Sea Princes nearly impregnable to direct
attack.

Today the princes have mostly exchanged
piracy for more "legitimate" enterprises.
Their ships still reach the same ports and
even head to those beyond the Flanaess but
rather than swordsmen they are filled with
exotic goods and shrewd merchants.
However, this didn’t put an end to piracy in
the region. The princes still support
privateers, issuing letters of mark to any who
promise not to attack ships bearing the
princes' coat of arms. Many ships from the
other nations of the Flanaess will often hang
the Sea Princes' colors in order to avoid such
attacks. On rare occasions a prince might
lead his personal fleet out on a raid if he
believes some particularly lucrative prize
might be won.
Though most of the princes' trade is
conventional, they are also the largest
exporter of slaves in the whole of the
Flanaess. Some slaves are taken in raids
across the Hool Marshes into the Yeomanry
and sometimes even Keoland, but the bulk are
captured from the native tribes that inhabit
the Amedeo jungle. This practice has made
the Sea Princes unpopular in all those areas,
but their mercenary armies and massive
fortifications are more than enough to stop
any assault on their major holdings. The fact
that their fleet could easily prevent any
sea-based invasion and the inhospitable
nature of the Hool Marshes, which provides a
natural barrier on the norther border, and
it’s easy to see why the Sea Princes can exist
as a separate nation despite their relatively
small size and regional unpopularity. Any
Keoish force besieging a Sea Principality city
would be at a great disadvantage; as a result,
an uncomfortable peace has existed between
the two nations for almost a century.

# Important Historical Events

While the Hold of the Sea Princes is a young
nation it has a vibrant and complex history.
The most important events in that history are
summarised here.

## Rise of the Sea Princes

In CY 447 the Princes and Plars of the Hold of
the Sea Prices signed the “Pact of Equals”
pledging non-aggression and cooperation
between the five princes and two Plars of the
region. Since that time the Council of Princes
have ruled the Hold of the Sea Princes in
unison; disagreement occurs often but all
stand together when a decision is made.

## Siege of Westkeep

In the spring of 453 CY, the king of Keoland,
Tavish III, led a large army through the
tangles of the Hool Marsh, intending to lay
siege to the fort city of Westkeep. The trek
through the swamp proved disastrous,
however, and many of the soldiers were
forced to cast away their armor in the
quagmire. The army's wagon train suffered
tremendous difficulty, and many soldiers
took sick within the first two days of the
march. Tavish seemed indignant. Westkeep
would fall, and he would hoist the Lion
Rampant upon its highest tower.
When finally the army arrived at the
outskirts of the fort city, the forces of
Westkeep were well prepared. Those
Keolandish soldiers who did not mutiny were
cut down by an unrelenting barrage of arrow
fire. The Siege of Westkeep, as it would soon
be known, lasted 70 minutes. Tavish himself
was slain in battle, and the forces of the Sea
Princes celebrated that night under the
standard of the crowned caravel (flag of the
Principalities).

## Battle of Jetsom Island

The Battle of Jetsom Island, which occured in
the late summer of 464 CY, saw the Keoish
navy sink the Sea Prince, the flagship of the
Sea Principalities fleet, with all hands lost.
Though not a decisive military victory for
Keoland, the action marked a turning point
for the holders. Thereafter, many of the old
captains retired from piracy, settling the
mainland and forming a more stabilized
government. The younger captains turned
from piracy to relatively legitimate pursuits.
Thus a chaotic nation of pirates and
scalawags turned their attention to
mercantilism, and the coffers of the Sea
Princes swelled to bursting.

## Climate

The Hold of the Sea Princes is in the
subtropical south. The weather tends to be
hot and humid during most months, with
mild to warm temperatures in the winter.
Strong storms occasionally batter the outer
islands, but most of the inner coast is
protected from violent storms, where there
are forest palm trees mixed with oaks and
other trees. The waters of the Seas around the
hold are quite warm and are considered
tropical. The coast of Jerlea Bay and the
southern islands are tropical, the waters full
of corals and other warm water species.
North of the islands is a bit cooler, with sea
grasses being the predominant eco-type.

## Dress and Culture

The culture of the Hold is extremely diverse
both because of the regional influences of the
seven original provinces but also due to the
enormous amount of foreign contact the
traders of the Hold encounter. Societal norms
are often a regional concern and even the
most conservative native will tolerate the
behavior, accent or dress of a foreigner. The
citizens of Monmurg are the most
cosmopolitan and are constantly adopting
the latest fashion or trend introduced to
them. The outer islands are almost as
adaptable and often try to keep up with their
more cultured cousins from Monmurg. As a
result, the residents of Monmurg and the
outer islands dress in an ostentatious
manner that is considered garish by other
cultures, especially Keoland and the
Yeomanry. Layers of silk, bejeweled hats, jade
masks, peacock feathers, and other even more
bizarre wares find their way into these
outfits, often all in the same one. Although
outfits are elaborate, all citizens prefer to
dress in light clothing, thin silks being
favored by the well off.
The culturally uninhibited attitudes and
adventurous nature of the coastal
population extends to the governance of the
region and most of the cities and villages are
accepting of a broad range of behaviors and
activities. Drugs, prostitution and other vices
are business opportunities, not things to
suppress and the local authorities usually
turn a blind eye to any activity that doesn’t
cause harm to another.

In contrast Plardom’s are much more
conservative and people generally wear the
traditional garb associated with their race. In
Westkeep the Oeridians generally wear cotton
and linen garments colored brown or dark
green. The Flan wear furs, leathers or, when
they can afford it, clothing like their Oeridian
counterparts. The Plar of Westkeep runs the
city like a military outpost and enforces strict
laws on all the city’s citizens. However, his
attempts to extend this law to the Flan
population outside Westkeep’s wall are often
unsuccessful.

Hokar and the Duchy of Berghof societies
seem to be from a past age and little has
changed in the last millennium. The Suel
scholars wear long flowing robes that are
usually white to indicate their vocation. The
overseers, when out of their armor, dress in
cotton togas dyed a color that indicates their
family allegiance. The slaves generally wear
undyed togas made from coarser material
but basically in the same fashion.
Hokar and Berghof are run by Suel
aristocracy overseeing a feudalistic society.
The structure of this society is hierarchical
and strict; most crimes are punishable by
death and the local lord has the power of life
or death over the slaves in his charge. Order
is enforced by hobgoblin mercenaries hired
from the nearby Hellfurnace mountains and
an efficient bureaucracy runs the central
government in Hokar.

Port Toli is in cultural conflict with itself,
containing both the richest and refined
citizens of the Hold as well as the most
desperate and despicable. The Suel
aristocrats live in opulent mansions away
from the harbors or slums of the city. While
the grandeur of Monmurg’s palaces far
outshines any building Port Toli has to offer,
the cultural tastes of the Port Toli aristocrats
are unequalled in the Hold, possibly even
more refined than the nobles of Gradsul. The
aristocrats import artisans, scholars and
actors to educate and entertain themselves
and the ruler of Port Toli is the greatest
patron of the arts in the Hold. The Suel
aristocrats often favor fashions that are
popular among the Gradsul nobility; fine
jacketed suits adorned with silk and gold
thread which are opulent yet tasteful.
In stark contrast to the Suel aristocracy are
the sailors, mercenaries and slavers that man
Port Toli’s fleets and military posts. A mixture
of Oeridian and Suel outcasts from the
different societies in the Hold, the average
Port Toli commoner is both uncouth and
incorrigible. They have reputations as being
greedy, meanspirited and untrustworthy.
While this may be true for some, many are
sailors or soldiers who had fallen into
misfortune, forced to serve in the Port Toli
aristocracy in order to survive. All have a
devil may care attitude as they recognize
fortune is fleeting, life is brutal and short,
and you may as well take what you can get
when you can.
Port Toli commoners have an unrefined
appearance that evokes their roguish
personalities and adventurous, seafaring
pursuits. Most adopt garish dress like the
buccaneers of Monmurg and the outer
islands, albeit with cheaper materials and
shabbier appearances. There is, however,
significant influence from the savages of the
Amedio Jungles and bone necklaces, jewelry
and more macabre items are prevalent in Port
Toli fashion.

## Monmurg

Monmurg is the capital city of the Hold of the
Sea Princes, and is ruled by Jeon II, the Prince
of Monmurg. The gilded domes and spires of
Monmurg are a testament to the Sea Princes
one-time profession; piracy. The ancestors of
the current princes ranged as far as Relmor
Bay, taking ships, maidens, and other forms
of plunder whenever they could find it.
Especially bold captains might even land near
an isolated town and attempt to sack it,
carting away wonderful treasures and many
slaves. Hence, the capital of Monmurg is truly
a sight to behold. Despite its small size when
compared with cities such as Greyhawk,
Monmurg's splendor may be without equal on
the continent. It possesses great onion domed
palaces covered in turquoise and precious
metals, many spired temples to the various
gods whose cults the sailors brought home
with them after their many excursions, and
slaves and citizens from all the four corners
of the world. Mahogany from the southern
continent and precious metals from beyond
the Sea of Dust fill its markets. It is no
wonder that so many adventurers make it
here to find work or plunder.
Monmurg is the largest city in the Hold, with
a population around 25,000 natives and
nearly 10,000 foreigners. It is the main naval
center for the Hold and is the home port for
many of the trading ships that ply the Azure
Sea that transport goods from as far away as
the City of Greyhawk and the Great Kingdom.
Four small hills make up the area that
Monmurg is built upon, and the royal castle
sits atop the highest. Although the shoreline
of the Monmurg peninsula is not walled, the
Western Wall protects most of the city from
the mainland.

Monmurg is the most metropolitan city in the
Sea Principalities. Trade ships from all over
the Flaness bring wealth that is both vast and
varied to Monmurg and influences from all
over Oerth have gained a foothold in the city
to access that wealth. Most of the citizens of
Monmurg are involved in trade in some
capacity or another and are consequently
well off financially. The large foreign
population are primarily traders,
mercenaries or adventurers engaged in
various activities that require the access and
freedom that the city of Monmurg provides.

## Ruler of Monmurg

The City of Monmurg is ruled by Prince Jeon
II, captain of all Fleets and Ruler of the Azure
Sea. Considered first among equals on the
council of five princes, Prince Jeon II wields
considerable power outside of Monmurg and,
although not the absolute ruler of the Hold,
he is considered the official leader.

In the city of Monmurg the prince rules
through a council of advisors made up of the
merchants, scholars and military leaders that
reside in the city. The most influential is Lord
Jamis, commodore of the fleet of Monmurg
and commander of the Harbor District. The
Prince trusts Lord Jamis implicitly and relies
on him to coordinate the security and defense
of the city.

The Prince of Monmurg is generally
considered to be a good man and a fair ruler
and is widely loved by the people of the hold.
Like all ruling members of his lineage Prince
Jeon is a swashbuckler of some repute.

### Foreign District

The foreign district is home to thousands of
merchants, mercenaries and adventurers
from all over the Flaness who have traveled to
Monmurg to seek fortune, adventure and
sometimes even more nefarious purposes. The
Foreign District is a crowded, labyrinthine
place rife with corruption; it is decadent and
squalid in roughly equal parts and is the only
location in the city of Monmurg that
non-citizens are allowed to take residence.

The district is nested next to the south of the
Harbor District and the Grand Market along
the eastern shoreline of the city. A wall
surrounds the district and is well patrolled,
but its purpose is to defend the rest of
Monmurg from the residence of the Foreign
District, not the other way around. The
district is ostensibly ruled by Lord Jamis, who
runs the Harbor District, but guard patrols
are scarcely found away from the wall. The
lack of a central authority, coupled with the
laissez-faire attitude the Prince of Monmurg
has towards commerce, ensures the Foreign
District to be a center of corruption, crime
and contraband. It is said that if something
cannot be found in the Foreign District then it
doesn’t exist.

Streets in the Foreign District are often
evocatively named and have been known to
change depending on use or a particularly
noteworthy event. Commonly referenced
locations are Ferd’s Tavern, behind which is
Bloody Alley, and the Shadow Docks. The main
meeting place is the Plaza of Dark Delights,
which is well known to cater to most of the
hedonistic tastes found throughout the
Flaness. The religious center of the Foreign
District is the Street of the Gods along which
numerous and bizarre cults seek to arrange
themselves in order of popularity, often
changing temples as their fortunes change,
allowing them to rent greater and more
opulent venues.

### Grand Market

The Grand Market is a huge marketplace
located between the four hills that make up
the city, the Foreign District, the Western Wall
and the Harbor District. All residents, both
citizens and foreigners, are welcome to the
Grand Market during the day and goods
from all over the Flaness can be found for sale
here. There are numerous other smaller
marketplaces around the city, but the variety
of goods found in the Grand Market cannot
be equaled.
Although there are some residences along the
Western Wall, primarily owned by citizen
merchants and business owners, most of the
structures in the Grand Market are not
permanent and can be erected or
disassembled when needed. Correspondingly,
there are very few residents in this district,
and most will return to either the Foreign
District or one of the Hill Districts when
trading is finished for the day.
Harbor District

The city of Monmurg has an extensive harbor
inside a curving two-mile-long spit of land
that sticks out into the ocean. A pair of forts,
one at the end of the spit, one the shoreside,
protect the harbor. The docks can
accommodate as many as one-hundred or so
ships at a time and it is often at full capacity
especially during the trading season during
high summer.
This district is run by Lord Jamis,
Commodore of the Prince of Monmurg, who is
the senior military commander under the
command of Prince Jeon II. In addition to
commanding a squadron of twelve of
Monmurg’s fastest ships, Lord Jamis
commands a well-trained battalion of
mercenary guardsmen that are charged with
keeping order in the Harbor and Foreign
Districts.

### The Hill Districts

The four Hills of Monmurg are home to most
of Monmurg’s native populations. The
districts are roughly segregated into
merchant, noble and scholar populations
while the fourth district is dominated by the
royal residence and the buildings housing his
family and servants.
The Palace district is on the tallest hill of the
Monmurg peninsula and is the location of six
of the most opulent palaces in the Flaness.
The largest, The Palace of the Prince, is the
residence of Prince Jeon II.

### Strait of Monmurg

The Strait of Monmurg is a ten mile wide
channel that connects the northern region of
Jerlea bay with the Azure Sea. Monmurg lies
on the western side of the channel while the
tip of Jetsom Island lies to the east. All of the
trade ships travelling to the Hold must pass
through this Strait or dock in Monmurg’s
Harbor District. Consequently it is possibly
the most navigated body of water in the
Flaness.

Prince Jeon II considers the strait a strategic
asset and has spent considerable resources to
control it. A squadron of warships constantly
patrol it and any ship that doesn’t fly the
trade flag of the Hold will be stopped and
boarded if they enter the strait.

### Helm Island

In the very center of the Strait of Monmurg
lies Helm Island, a rocky island about one
mile in diameter. Several lighthouses and
watchtowers have been constructed on this
island to help ships navigate the strait and to
provide advance warnings of threats or
interlopers to the Sea Principality navy.
In addition to the other structures the island
contains a small but impressively fortified
castle on its northern shore, known as The
Helm. This castle is the traditional location
used by the Princes and Commodores of the
Hold to plan naval campaigns.

## Hokar

Hokar, population 11,000, is the only major
city of the Hold that is not on the coast or a
major waterway. While the other cities in the
Hold rely on trade, Hokar is the production
capital of the realm. Hokar sits at the head of
the fertile, low grasslands of the Hold, as well
as being adjacent to the mountains and hills
in the south of the country. From Hokar, the
Plar of Hokar administers the farms,
plantations, and mines in the area known as
the Duchy of Berghof. While Monmurg and
the other cities control trade, Hokar controls
production. These lands are mostly organized
as feudal plantations run by a Suel
aristocracy and worked by slave laborers. The
mines in the mountains and hills are the
same, requiring large amounts of slave labor.
The Plardom of Hokar and the Duchy of
Berghof are the largest importers of slaves in
the Hold, effectively keeping that practice
alive even as the rest of the Sea Princes have
moved towards trade. More than half of those
enslaved by the raiders of Port Toli are
exported to the city of Hokar and the Suel
overlords of the Plardom are frequent visitors
to Port Toli’s Slaver Square. These lords get
the first look of the yearly “harvest” and are
picky about the slaves that they choose to
purchase, paying gold for those that they
want but rejecting the rest. The phrase “The
Feraixian’s Share” refers to the company of
slaves the overlords of Hokar purchase at the
end of the raiding season and has come to
mean the greater, higher quality, part of any
division of wealth.

### Ruler of Hokar

The Plar of Hokar is a sorcerer of great power
and repute and, although not considered evil,
he has been said to work with strange and
ancient magic. Consequently, he has the most
mysterious reputation of the leaders of the
Hold, if not the darkest. There are constant
rumors that the Plar is seeking to dominate
the other leaders of the Hold and turn the
lands into his own domain. Other rumors say
he is just interested in studying ancient
magics or knowledge associated with the Suel
Empire. Whatever the case, the other rulers of
the Hold do not seem overly worried about
the Plar as he is uninterested in the wealth or
pageantry displayed by his peers. Other than
his modest palace in Hokar, the Plar of Hokar
has a smaller citadel near the Hool marshes,
on the shores of the Hool river. Both are quite
old and are thought to have been constructed
during the days of Suel colonization.
The Plar is also quite old and is known to
have ruled as the Plar of Hokar for over 150
years. Strangely, he has the appearance of a
30-year-old man. Many think he must be
using strong magic to keep himself young.

## Port Toli

Port Toli, with a population of 18,000, sits
astride a point of land that stretches far out
into Jerlea Bay. The south coast of the point
consists of wave battered, rocky cliffs. This
coast is a favored place for the rich Suel
nobles to build grand manor houses
overlooking the ocean. There is one well
protected embayment in the cliffs, opening to
a small bay. Two small fortresses sit astride
the opening to this embayment, protecting
the ships within. The north coast of the point
is better protected from the elements and
possesses a wide, protected harbor.
While the city of Port Toli still supports a
large amount of sea trade the commerce that
passes through its ports is less than in its
heyday and is far eclipsed by that which
passes through Monmurg these days. Port
Toli tends to be the main port for those ships
sailing to the south and east coasts of Jerlea
Bay, usually raiders and slavers on excursions
to the Amedio Jungle. These ships bring back
rare woods, spices, ivory, gold, but especially
slave labor from the jungles to the south. Port
Toli has the only remaining slave market in
the Hold, and slaves are now its main export.
However, Port Toli still has several upscale
marketplaces catering to the rich Suel
population that is prevalent.
The districts populated by the commoners of
Port Toli are dangerous, home to the
buccaneers, slavers and mercenaries hired by
the Port Toli captains that raid into the
Amedio jungle. The men involved in these
endeavors are used to violence, oppression
and death and do not shy away from
confrontation or take insults lightly. There is
a fine line between comradery and conflict,
and casual ribbing can quickly turn into a
fight to the death.

### Ruler of Port Toli

Sacnon Toli, the Prince of Port Toli, is
considered to be second only to Prince Jeon II
in terms of political power in the Hold of the
Sea Princes. Of pure Suel descent, Sacnon
commands the loyalty of the entire Suel
population of Port Toli and is closely allied
with the Plar of Hokar and the Plar of
Westkeep. He has political connections
throughout the Flaness including the leaders
of Keoland, the Great Kingdom and Nyrond.
If Prince Jeon II is the official ruler of the
Hold, Prince Sacnon Toli is the official
diplomat. He often entertains the
ambassadors of other nations at his opulent
estates.
The Toli family is large and pervasive in the
governance of the Sea Principalities. Many of
Sacnon’s cousins hold positions of power
throughout the Hold or have diplomatic posts
in other nations. Because of this Sacnon is
usually well informed about events in the
Hold and throughout the Flaness.

### The Cliffs

The majority of the Suel population reside on
The Cliffs, a neighborhood of rich mansions
located on the coastline south of the city. The
manor houses here sit on large, lush grounds
and, although they don’t approach the
grandeur of the palaces of Monmurg, are
very opulent. All the houses and grounds are
well guarded, and the owners have been
known to deal with intruders by their own
methods.

The Toli family and the Prince of Port Toli
have several properties on The Cliffs and
preside over the societal activities of the Suel
residences.

### Slaver’s Square

Slaver’s Square is a place that the civilized
people of Port Toli avoid. It has been referred
to by the Duke of Gradsul as the blight of the
western lands and is the only place slaves are
still sold in the entire southwest of the
Flanaess. Strangely, the traditional language
used by the merchants, auctioneers and
buyers in Slaver’s Square is giant. Buyers that
speak that language get special treatment
and are usually given a preview of the daily
parade.

'The show' commences about four o'clock in
the afternoon. The slaves, set off to the best
advantage by having their skins cleaned and
burnished with oil, their faces painted with
red and white stripes and the hands, noses,
ears and feet ornamented with a profusion of
bracelets of gold and silver and jewels, are
ranged in a line, commencing with the
youngest, and increasing to the rear
according to their size and age. At the head of
this file, which is composed of all sexes and
ages from 6 to 60, walks the person who owns
them; behind and at each side, two or three of
his domestic slaves, armed with swords and
spears, serve as guard.
Thus the procession begins, and passes
through the marketplace and the principal
streets . . . when any of them strikes a
spectator's fancy the line immediately stops,
and a process of examination ensues, which,
for minuteness, is unequalled in any cattle
market in the Hold. The intending purchaser
having ascertained there is no defect in the
faculties of speech, hearing, etc., that there is
no disease present, next proceeds to examine
the person; the mouth and the teeth are first
inspected and afterwards every part of the
body in succession, many of whom are
handled in the most indecent manner in the
public market by their purchasers.

### Outposts of the Amedio

The aristocrats of Port Toli maintain a chain
of fortifications and outposts along the
eastern coast of Jerlea Bay along the edges of
the Amedio Jungle. These outposts are used
to trade with the natives of the Amedio and
are used as base camps for raids into the
jungle.
Most of the outposts are only garrisoned
during the summer raiding seasons and are
usually ramshackle structures made from
whatever materials can be found. However,
several of the older outposts near the
Hellfurnace mountains were constructed
during the period of Suel colonization and
are impressive fortifications. These are
garrisoned year round by hobgoblin
mercenaries from the Hellfurnace Mountains.

### The Arcane Order

While practicing magic is generally accepted
in the Hold of the Sea Princes most of the
greatest wizards or sorcerers of the Hold can
be found among the Suel aristocracy of Port
Toli. It is rumored that a secret organization,
the Arcane Order, exists in Port Toli which
possesses the magical knowledge of the Suel
Empire. The Arcane Order is said to jealously
protect this knowledge, teaching it only to
those among the Suel aristocracy that display
the necessary talent and loyalty to the Suel
race. The Arcane Order supposedly protects a
secret library containing many of the original
tomes written during the height of the Suel
Empire. If such a library exists the knowledge
found there would surpass what could be
found in all the other libraries in the Flaness
combined.

## Westkeep

Westkeep is built where the Javan river runs
along the south edge of the Hool Marshes.
The city grew from a village to a mighty city
as trade increased on the river. Westkeep,
with a population of about 14,000, is the
center for river trade and a hub for
mercenaries and others providing protection
for the trade guilds. In Westkeep, trade
moving on smaller boats and barges transfers
to larger vessels that continue down the river
and over the short ocean trip to Monmurg.
But the main industry of Westkeep is
protecting shipments that must travel
through the perilous Hool Marshes.
If Monmurg is a metropolitan city, Westkeep
is a militant city. It is a haven for
mercenaries, and there are numerous guilds
of fighters, warriors, scouts and other
martial types looking to hire themselves out
to trade houses or independents moving
through the swamps or up and down the
river. The city is well equipped and used to
having many combatants. There are many
places that serve the needs of the
mercenaries, providing entertainment of all
sorts for those coming with new pay.
The city is predominantly composed of
wooden buildings of two or three stories. The
grandest structure is the Keep of the Plar, the
Westkeep as it is known.
This was built as a great stone tower when
the first Plar settled here long before they
began to collect tribute from trade moving by
on the river. As money flowed into the Plar’s
coffers, the keep continued to grow, until now
it is a fortified castle surrounding the
original stone tower.

### Ruler of Westkeep

The Plar of Westkeep is a wealthy and
powerful lord among the Sea Princes owning
large land holdings to the south and west of
the city. Many of these holdings are leased to
the Plar of Hokar and are cultivated by the
numerous slaves from the Duchy of Berghof.
This income, in addition to excise taxes levied
on the trade that passes through Westkeep,
provides the Plar of Westkeep an ample
treasury. The Plar is both shrewd and
cunning and he uses his significant wealth to
mitigate the influence of other nations on the
Hold’s northern border. Because of this the
Plar is one of the most hated enemies of both
the Yeomanry and Keoland as he is known to
engineer many plots against both nations to
weaken their control of southern territories.
This can include military operations such as
ambushes and raids but other, more
clandestine, operations are also financed.

## Swampton

Swampton contains the largest population of
Flan that reside in the Sea Principalities.
While not officially considered a part of
Westkeep the men of Swampton operate most
of the barges and watercraft that venture up
and down the Javan and most find
employment with the trade fleets that
disembark from Westkeep.
Although there are roughly 5,000 residents
Swampton is basically a shanty town with
mud streets, rudimentary sewage and no
fortifications. Many of the buildings can only
be reached by water craft.

### The Followers of Insamiar

Swampton is infiltrated by a strange cult, the
Followers of Insamiar, a semi-religious
organization that attend swamp kolbold
fertility ceremonies. The Followers of
Insamiar are banned throughout the Hold of
the Sea Princes but, despite efforts by the
lords of that land, the religion still exists. The
lord of Westkeep is particularly opposed to
the group and will execute anyone found to
be a member of it.
It is thought that the “Gift of Insamiar” used
in the cult’s ritual is a powerfully addictive
potion, which may explain why the cult has so
many members despite the suppression by the
lords of the Hold.

## The Outer Islands, Fairwind, Floatsam and Jetsam

These three large islands, along with a
number of smaller islands, lay off the east
coast of the mainland holdings of the Hold.
These islands were the first places settled by
the original Oeridean clans that would later
become the buccaneers and pirates of the
Hold. These islands are now home to the rich
trade families of the Hold of the Sea Princes
and large manor houses and large estate
grounds cover these islands. Each manor
house is the center of its own small region but
several smaller villages and towns dot the
shores and beaches that face Jerlea Bay. The
sides of the islands that face the Azure Sea
are sparsely populated as fierce storms
punish those shores during the late summer’s
stormy season.

### Rulers of the Outer Islands

Each of the Outer Islands are ruled by a
prince who is chosen to represent the island.
Unlike the Prince of Monmurg or the Prince of
Toli, the Princes of Jetsom, Flotsam and
Fairwind do not gain their offices through
their bloodlines, instead they are elected by
the commodores of each island. However,
once appointed the new Prince will serve in
that position for life.
Each Prince has a voice in the council of five,
the ruling body of the Hold, in addition to
serving as military leader and chief
magistrate to their island.

## Major Natural and Geographic Features

The Hold of the Sea Princes is surrounded by
seas, mountains and marshes that both
isolate it and protect it from invasion. Luckily
the seas of the Flanesse provide a highway to
the Sea Princes that can be used for both
trade and exploration.

### Hool Marshes

Once the swift-flowing Hool River leaves the
foothills of the Hellfurnaces it spreads into a
broad, slow meander across the plains, giving
rise to the waterlogged lands of the Hool
Marshes. The bogs here are dotted with
standing pools of deep, fetid water - home to
numerous lurking horrors. The marshes form
the border between The Yeomanry and the
Hold of the Sea Princes. Outlaws and
renegade humans live in the outer edges,
while lizard men patrol within. Other
humanoids and monsters also inhabit these
swamps.

### Hellfurnaces

The Hellfurnaces range is part of the
Crystalmist Mountain range, but it is active
volcanically in many places, and thus its
different name. As with the northern part of
the chain, the Hellfurnaces are a hive of evil,
being populated with all sorts of monsters,
particularly those which enjoy warmer
temperatures, such as Fire Giants. There are
reported to be whole labyrinths of passages
under this range of mountains, these
underworld highways going for scores,
possibly hundreds, of miles in all directions.

### Azure Sea

The Azure Sea carries a large number of trade
ships plying the coasts from the Hold of the
Sea Princes to Idee. Most ships stick to the
coasts (except when crossing the entrance to
the Sea of Gearnat), since the deep waters
near the middle of the sea harbor large
monsters. Pirate activity is common on the
Azure Sea, with all the coastal nations
offering Letters of Marque to privateers. The
Sea Princes are particularly active in this
field. Several independent pirate groups also
terrorize the sea, mostly based in The Pomarj
or the uninhabited coasts of the Amedio
Jungle or the headland occupied by the
Scarlet Brotherhood. Some organized piracy
operates out of the Olman Islands.
In summer, hurricanes can develop on the sea
and sweep in almost any direction, but most
often strike the southern lands. The Azure
Sea is a known habitat of merfolk, many
living in the coastal waters near civilized
lands. Sahaugin seem to prefer colder waters
and encounters with them are rare.

### Jerlea Bay

Little more than a private sea for the Hold of
the Sea Princes, Jerlea Bay sees little shipping
nowadays. The Sea Princes annually mount
expeditions to the Amedio Jungle for rare
woods and spices using Port Toli as the
jumping off point. The sea is generally calm
and is easily navigable.

### The Dragon Isles

Four leagues north of Jetsom Island lie two
fairly large islands known as the Dragon Isles.
Both are well positioned in the Azure Sea for
forward naval bases and have decent natural
harbors, however, neither the Sea Princes or
Keoland have ever attempted to claim them.
It is said that these islands are used by giant
dragon turtles as nesting grounds and that
any ship going too close to them will invite
attack. Because of this ships usually give
these islands a wide berth. It is rumored that
Prince Jeon II gives a yearly tribute of
treasure to the eldest dragon turtle,
Oceanum Perfide, to keep it from attacking
shipping going to and from Monmurg.

### The Scimitars

Off the west coast of Jetsom Island three
leagues north of the Strait of Monmurg lies
an archipelago known as The Scimitars. These
rocky, windswept islands are of various sizes
and shapes but all are inhospitiable, being
both impossible to harbor at or to build upon.
During the daytime they are easily avoidable
but at night their dark color and low profile
make them nearly invisible against the
silhouette of Jetsom Island. Over the years
many ships making the journey to Monmurg’s
harbors have ended up wrecking upon these
islands, especially ships whose captains are
foolish enough to think they can navigate
them at night.

## Sayings

The people of the Hold have a plethora of
aphorisms, mottos and sayings that belong to
regional dialects. Over the years, however,
some have penetrated the entire lexicon of
the Hold and are universally understood.
These are some of the most popular.

### Toli Oath

The denizens of Port Toli are nefarious
rouges, even when compared to the
buccaneers of the outer islands. They are
known to shift alliances and break promises
to gain even temporary advantage. While this
is considered standard behavior in Port Toli
the rest of the population of the Hold have a
low opinion of someone from that city. Giving
a “Toli oath” means making a promise that is
intended to be broken. Common usages are “I
gave the fool a Toli oath” or “That means as
much as a “Toli oath”.

### Rounding the Horn

The northern portion of the Amedio jungle
ends in a three-hundred-mile-long peninsula
called the Horn of the Amedio. Jerlea Bay lies
to the west of it while the much larger and
stormier Azure Sea lies to the east. The
passage between the tip of the horn and the
islands of the Sea Princes is treacherous,
filled with hidden reefs and tricky currents,
and only the most experienced captains dare
navigate it. Among the sailors of the Hold
“Rounding the Horn” refers to navigating the
dangerous seas between these two bodies of
water instead of sailing through the calmer
waters near the outer island. It has come to
mean “taking the longer and more dangerous
path” among the rest of the Hold’s
population.

### Riding the Dragon

While drug use is generally acceptable in the
Hold one substance is outlawed; “The Blessing
Insamiar”. This strange concoction is used by
a strange cult, The Followers of Insamiar, in
their religious ceremonies. It is obtained by
the cult members from tribes of kobold’s that
inhabit the Hool Marsh around Westkeep,
who use it in their fertilization ceremonies.
Those who take the drug enter a stupor that
can last for days and often describe visions of
riding a great dragon. Many who participate
in this activity die and those that survive are
considered traitors to the Hold. The term
“Riding the Dragon” is what the Followers of
Insamiar call this ceremony but the term has
become synonymous with performing an act
which is both highly dangerous and highly
illegal.

### The Fieraxian’s Share

The phrase “The Feraixian’s Share” refers to
the company of slaves the overlords of Hokar
purchase at the end of the raiding season in
the Slaver’s Square of Port Toli. The overlords
always get the first pick of the “harvest” and
are very particular about which slaves they
take. Among the inhabitants of the hold this
term has come to mean the greater, higher
quality, part of any division of treasure.

### Tie the Knot

Among the sailors of the Hold, “Tie the Knot”
is a test for new recruits asking them to prove
that they can tie the most basic nautical knot
using a length of rope. It has come to mean
“prove yourself” among the rest of the Hold’s
population and is usually contextual.

### Playing with Scimitars

“Playing with Scimitars” originally referred to
a ship’s captain or navigator making the
approach into the Strait of Monmurg at
night, risking a wreck on the island chain
known as The Scimitars. The saying has come
to mean taking an ill-advised risk for no
discernable gain.

## Note — The Amedio Jungle (geography and the slave trade)

The **Amedio Jungle** lies **southeast of the Sea Principalities**, across the Azure Sea. It is the source of the region's slave trade: Toli raiders (and other Sea Prince expeditions) sail there for gold, ivory, and — above all — captives taken from the jungle tribes, the bulk of Toli wealth. Prince Jeon of Monmurg has campaigned for years to end that trade throughout the Principalities, which is precisely the leverage the Duke of Berghof and the Toli use against him — throttling the anti-sahaugin powder and pressing him toward ruin (see `characters/Jeon.md`, `characters/Jamis.md`, `characters/Fairwind.md`; Ch. 43).

**Spelling:** it is the **Amedio** Jungle throughout (as in Ch. 4 and the setting references above) — not "Mediko."

## The title of Plar (reference note)

A **Plar** is a noble title of this world for a ruler set over a foreign — and usually conquered — people, governing them on behalf of the power that appointed him rather than ruling his own folk by hereditary right. A Plar's domain is a **Plardom**. Known Plars:

- The **Plar of Hokar** and the **Plar of Westkeep** — the two Plars who sit in council alongside the five Sea Princes of the Hold (the Plar of Westkeep also holds command of the nation's standing army). Both are Plardoms *within the Principalities*.
- The **Plar of Salinmoor**, **Lord Gloin Baywin** — on the Keoland side of the water, the Duke of Gradsul's appointed overlord of the border province of Salinmoor. This is the Keolandish commander the smuggler Owen Black calls "the power of Baywin" (see `Guide_to_Salinmoor.md`, `characters/Owen_Black.md`).
