# Albashon's Journal

A reference document holding an in-world artifact: the text of the **third book in Albashon's knowledge box** (`Albashons_Knowledge_Box.md`, Item 3). **Final text, approved by the author on 18 October 2026.** Further changes should be logged as author rulings below.

## About the book

- **What it is.** Albashon's personal journal and daybook, kept for years. Most of it is a working ledger: experiments, reagent prices, stores used, potions made and sold, debts and payments. Personal remarks are rare in the early years and become more frequent in the last year.
- **Physical description.** A thick octavo in plain brown goatskin, worn smooth, with a leather tie instead of a clasp. Gatherings of different paper have been sewn in as it filled. The early pages are cramped columns of figures. The later pages loosen into longer prose. The last written page is dated the morning of the **4th of Wealsun**, the day he died.
- **Language.** Suel, in Albashon's working hand. Figures are in ordinary numerals. Some later entries use a private shorthand of Suel abbreviations he also taught Leslie for lab notes, so Leslie reads the journal easily and anyone else reads it slowly. (This gives a kernel of truth to Leslie's claim in `Jude_Paul_and_Leslie_collect_Alchemy_tomes_5th_Wealsun` that only he can translate it.)
- **Dating.** The raid on the shop falls on the **4th of Wealsun, 576 CY** (author ruling: the current year is **576 CY**). Entries are dated on the calendar of the Flanaess: 28-day months (Fireseek, Readying, Coldeven, Planting, Flocktime, Wealsun, Reaping, Goodmonth, Harvester, Patchwall, Ready'reat, Sunsebb) and the festival weeks of Growfest, Richfest, Brewfest and Needfest. **Needfest opens the year**, so the Needfest entries belong to 576 CY, not 575.

**Rendering rules.** Albashon treats the dragon-origin of the Gift as **rumor** (per `Insamiar_the_Black.md`). There are no game terms in Albashon's own words: spells appear only by effect or by the titles he uses in his spellbook (Item 1). Bracketed **[ed.: …]** notes are author-side cross-references, not part of the journal text.

## Part One — The ledger years (selected entries)

*The early years are mostly figures. A representative selection follows. Ellipses mark omitted routine entries.*

### Some five years before the raid (571 CY)

**3rd of Coldeven, 571 CY.**
Bought of Harrud the Chandler: sulfur, 6 lb, 18 sp. Saltpeter, 4 lb, 1 gp 2 sp. He swears the saltpeter is Keoish. It is not. It is wet.
Sold: two draughts of the green restorative to the Hareth woman, 8 sp. One ointment for burns, 3 sp.
Lamp oil running low. The athanor eats charcoal like a bishop eats lamb.

**11th of Coldeven, 571 CY.**
Sublimation of sal ammoniac, third attempt. Clean crystal at last. The fault was the seal on the alembic, not the salt. Note: luting of clay and horse dung holds better than wax past a certain heat. I have known this for forty years and forgot it for three days.

**19th of Planting, 571 CY.**
A young Oeridian came to the door asking to be taught. Merchant's son, too well fed, reads more than is good for him. He had already worked through the Scholars' District shelves and wanted more. I told him the Suel do not teach Oeridians. He asked me why. I did not have a good answer, which annoyed me more than the question did.
Name: Leslie.

**20th of Planting, 571 CY.**
He came back. I gave him a pestle and three pounds of oak gall to grind. He ground them properly. That is more than I can say for most.

**2nd of Flocktime, 571 CY.**
Leslie began on the letters of Ancient Suel today, from the *Opus*. He learns the forms quickly and the meanings slowly, which is the right way round.
Stores: quicksilver, 1 flask remaining. Must write to Gradsul. Price will have risen. It always has.

### Some four years before the raid (572 CY)

**7th of Reaping, 572 CY.**
Potions sold this month: restoratives, 14, at 4 sp: 5 gp 6 sp. Sleep draughts, 9, at 3 sp: 2 gp 7 sp. Ointments, various, 22, at 3 sp: 6 gp 6 sp. Water-breathing elixirs, 2, at 500 gp: 1,000 gp (to the pearl divers off the Harbor District, who haggle like fishwives and pay like lords, half of it in pearls).
Income 1,014 gp 9 sp. Expenses 526 gp 7 sp: 500 gp of it the pearls, ambergris and quicksilver for the divers' elixirs, the rest copper and charcoal. Good month. The divers keep this shop. Without them it would have been a lean one.

**15th of Goodmonth, 572 CY.**
Wrote again to the Harbor Office requesting the smallest sample of the protective powder for study. Refused again, politely. They do not know what it is, and they do not want anyone else to know either. I understand the second part better than the first.

**24th of Harvester, 572 CY.**
Copper vitriol and sulfur in seawater, trial eleven. Nothing of note except a headache and a ruined beaker. Whatever the Hold's powder is, it is not simply these two things put together. The *combination* is the secret. Set the question aside again.

**8th of Sunsebb, 572 CY.**
Leslie copied out his first spells today into a book of his own, the five I chose for him. His hand is clumsy but correct. I gave him the old sharkskin cover from my first book to bind it in. He pretended not to be moved, badly.

### The two years before the raid (574–575 CY)

**4th of Fireseek, 574 CY.**
Stores: charcoal, 2 sacks. Lamp oil, 1 jar. Vinegar, 3 jars. Sulfur, 9 lb. Copper filings, 2 lb. Quicksilver, half a flask (Gradsul again, again).
The cold has brought the Warren to my door. Nine sold coughing syrups at cost, three given away. Leslie disapproves of giving things away. Leslie has never been cold.

**17th of Coldeven, 574 CY.**
A Keoish dealer came through selling a copy of Paracelsus's commentary, "complete." It was not complete. The Office leaves its fingerprints on everything it touches: the cut always falls at the most interesting sentence. Paid 2 gp for it anyway, for the binding.

**4th of Growfest, 574 CY.**
Festival in the streets. Shop closed. Leslie went out, came back at dawn and slept until noon. I distilled in peace. Best day of the year.

**12th of Flocktime, 575 CY.**
Sold: restoratives, 11. Sleep draughts, 15. Odd. The sleep draughts are going to the Warren. They cannot afford them. Two paid in fish. One paid in a brass button. Accepted the button.

## Part Two — The Warren (Wealsun 575 CY to Wealsun 576 CY)

*Beginning about a year before the raid, the entries lengthen and the figures thin out.*

**22nd of Wealsun, 575 CY.**
Mother Tansy, who sells eels at the mouth of the Warren and has cheated me on every eel for six years, did not try to cheat me today. She sat in her doorway with the basket in her lap and let me take two without naming a price. When I put the coin in her hand she looked at it for a long time, as if she did not remember what it was for.
Age comes for us all. She is younger than I am.

**3rd of Richfest, 575 CY.**
The festival in the Warren was quieter than any I remember. No fights, no singing, no one trying to steal the shutters. Leslie said it was peaceful. It was not peaceful. It was empty.

**9th of Reaping, 575 CY.**
Leslie says some of the beggars on the Row of the Gods have stopped begging. They sit with their bowls and do not hold them out. I walked down to see. He is right. They stare at the street the way a cat stares at a wall where there is nothing.
A slowness has come over the Warren. Bad grain is the first thing to suspect. Ergot gives visions, and then worse.

**11th of Reaping, 575 CY.**
Bought three loaves from the Warren baker and two from the bakers on the Row. Examined all five under the glass and by infusion. No ergot. No mold beyond the usual. The bread is bad, but no worse than ever.
Not the grain.

**2nd of Goodmonth, 575 CY.**
Drew water from the Warren cistern at the Crooked Stair and from the public fountain on the Row. Boiled down, precipitated, tested with oak gall and with vitriol. Both are the dirty water of a port city. The Warren's is no dirtier.
Not the water.

**18th of Goodmonth, 575 CY.**
A man has lain in the alley behind the shop for two days. Not dead: breathing slowly, eyes half open, dry lips. Leslie and I lifted him in and gave him broth. He would not swallow until I held his nose. He spoke once, very clearly, without waking. He said he was "riding." Then he laughed, and it was not a pleasant sound.
He left in the night through the side door. The broth bowl was licked clean.

**6th of Harvester, 575 CY.**
The slowness continues and now they do not eat. Faces in the Warren have the look of the starving, but there is food. Cheap fish lies rotting in the baskets. They are not hungry. They do not want anything.
Restoratives sold: 2. Sleep draughts: none. They no longer need help sleeping.

**4th of Brewfest, 575 CY.**
No festival in the Warren at all. I have lived in this street through four bad harvests, a fever year and the blockade. I have never seen the Warren not drink at Brewfest.

**10th of Patchwall, 575 CY.**
Something new. A man I know by sight (a rope-mender, I think) stood at my door for an hour today insisting that his children were not his children, that they had been exchanged in the night and that I, as a Suel and a wizard, must know how to have them changed back. He was calm about it. He spoke the way one speaks of a debt. When Leslie told him to go home, he went home.
Irrationality, then, as well as torpor. A fever of the brain? But there is no fever.

**19th of Patchwall, 575 CY.**
A girl of perhaps nine tried to sell me her shadow. She had worked out a price. A boy with her, when I asked him what was wrong with the grown-ups, said only "the dragon," and then would not say anything else.
I wrote that down. I do not know why.

**26th of Patchwall, 575 CY.**
Leslie was jeered at in the Warren by a crowd of people who, he says, did not seem to know why they were jeering. When he stopped and faced them, they stopped too and wandered off. He made a joke of it at supper. He did not eat much.

**8th of Ready'reat, 575 CY.**
Violence now. In the Warren last night, two men fought over nothing anyone can name. One bit the other's ear off and then sat down beside him in the street, apparently content. Lord Jamis's guardsmen took the bitten man to the Harbor District to be stitched and left the biter where he was. They do not go far into the Warren. I cannot blame them.

**20th of Ready'reat, 575 CY.**
Three more fights. A woman beat her husband with a cooking pot until he stopped moving, then could not tell the guard who he was. He lived. She does not seem to know that either.
Torpor, unreason and rage, in that order, in a widening circle from the deep Warren outward. That is not how a common disease moves. It moves like a supply.

**5th of Sunsebb, 575 CY.**
A woman of the Warren came at Leslie at the shop door with a broken bottle. I put her down with the green breath [ed.: Item 1, Ray of Sickness] before I knew what I was doing. Leslie is unhurt. She was carried off by her neighbors. I sent the burn salve after her.
Bought: gold dust, 1 oz, 25 gp, for sealing the box and the inner door. I hate to part with gold. I always have, and the year has given me less reason than ever to like it: the pearl divers have bought nothing since Harvester, and without them the shop lives on silver. I would hate more to need it and not have it.
Have set the silver thread [ed.: Item 1, Alarm] on the front door every night from now on.

**14th of Sunsebb, 575 CY.**
Masked strangers in the Warren. Leslie saw two, and Mother Tansy's grandson says there are more. Plain dark masks, hoods up, walking openly in the lanes after dark as if they owned them. No one in the Warren will say who they are. They say "the masked ones" the way children speak of the dead.

**2nd of Needfest, 576 CY.**
Fire in the deep Warren last night, set deliberately, the guard says, by a man who then sat down to watch it. Eleven dead. The guard has had the bodies laid in the old fish-market. It is a mercy the fire did not spread. The deep Warren is old timber and tarred rope, wall against wall, and the wind was off the land. Had it turned, I would be writing of the whole street.

**3rd of Needfest, 576 CY.**
Paid the brother of one of the dead 4 sp for leave to examine the body and promised to return it whole for burial. Leslie refused to help. Afterward he helped.
The man died of the fire, but he was dying before it. Wasted muscle. An enlarged, mottled liver. **Black staining in the corners of the eyes and along the tear ducts**, like old ink that has soaked into the flesh. The same black in the lining of the stomach. Not soot: soot does not go there.
Something black and taken by mouth, over months.
Salt and copper on the body afterward [ed.: Item 1, Gentle Repose]. Returned it to the brother as promised.

**9th of Fireseek, 576 CY.**
I have been reading what I have on the marsh. Westkeep's law against the Followers of Insamiar, the reports of the kobold rites, the black draught that brings a sleep of visions the Followers call "riding the dragon." *Riding.* The man in the alley.
But the marsh draught is a rite, taken by the willing a sip at a time, and it gives hours of stupor, not months of ruin. What is in the Warren is the same draught, I would wager, given differently.

**20th of Fireseek, 576 CY.**
I have the name now. A rope-mender's wife (the rope-mender of the children) told Leslie that the masked ones bring "the Gift." I asked her what Gift. She smiled and said, "Insamiar's." Then she asked whether I had any.
**The Gift of Insamiar.** The marsh folk say it is drawn from the dragon itself. That is the sort of thing marsh folk say. Whatever it is, it is a poison, given to people who did not ask for it, in quantities large enough to break them.
I do not know how it reaches them, in drink or food or by the masked ones' own hands. I do not know why. A slow poison given to the poorest street in Monmurg makes no sense as a business. It makes sense only as a trial.

**1st of Readying, 576 CY.**
Wrote to no one. Who would I write to? The Prince's officers will hear "the Warren is mad" and answer "the Warren was always mad." Lord Jamis's men will not walk past the Crooked Stair. I am an old foreigner who sells sleep draughts.
If I cannot cure it, I can hold it off. This week I compounded a **white draught**: milk of poppy, willow, chalk to settle the gut, and a binder of my own to slow the whole into the blood. It does not touch the poison. It eases the pain and the rage and slows the slide. A full jar keeps the dragon's breath away for a month, if the taker is not too far gone.
Cost to make, per jar: near 30 gp, most of it the poppy. Priced it at 50. The Warren cannot pay that. The Warren will not be asked to.
Stores: sulfur 4 lb, copper 1 lb, quicksilver none. Sold nothing this week. Spent the week writing this and at the bench.

**15th of Readying, 576 CY. Lord Phranck.**
Visitors in the afternoon. Three men, with the door standing open as I leave it in fair weather.
A **guide**: a hard, quiet man of the Harbor with the look of the old privateer crews. I have seen him about the district. He knew my alley, which few do, and stood where he could see the street.
A **gentleman of Keoland**: well dressed, well spoken, a wizard by his bearing and by the way he came through my door. He stopped on the threshold and breathed in the shop the way a vintner breathes in a cellar, and I watched him sort it: the herbs, the sulfur, the metal fumes. He knew a laboratory when he smelled one. The guide called him "my lord," and once, at the door, "Lord Phranck."
And a **younger man**, the lord's companion. Even through the front window I could see it on him: the faint grey at the corners of the eyes, the slowness of the pupil in the light, the stillness in the hands. Not far gone, but started. **The shadow of the Gift was on him.** Where did he take it? Not in the Warren. He had the look of a man who has never set foot in it.
I asked the young one plainly whether he had come for a treatment. The lord began to say he wished to buy some formulas "for a friend" and let the sentence die in the air.
Then I felt it: a light touch at the edge of my thoughts, like a hand trying a latch in the dark. A wizard reading my mind, in my own shop, without so much as a by-your-leave. My old master taught me to think in signs and not words when strangers are near, and it is a habit I have kept for sixty years. So I let him read sigils. Mercury, salt, sulfur, the green lion, the serpent eating its tail. I hope he enjoyed them.
He said they would like to see "the cure." I told him there is no cure, only treatment, and brought out the white draught.
Then he asked me, "What of the Red Dust? Do you have any of that?"
I nearly dropped the jar. **The Stone.** *Lapis philosophorum.* A man of Gradsul standing in my shop asking for the red stone as if it were a jar of pickled herring. I told him the truth, because the touch was still at my thoughts and he would have known a lie: yes, that would be a cure, but only the Duke of Berghof's house would know of such a thing, and the Stone has not been heard of in centuries. He seemed to have expected that answer. I would give a good deal to know where he learned the name, and from whom.
He asked the price of the treatment. I said 50 gold for the jar, a month's keeping. He offered 15 for a quarter of it, a week's worth, as though he were driving a bargain. A quarter of 50 is twelve and a half. I took his 15, poured the quarter into a vial and said nothing. The young man also bought a true healing draught, 50 gp, without haggling. Then they left, and the lord seemed well pleased with his afternoon.
They did not say who sent them to me, and I did not ask. The guide knew the way. Someone told the guide.
A strange man, this Lord Phranck. His mind is like a good book left out in the rain: much of it legible, some of it brilliant, and here and there a page simply missing. He began sentences and abandoned them. He knew the name of the Great Work's last fruit and did not seem to know the first thing about the Work. I do not know what has been torn out of him, or who did it.
Ledger: Sold, white draught, one quarter jar, 15 gp. Sold, healing draught (true), 50 gp. Best afternoon's trade this year, and I would give it all back to know what that boy has been drinking.

**16th of Readying, 576 CY.**
Could not sleep. The Red Dust. Went back through the *Opus* for anything Ujor wrote on the Stone as a remedy, and on the dragon of the Hool. He made his orb against Insamiar six hundred years ago, so he knew the marsh. There is one passage near the end that the Office's knives left alone, I think because they did not understand it any better than I do. Ujor writes of **"the willing gift"** and does not explain it.
Wrote in the *Opus*: *I do not yet understand what he means by the willing gift.*
And the young man: started on the Gift, and not from the Warren. So it is not only the Warren.

**28th of Readying, 576 CY.**
If I cannot stop it, I can ease it. Began giving the white draught, in small measures, to the worst in the Warren through Mother Tansy, who still knows who everyone is even if she no longer always knows the day. She takes the vials and does not ask. Six patients. No charge. The Keoish lord's 15 gold has paid for a week of poppy, which is some justice.
Stores: poppy, 2 flasks. More from the Harbor market, at a robber's price.

**9th of Coldeven, 576 CY.**
Fourteen patients. The white draught eases the rage a little and the pain a great deal. I had not known there was pain. They do not say so until it is gone.
A man died tonight with his hand in mine. Before he died I gave him what I could of myself [ed.: Item 1, Life Transference — see the marks in the margin there]. It bought him an hour and he was clear for all of it. He asked for his mother. Then he asked what day it was. Then he went.
Cost to me: a day in bed. Leslie fussed. I let him.

**3rd of Growfest, 576 CY.**
Festival. The Warren did not notice.
Patients: 22. Deaths since Readying: 5. The white draught is not a cure. Nothing I have is a cure. The Keoish lord asked for the Red Dust as if I kept it in a drawer. I wish I did.

**12th of Planting, 576 CY.**
The masked ones have noticed me. Twice this week Leslie saw a hooded figure standing at the mouth of the alley across from the shop, watching the door. When he looked back it was gone.
Tansy says the masked ones asked her who sends the little bottles. She told them she did not know. She told me she did not know whether she had told them the truth. I believe her about the second part.

**26th of Planting, 576 CY.**
Began a systematic trial: blackened residue scraped from the eyes of the dead (three subjects, with family leave, salt and copper again), dissolved, precipitated, set against my reagents.
Result: an organic poison, alkaloid in character, with a mineral carrier I do not recognize. It acts slowly at small doses and fast at large ones. In the larger quantities it would kill outright, with black from the eyes.
I have not seen this in any book. I have now seen it in five dead men.

**8th of Flocktime, 576 CY.**
Letter by sea from Gradsul, months late. **Edium Nicond is dead.** The best transmuter in Keoland and, I am vain enough to say, the only one living whose work I was afraid of. The letter does not say how. It does say that his student, the gnome **Seebo Beren**, has gone quiet, and that the gnome is known to **an Inquisitor of the Office of Forbidden Magic named Phranck**. I heard of Beren once, years ago, from Zafar Azane, who thinks highly of him. Zafar is a dangerous man to know and a useful one. When the Office decides that a thing is newly forbidden, he is the one who decides it, and I would rather hear it from him over a cup of wine than from an Inquisitor at my door. If anyone could help a young prince with the Hold's powder now, it would be him.
So my Keoish lord of the Red Dust was an Inquisitor. I spent a lifetime keeping out of that Office's way, and it came into my shop, read my thoughts and haggled itself upward.
Note to myself: the Office cut Ujor's book to pieces because a man used it to make medicine. I will keep my medicine out of its sight.
There is no alchemist better than me left alive now. That is not a boast. It is a bad thing to be true.

**22nd of Flocktime, 576 CY.**
The fleet sallied again this week and came back two ships short. Fog out of a clear sky, then the storm, on the very hour they cleared the harbor mouth, as it has been every time. I have watched these storms from the roof for months now. They come only when our ships sail. They rise against the wind, they sit low and heavy on the water, and they go nowhere, the way the old sailors describe the weather of the turtle dragons. No sky makes weather like that. If it is the turtle dragons, then they are breaking every sally, and only the sallies.
Turtle dragons do not come when called. A hundred and fifty years of tribute has bought this city their patience, never their obedience. No prince has ever whistled one up like a dog.
Unless the one calling has a Dragon Orb.
I wrote that and sat looking at it until the candle guttered. If such a thing has come back into the world, I can guess whose hand it is in. The storms serve no one but the Toli, and Port Toli keeps the old Suel shelves.
Poppy up again by a third. Everything that comes by sea is up again.

**1st of Wealsun, 576 CY.**
Thirty-one patients. The poppy has run out twice. Leslie has begun stealing it back for me from the Harbor market by paying fair prices, which he considers stealing.
Something in Ujor's passage is coming clear. Writing it down before I lose it.

**2nd of Wealsun, 576 CY.** *(day; the explanation of "the willing gift"; author-approved)*
I think I understand Ujor at last, or the beginning of him.
The Gift is not one thing to every body. Ujor's **"willing gift"** is the draught taken **willingly**, in the rite, under the eye of those who know the dose, by a mind that opens itself to it. To such a taker, he says (if I read his riddle rightly), it is not a poison but a **passage**. It changes the body slowly toward the shape of the scaled ones, and the mind toward a terrible clarity that its takers call wisdom. That is how the dragon's servants are *made*.
The **unwilling** gift is what has been done to the Warren. The same substance, poured into people who did not choose it, did not know it, and fight it without knowing they fight. In them it does not open a passage. It breaks the walls. First the will goes (torpor), then reason (the children who are not their children), then the last restraint (the bitten ear, the cooking pot, the fire). Then the body goes.
The masked ones walk openly because they have been changed and are proud of it. I would bet my shop that under those masks are the **willing**. And the Warren is their trial: a test of the dose, the method and the speed at which a street, and then a city, can be undone.
I cannot yet prove one word of this. But it would explain why the Warren and the black sickness of Salinmoor look alike, why Phranck's Sons of Olan need not be the hand behind either, and why the dead weep black.
Some are more vulnerable than others. Two brothers in the same house, the same food, the same water: one is lost, one is only slow. It is in the blood, as some families take fevers worse.

**2nd of Wealsun, 576 CY.** *(night)*
A customer after dark. An elf, Jude by name. He wants a still, for spirits. Sold him the fittings, to be delivered within the week, and let him copy three recipes for simple spirits from the shop copy of the *Opus*, the one I bought in Port Toli and keep on the counter for customers. Not mine. Mine stays where it is. He reads Suel.
His eyes. He has been molded by a transmuter, and not gently. The Scarlet Brotherhood, he says, and I believe him. The work has held. Whoever did it knows more of the Changing of the Shape than I do. He says he means to kill every one of them. I told him I understood. I did.
He asked nothing about Lord Phranck, which is how I know he came about Lord Phranck.
One of Lord Jamis's watchers was at the corner again when he left. Perhaps the elf is Jamis's too. That is either very good or very bad.
Ledger: still fittings, sold. Paper and quill, 2 sp.

**3rd of Wealsun, 576 CY.**
A messenger from the palace: the Prince's nephew, the masked young man they call Paul Revero, wishes to consult me tomorrow about the **protective powder**, the Hold's powder that keeps the sea-devils off. The elf will bring him.
Forty years I have asked the Harbor Office for a grain of it. Now the palace comes to me.
Leslie to sweep the front. Bought: sulfur, 3 lb. Copper filings, 1 lb. Chalk, 1 box, and a slate. If the boy's notes are as I expect, I will need to teach him from the beginning.

**4th of Wealsun, 576 CY.** *(last entry, morning)*
Slept badly. The silver thread rang twice in the night. Both times, no one at the door.
Gift of Insamiar, tincture of poppy, my slow theory: I must tell them all of it today, the boy and the elf. If Lord Jamis has an interest, let it be in this.
Tansy did not come for the bottles this morning.

*(The rest of the page is blank.)*

## Author notes

### Timeline built into the journal
- **First signs:** 22nd of Wealsun, 575 CY.
- **Torpor:** Wealsun to Harvester (bread and water ruled out in Reaping and Goodmonth).
- **Irrationality:** Patchwall.
- **Violence:** Ready'reat 575 to Needfest 576. The fire and the post-mortem fall in Needfest 576.
- **Identification as the Gift of Insamiar:** 9th and 20th of Fireseek.
- **Lord Phranck's visit:** 15th of Readying, 576 CY (as specified).
- **White draught compounded:** 1st of Readying (sold to Phranck's party on the 15th). **Free treatment in the Warren begins:** 28th of Readying (fits `Jude_and_Paul_interrogation_cont_5th_Wealsun`: he was "giving those affected by the gift some substance that helped take away their pain").
- **Edium Nicond's death reported:** 8th of Flocktime (fits Albashon's dying words in `Jude_Leslie_escape_scarlet_brotherhood_4th_Wealsun`).
- **The Dragon Orb guess:** 22nd of Flocktime, 576 CY, after a failed sally.
- **Jude's visit:** the night of the 2nd of Wealsun, 576 CY, alone (`Jude_2nd_Wealsun`; Book One Ch. 4).
- **Last entry:** the morning of the 4th of Wealsun, 576 CY.

### Canon ties used
- **The 15th of Readying visit follows the author's account of the scene** (Phranck, with Gouge as guide and Gedeon as companion): Phranck's surprise at the shop's smells; Albashon greeting them; Albashon seeing the shadow of the Gift on Gedeon and offering treatment; Phranck's Detect Thoughts finding only alchemical symbols; "There is no cure, only treatment"; the jar of white liquid; Phranck asking for the Red Dust and Albashon's answer (*Lapis Philosophorum*, only the Duke of Berghof would know, unheard of in centuries); 50 gp a month, Phranck buying a quarter for 15 gp; Gedeon buying a healing potion for 50 gp. In the journal, Albashon does not know the guide's or the companion's names, and he does not learn that Phranck is an Inquisitor until the Gradsul letter (8th of Flocktime).
- Albashon **notices** the mind-reading and deliberately thinks in symbols, a discipline from his own master. That is why Phranck saw sigils instead of thoughts.

### Author rulings (settled)
1. **The year is 576 CY.** The raid is on the 4th of Wealsun, 576 CY.
2. **The "willing gift" explanation (2nd of Wealsun, 576 CY) is canon.** Taken willingly in the rite, the Gift is a *passage*: it slowly changes the body toward the scaled ones and the mind toward a terrible "clarity." This is how the masked Brotherhood agents (the Yuan-ti) are made. Forced on the unknowing, the same substance *breaks* them: will, then reason, then restraint, then the body. Susceptibility varies by blood. This fits the prisoner's "insanity or complete clarity" and his claim that Jude is susceptible.
3. **How the Gift reaches the Warren is a mystery.** Albashon never learns it, and the journal must not resolve it.
4. **Who sent Phranck to Albashon is a mystery.** Albashon only knows that the guide knew the way.
5. **The minor characters are kept** (added to `characters/Minor_characters.md`).
6. **Edium Nicond's student is Seebo Beren, a gnome alchemist.**
7. **The companion's name is spelled Gedeon** (not "Gideon").
8. **Gouge's timing.** On the 15th of Readying, 576 CY, Gouge guided Phranck's party under a **short-term hire**. This was separate from, and earlier than, his bodyguard hire by Gedeon on the 5th of Planting (`characters/Gouge.md`).
9. **The Gift and the marsh Blessing are the same substance** (`Insamiar_the_Black.md`). Albashon's 9th of Fireseek entry guesses as much.
10. **Gedeon was affected in Salinmoor without ever taking the Gift**, through exposure to people who had. He does not know when it happened. Albashon only sees that the young man was not dosed in the Warren, and the journal does not explain further. How exposure passes it on is unspecified, and it does not solve how the Gift reaches the Warren.
11. **Jude's first visit in the journal is the night of the 2nd of Wealsun, 576 CY.** He comes alone (Horace has already left Ferd's, per Book One Ch. 4), buys still fittings and copies three spirit recipes. He does not ask about Phranck on this visit.
12. **The shop copy of the *Opus*.** The copy Albashon shows customers is a second, unannotated copy he bought in Port Toli some twenty years ago (`Jude_2nd_Wealsun`). His own annotated copy (Knowledge Box Item 2) is a different book and stays hidden.
13. **Albashon and Zafar Azane.** Albashon knows Zafar Azane, who runs the Office of Forbidden Magic (`magic_system.md`), and treats him as a dangerous associate: someone he keeps on terms with because Zafar tells him which way the Office's policy is moving. This is how Albashon kept out of the Office's way, not a contradiction of it.
14. **Shop prices.** Green restoratives 4 sp, sleep draughts 3 sp, ointments 3 sp (all mundane). The true healing draught 50 gp and the divers' water-breathing elixir 500 gp, per the recipe scrolls (Knowledge Box Item 4). The 7th of Reaping, 572 CY ledger uses these prices.
15. **Albashon is a miser.** He hates parting with gold, always. He is not mean with his work: he gives syrups and the White Draught away to the Warren. It is coin he grudges, never cures.
16. **The divers' trade dried up** after Harvester 575. That is why the shop lives on silver by the time of the 5th of Sunsebb, 575 entry. The cause is not stated.
17. **The Dragon Orb guess (22nd of Flocktime, 576 CY).** Albashon deduces from the storms themselves, not from any rumor, that they are the weather of the turtle dragons and that the dragons are being made to break Monmurg's sallies. He wonders whether a Dragon Orb is being used and guesses the Toli hold it. Cain Toli is not named. He does not explain what a Dragon Orb is, and he does not connect it to Ujor or to Insamiar. This keeps the in-world mystery intact (`The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`, register note). The entry falls a few months after Cain Toli recovered the orb.
