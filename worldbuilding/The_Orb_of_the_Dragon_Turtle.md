# The Orb of the Dragon Turtle

A reference document, not an in-world artifact — this is the continuity ledger for the artifact at the center of the turtle-dragon threat: the **Orb of the Dragon Turtle**, the relic by which Cain Toli commands Oceanum Perfide against Monmurg. It resolves the "Toli artifact" open thread carried in `magic_system.md`. Compiled from author-supplied canon (this discussion) and cross-checked against `worldbuilding/magic_system.md`, `worldbuilding/The_Dragon_Isles.md`, `worldbuilding/The_Anti-Sahaugin_Powder.md`, `The_Sea_Principalities.md`, and manuscript Book One Ch. 2–3, 12–13, 21, 31 and Book Two Ch. 1–3.

**Register / spoiler note.** In-world, this is still a mystery the characters are actively chasing — Jamis has charged Jude and Paul to "research its nature, and how they're using it against the dragons" (Book 2 Ch. 3); no one in Monmurg yet knows it is an Orb of Dragonkind. Author-side we now know exactly what it is. On the page, keep rendering it by **effect** — the wrong fog, the vast shape in the murk, Cain Toli's inexplicable demands and errands — never as an exposed stat block. The mechanical appendix at the foot of this file is for the writer's desk only, per the standing craft rule (`magic_system.md`, "Craft Note").

## What It Is (in-world)

The Orb of the Dragon Turtle is a roughly spherical stone, eight to twelve inches across, that appears fragile but shrugs off nearly all harm — even a dragon's own bite or breath. Like the Orbs of Dragonkind it was modeled on, it holds the bound **essence** of the creature it commands (here, the dragon turtle), and that essence has a will of its own: **not especially evil, but avaricious, willful, and stubborn.** It resents being spent on trivial errands and will often make its bearer perform strange, arbitrary tasks before it consents to act. This is the lever that turns the artifact into character: an outsider watching Cain Toli sees a man given to unaccountable demands and errands, appeasing something no one else can see. Keep that as the on-page tell — the *behavior* of the orb-bearer, not the orb.

Crucially, it commands **one dragon turtle at a time.** Cain Toli has bound it to the largest and most powerful of the Dragon Isles' brood — **Oceanum Perfide**, the very eldest, the one Monmurg has paid tribute to for a hundred and fifty years (`The_Dragon_Isles.md`; Ch. 3). This is what resolves the Book One / Book Two wording: Book One's "the greatest of them" is exact; Book Two's looser "his dragons" is vernacular for the single dragon he wields.

**What the orb does, and what the dragon does.** The distinction matters for staging. The orb's own weather working is modest — it can raise a bank of fog. The **storms** that actually break Monmurg's sallies "with suspicious punctuality" (Ch. 2–3) are **the dragon's own power**, not the orb's: a turtle dragon of Oceanum Perfide's age can call true fog and storm at sea as a matter of nature, and the orb's fog is only a crude, artificial echo of that gift. So the orb *calls and incites*; the weather that drowns the fleet is the living leviathan loosed. (See `The_Dragon_Isles.md`, "The Turtle Dragons," where this storm-raising is now recorded as the creature's own trait.)

## The Orbs of Dragonkind (the parent tradition)

Ages past, the **Suel Empire** and the **Fieraxian kings** waged a terrible war on dragonkind. **Empress Landal** of the Suel and the **king of Firiax**, both mighty wizards, came together to forge **five Orbs of Dragonkind** — one for each of the chromatic breeds (red, blue, green, black, white). With them the Suel mages *lured* dragons in, *enslaved* those that yielded and *destroyed* those that would not, and drove the war to a victorious end.

- Each orb holds the **essence** of the dragon-type it was made to attract, and is said to be made from a **dragon's egg** — a sphere of eight to twelve inches. When worked, an orb takes on a physical echo of its breed (the Red grows hot, the White turns cold, and so on).
- Over centuries some were destroyed and the rest faded into legend. The survivors' magic **warped and strengthened with age, as dragons do.** They still *call* dragons, and are rumored to grant a measure of *control* over them. The craft of making them was lost long ago.
- Every orb carries a **resentful presence** that fights being used and can **dominate** a weak-willed wielder, enslaving them to work the dragon-essence's ends. Their use is discouraged for all but the strongest-willed mages.

**The deeper truth.** The orbs are, in fact, the **first Transmutation Stones ever made** — the craft of **Illustrium Arcanum**, the first Transmutor and **the greatest king of Firiax** (he is that "king of Firiax" of the popular account; the two are one figure). Their making resembles an ordinary Transmutation Stone but is far more potent, achievable only by a high-level Transmutor with the deepest giant-alchemical craft.

**Why this belongs to the Toli.** Two threads of established canon meet here. The Suel Empire's surviving arcana are exactly what the **Arcane Order of Port Toli** is rumored to hoard (`magic_system.md`; `The_Sea_Principalities.md`), and Cain Toli is of pure Suel descent — a Suel-forged dragon-relic in Suel hands. And the **Fieraxians** are already in the ledger: the mountain people of the Duke of Berghof's southwestern border sometimes wrongly credited with the anti-sahaugin powder (which was in fact **invented and mass-produced by the Duke of Berghof**, not the Fieraxians) (`The_Anti-Sahaugin_Powder.md`; `magic_system.md`), and the namesake of the gazetteer's "Fieraxian's Share." They are one people with the Orb-forging Fieraxian kings of Firiax, the once-appealing idea that **the powder and the Orbs of Dragonkind are two legacies of one ancient alchemist-culture is now set aside.** The Orbs are Suel/Fieraxian, but the powder is a **Duke of Berghof invention**; the Fieraxian–powder link is only a popular misattribution. What ties the sahaugin war, the dragon war, and the Toli/Berghof present together is the plot, not a shared ancient maker. *Spelling (canon):* the realm is **Firiax**; the people and the adjective are **Fieraxian**. The variant spellings that were scattered across the gazetteer, `magic_system.md`, and `The_Anti-Sahaugin_Powder.md` have been standardized to **Fieraxian**.

## Its Maker — Ujor Udias

Ujor Udias is **already established canon**, and this deepens him rather than inventing anyone. He is the transmuter whose **Magnum Opus** Theophrastus Paracelsus deciphered and used to treat Keoland nobles — its "bread and wine" being euphemisms for **flesh and blood** — the scandal that led Keoland to found the **Office of Forbidden Magic** (`magic_system.md`). His is also the **tome Jude has read**, which (Jude notes) contains no homunculus recipe at all (Ch. 12). So the man behind Cain Toli's orb is the same shadowy author whose work an entire Keoish office exists to suppress.

**Ujor Udias and the name "Acerak."** In-world, Toli sources — and the necromancer Ixid of the Sons of Olan — credit the orb to "the Lich Acerak." They are imprecise rather than wrong: the famed lich **Acerak** vanished hundreds of years before Ujor Udias lived, and Ujor — himself grown into a lich across an unnaturally long life — **took (inherited) the vanished lich's name.** So the "Acerak" who made the Orb of the Dragon Turtle is Ujor Udias under that borrowed name; the original Acerak of the Dreadwood and the Dread March (`Guide_to_the_Hool_Marshes.md`) is the earlier, separate bearer of it.

Ujor built the Orb of the Dragon Turtle **in imitation of the Orbs of Dragonkind**, working only from his own **Spiritual Alchemy** and secondhand arcane descriptions of the Dragon Orbs — he was **not a true student of Giant Alchemy**. The result is **imperfect but still powerful**, and it behaves differently from a true Orb in ways that matter (below): it needs no attunement, it cannot be attuned, and it is harder to coax than a true orb — but it also cannot enslave its bearer outright, only badger and bargain.

## Origin & Purpose — Insamiar the Black

Ujor made the orb to **protect the lizardfolk of the Hool Marshes from Insamiar, the Black** — an ancient **black dragon** of the marshes. (Black dragons are swamp-dwellers, and "the Black" answers to the black-dragon Orb of that breed.) The protection is **spiritual, not martial.** A turtle-dragon orb was the right instrument precisely because it *calls dragon turtles* — and the **physical presence of the turtle dragons gives the marsh's lizard men a real, natural god to worship.** So long as the great turtles are actually there in the marsh's tidal waters and river-mouths, the lizard men have a living deity of their own, and are anchored against Insamiar. **Remove that presence and the anchor is gone:** with no turtle dragons before them, the lizard men are pulled by the draw of the **Black Kobolds — Insamiar's thralls** — and drift into the black dragon's worship. The orb's whole purpose was to keep the turtles present enough to hold the marsh's faith.

This reframes the *Call Dragon Turtle* power (below): it was never only a summons for war — in Ujor's design it was the means of *keeping a god in front of the faithful.* In Cain Toli's hands that original, protective purpose has been abandoned entirely; he uses the orb to wield one turtle dragon as a weapon at sea, which by the same logic **leaves the Hool Marsh lizard men spiritually undefended** — a slow consequence with real plot potential.

**The cult connection (now wired in).** The black dragon's name is **Insamiar** — the same name its worshippers venerate it under, so the gazetteer's existing entries all point back to this one ancient being. Full ledger in `Insamiar_the_Black.md`; in brief:

- The **Followers of Insamiar** — the banned, all-human Hool Marsh cult (`The_Sea_Principalities.md`, "Swampton") — take part in the *kobolds'* rites, which are led by the kobold **All-Watchers**. This kobold-led human–humanoid faith is a genuine anomaly.
- The **Black Kobolds** are **Insamiar's thralls** — the agents of its pull on the lizard men. These are the same kobold tribes the gazetteer places in the Hool Marsh around Westkeep, who brew the potion and run the fertility ceremonies (`The_Sea_Principalities.md`, "Swampton" and "Riding the Dragon").
- The **Blessing of Insamiar** is the addictive black potion of the monthly egg-rite; the **"gift"** is the offering (a polished stone or silver Insamiar-image) a worshipper trades for a sip of it. Drinkers fall into an hours-long stupor and see visions of Insamiar — the rite the faithful call **"Riding the Dragon."**
- So the marsh holds two competing faiths pulling at the lizard men: the **living, natural god** (the turtle dragons, while present) and the **thrall-cult of Insamiar** (the Black Kobolds and their drug, when the turtles are gone).

A standalone entry for **Insamiar the Black** (a live or dormant black-dragon threat in the Hool Marshes) is a natural next file if you want to develop it.

## How Cain Toli Came By It — Turtle Island and the Appleyard Bargain

The literal path by which a Suel-forged relic reached Suel hands, and the events it has already disturbed off the Salinmoor coast. It came to Cain Toli **only a few months ago** — consistent with his Salinmoor/Saltmarsh operations "about two months back" (`characters/Owen_Black.md`).

- **Where it lay: Turtle Island.** After making the orb, **Ujor Udias sealed it away nearly 600 years ago inside the ancient shell of a dead turtle dragon** — the small island off the Salinmoor coast, near Appleyard, known today as **Turtle Island (also called Lizard Island)** (`Guide_to_Salinmoor.md`). A dead turtle's shell as the reliquary for a turtle-dragon orb is fitting, and it sat there, its location lost, for centuries — the Dragon Turtle essence within it **growing in strength across the roughly six hundred years since**.
- **The tip-off: the necromancer Ixid.** Cain learned the orb's location from **Ixid, a necromancer of the Sons of Olan** — the necromantic cult (their madness-inducing "black sickness") that Lord Phranck was sent south into Salinmoor to hunt (Ch. 3). Ixid wanted access to the **abandoned, ward-protected keep at Appleyard**; Cain could **lower the ward**, and in exchange **Ixid gave him the orb's location on Turtle Island.** A clean villain's bargain — necromancer and Toli adventurer each trading the one thing the other could not get alone.
- **The retrieval — and the Ch. 44 mystery, solved.** Cain's forces took the orb from Turtle Island, which explains the **hobgoblins Owen Black saw massing and regrouping on Turtle Island** in the story present, "for reasons not yet clear" (Ch. 44; `Guide_to_Salinmoor.md`). They are Cain's hobgoblins — the same stock that crews the *Sea Ghost* and took the Helm — securing and working the site. *(Open: why they are still there now, months after the orb was taken — holding the site, extracting more, or something the full-moon Call is drawing to the shell.)*
- **The fallout in Salinmoor — and a Toli–Keoland clash.** The Appleyard keep was long **abandoned behind a powerful ward**; once **Cain broke the ward, he began investigating the keep** — until **Lord Gloin Baywin, the Plar of Salinmoor, drove Cain Toli off and retook it for Keoland.** It is the same shell-keep Baywin holds in the story present, where he later broke Owen Black's arm (Ch. 44). So the abandoned warded keep and Baywin's shell-keep are one structure at two moments: **Cain opened it, Baywin seized it.** That direct clash between a Toli adventurer and Keoland's Salinmoor overlord is worth keeping in view in the larger Toli/Gradsul/Keoland politics.
- **He has not mastered it.** Cain **has not fully figured out how to use the orb, and is struggling against the will of the great dragon turtle** (Oceanum Perfide). This fits the artifact's willful, appeasement-demanding nature exactly: his control is partial and contested, the errands and demands still half-understood — a live vulnerability the protagonists could turn, and a reason the fog-and-storm attacks may be inconsistent or costly for him rather than clean.

## How Cain Toli Uses It (the present of the story)

**The unified scheme — dragon and powder are one plot.** Cain Toli uses the turtle dragon to **isolate Monmurg** (the blockade Keoland could not otherwise hold) *and* to **seal off the remaining anti-sahaugin powder stores** — he holds the mid-bay store at **Pocra Sententia** and has stopped its shipments (`The_Anti-Sahaugin_Powder.md`). The aim is to leave Monmurg cut off and unable to renew its warding, **defenseless against the sahaugin.** Strangling the city's trade and denying it the powder are the same design; the shark-folk are meant to finish what the blockade begins.

- **He commands Oceanum Perfide with it** — the single greatest dragon turtle — and turns Monmurg's bought peace into a weapon: fog and storm rise whenever the fleet sails, ships are lost, and Keoland's blockade holds only because of it (Ch. 3; Book 2 Ch. 3).
- **The orb is mobile and travels with the bearer.** Its fog is centered on the orb, and the dragon must be near to be incited — so the threat is wherever Cain and the orb are, most likely aboard the **Sea Ghost** (his ~300-foot slaving hull, still at large; `The_Dragon_Isles.md`, `Helm_Island.md`), not a fixed curse hanging over the Dragon Isles. This matches "ships come and go from the fort, and the storms rise when the fleet sallies" (Ch. 2).
- **No attunement, but a hard master.** Anyone may pick it up and try it — but each use demands real **force of personality**, and it must be **appeased** (its errands and demands met) to work smoothly. A wielder who keeps the essence content finds it far more pliable; one who spends it on trifles finds it balky. Cain Toli is thus not necessarily a mage — his hold on the orb is a matter of **will**, not wizardry, which fits a Toli adventurer-princeling rather than an Arcane Order scholar.
- **The full-moon wildcard.** The orb **calls dragon turtles to itself every full moon**, whether or not the bearer wills it. This is a planted, recurring beat: on the full moon the orb's location may draw *more* dragon turtles toward it (or betray where it is), a monthly pressure Cain must manage and a lever the protagonists could exploit once they understand it.

- **The orb's own agenda.** Its avaricious, willful essence wants things; those wants "will be compatible with the aspects of the dragon it is derived from." What the dragon-turtle essence specifically covets (and the erratic tasks it sets Cain) is open to define.
- **Ixid the Son of Olan.** *Established:* Ixid is a necromancer of the **Sons of Olan** (Ch. 3), and gave Cain the orb's location for the lowering of the Appleyard ward. Still open: what he wanted inside the keep, his rank within the cult, and whether the black sickness thread and the orb thread converge later.

## Appendix — Game Mechanics (author reference only; NOT for the prose)

Recorded verbatim from canon so the effects stay consistent. Per the craft rule, none of this — DCs, charges, saves, damage dice — appears in the narrative; it is rendered by effect only.

### The Orb of the Dragon Turtle (Cain Toli's artifact)

- **Attunement:** none required, and the orb **will not** attune to a user. It resists use but grows pliable when its demands are met and it is "happy."
- **Activation:** using any power costs 1 action and a **Charisma ability check, DC 20**, to convince the orb to act. If all the orb's demands have been met in the last 24 hours, the check is made **with advantage**. A failed check carries **no penalty** (you simply don't get the effect).
- **Charges:** 7; regains **1d4 + 3** at dawn.
- **Powers:**
  1. **Call Dragon Turtle (1 charge).** Telepathic call radiating 400 miles in all directions. Dragon turtles in range are compelled to come; on a failed Charisma save vs. the orb's DC they travel the most direct route without stopping until they reach it. Dragon turtles do **not** resent the call — they find it comforting.
  2. **Incite Attack (1 charge).** Forces a dragon turtle within 1 mile to attack a target (Wisdom save DC 15 or be compelled). It will **not** fight to the death — it flees if reduced below half its hit points.
  3. **Create Fog (1 charge).** A fog bank 1 mile in diameter around the orb; visibility limited to 30 feet. Moderate winds quickly dissipate it.
- **Automatic effect:** the orb **triggers Call Dragon Turtle every full moon** on its own.
- **Destruction:** appears fragile but is impervious to nearly all damage, including dragons' attacks and breath weapons. It can be destroyed **only by a special spell of unmaking.**

### The Orbs of Dragonkind (parent tradition, for reference)

- **Attunement required.** While attuned, an action + command word + **Charisma check (DC by color)**: success = control while attuned; failure = **Charmed** by the orb while attuned. Attunement breaks only when another takes the orb and wins a Charisma contest vs. the currently attuned wielder.
- **While Charmed:** cannot voluntarily end attunement; the orb casts **Suggestion** at will (save DC by color) toward the dragon-essence's desires.
- **Charmed & Suggestion DC by color:** Red 16 · Blue 15 · Green 14 · Black 14 · White 12.
- **Attunement properties (compulsions & boons) by color:**
  - **Red** — immunity to fire; +2 Cha; when contradicted (even when wrong), Cha save DC 16 or fly into a rage and dominate (not kill) the one who contradicted you; must hoard gold and never let it leave your possession.
  - **Blue** — immunity to lightning; +1 Str, +1 Cha; demands loyalty — a disobeyed order forces a Cha save DC 15 or you reject the defier forever; must hoard gems, especially sapphires.
  - **Green** — immunity to poison; +1 Int, +1 Cha; ignores those outside your inner circle and demands flattery within it — insufficient obsequiousness forces a Cha save DC 14 or you secretly "punish" them while keeping them close; recruits the intelligent and charismatic, and treats any who leave the circle as enemies.
  - **Black** — immunity to acid; +1 Str, +1 Con; turns you sadistic — a captive forces a Cha save DC 14 or you torture them without reason; obsessive collector of your culture's artifacts and relics.
  - **White** — immunity to cold; +1 Dex, +1 Con; withdrawn and reclusive, interacting freely only with a number of people equal to your Cha modifier (else a Cha save DC 12); must hoard gems, especially diamonds.
- **Charges:** 7; regains 1d4 + 3 at dawn. **Spells (spend charges while in control):**
  1. **Call Dragons (1)** — telepathic call, 400 miles; associated dragons failing a Cha save vs. DC travel directly to the orb; drawn dragons may be hostile for being compelled.
  2. **Intimidate Dragon (1)** — a single dragon within 1 mile; on a failed Cha save vs. DC it flees or negotiates for up to 1 hour, treating the wielder as a dragon of equal power.
  3. **Torment Dragon (1)** — psychic damage to a dragon in range (dice by color; half on a successful Cha save vs. DC).
  4. **Dominate Dragon (2)** — a single dragon; failed Cha save vs. DC = submits and obeys for 1 hour; a successful save blocks further domination for 24 hours; either way the dragon hates the wielder.
- **DC & Torment damage / range by color:** Red DC 16, 26d6 (90 ft) · Blue DC 15, 16d10 (120 ft) · Green DC 14, 22d6 (90 ft) · Black DC 14, 15d8 (90 ft) · White DC 12, 16d8 (90 ft).
- **Immune to the orbs entirely:** dragon deities (e.g., Tiamat).
- **Destruction:** impervious to most damage, including dragons' attacks and breath; destroyed only by a special spell of unmaking.
