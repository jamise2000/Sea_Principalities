# The Sahaugin

Race-specific reference for the sahaugin — the "shark-men" (also "shark-folk") of the deep. This file is the single source of truth for what sahaugin *are* and *can do*; it is expected to expand as the manuscript reveals more. For the copper-and-sulfur substance used to ward water against them, see `The_Anti-Sahaugin_Powder.md`; for their assault on Helm Island, see `Helm_Island.md`.

**Spelling:** always **sahaugin** (matching the manuscript's 70+ uses and `The_Anti-Sahaugin_Powder.md`). Do not use the variant "sahuagin" that appears in `Guide_to_Salinmoor.md`.

## Appearance
Man-like in outline but inhuman in every part: a wide, lipless mouth crowded with teeth, flat black eyes "empty of warmth," webbed feet and clawed, webbed hands. They fight with barbed spears and claw. In the water they are marked from a distance by an unnatural density of sharks (established Ch. 16, Ch. 19).

## Endurance ashore — the four-hour rule
Sahaugin can hold on land for **roughly four hours before they suffocate for want of the sea.** They hate the dry, and will not press far inland unless something drives them to it. This is the authoritative statement of the mechanic, given by Merrick — the party's sahaugin expert — at the fall of the Helm (Ch. 20).

- Paul's rougher field-guess in Ch. 19 — that they grow "weaker the farther from the water" — is a layman's approximation, not the true rule, and Jude deflates it in the moment ("The whole island counts as coastline, more or less"). Treat the four-hour limit (plus their aversion to dry ground), not a distance-based weakening, as canon.
- Practical consequence: high ground well clear of any coast is the safe posture against them; they cannot besiege an inland position for long.

## Terms
Characters call them **sahaugin**, **shark-men**, or **shark-folk** interchangeably. Merrick and Nado use "shark-men."

## History and wardings
- The sahaugin "once terrorized the Sea Principalities" and were **beaten out of these waters generations ago** — before Merrick's grandfather drew breath. A **manner of warding has held for over a century** since that last great war (see `Helm_Island.md`).
- A few still lurk **out in the deep, beyond the old wards.**
- The warding is bound up with the anti-sahaugin powder stockpiled on the Helm, and folk belief ties it to the **turtle dragons**, said to prey on sahaugin as they do on sharks (see `Helm_Island.md`).
- **The broken warding is the true emergency** of the Helm crisis: at the fall of the Helm a swarm of sahaugin — hundreds — came up out of the sea and dragged the corsair's complement (~200 Marines) under off the island itself, the first such attack in centuries. That the wards failed quietly, with no one the wiser, is the deeper alarm behind the hobgoblin garrison (Ch. 16–20).

## Personal stakes
- **Captain Nado**, Merrick's father, is rumored to have been taken by sahaugin in the deep years ago (body never recovered). See `characters/Merrick.md`.
