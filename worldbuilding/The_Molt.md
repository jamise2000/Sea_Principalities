# The Molt — a hidden cove of the Dragon Isles

**The Molt** is Owen Black's name for it — a smuggler's clipped word for the shed scales that carpet the place. A location reference and session set-piece: the reef-hidden cove on the seaward flank of the Dragon Isles where Owen stashes smuggled goods, once a turtle-dragon basking-and-nesting site, long disused. Built to fit established canon (`worldbuilding/The_Dragon_Isles.md`): two large islands four leagues north of Jetsom, in the **cold northern waters** of the Azure Sea, reef-fringed, unclaimed, shunned by shipping because of the turtle dragons. This is the seaward (Azure-facing) side — the side sailors never willingly approach.

## The Approach — reefs and the way in

The Dragon Isles show a hard face to the open sea: a coast of black, wave-battered rock with no obvious break in it, the kind of shore a navigator reads once and swears off. Perhaps a half-mile out, the water changes color — the deep grey-green of the northern Azure paling to a sick, bright turquoise over shallows — and that paleness is the warning. A **broken reef** runs the length of this shore a few fathoms down, close enough to the surface that the swell stands up and breaks white over it in long, ragged lines. Sailors who know the isles call this stretch **the Teeth**; most give it a full league's berth.

There is one gap. Owen lines it up on two marks: a **leaning sea-stack** shaped like a bent finger — locals would call it a monument if anyone came here to name it — standing a little off the point, and behind it, on the cliff, a **pale scar of rockfall** bright against the black stone. Bring the finger-stack to cover the scar, hold that line, and the reef opens in a channel barely two boat-lengths wide, dog-legging left where a submerged shelf would gut a hull that ran it straight. It is passable only near **high water** and only in a shallow-drafted boat; at low tide the channel shows its bones and the cove seals itself. This is the whole reason the place works as a stash — the approach cannot be blundered into, only known.

Inside the Teeth the sea goes abruptly quiet. The reef takes the swell and the water within lies flat and cold and startlingly clear, kelp standing up from the bottom in slow brown columns, the pale shapes of fish hanging in the green light beneath the keel.

## The Cove Itself

The cove is a near-enclosed basin, perhaps two hundred yards across, bitten into the island's flank — a rough horseshoe of cliff and fallen rock open only through the reef-gap behind. The walls are **dark basalt**, wet-black at the waterline where the tide has worn them hollow into undercuts and blowholes that boom softly on the bigger swells. Above the tideline the rock goes grey and lichened, streaked white with the droppings of seabirds; **guillemots and gulls nest the ledges thick and undisturbed** — a small detail, and a telling one, because nothing that nests a cliff will do it within reach of a living dragon.

Landmarks, going in:

- **The Shingle.** The head of the cove is not sand but a steep bank of **grey shingle and broken shell**, coarse and loud underfoot, sloping up to a tumble of storm-thrown boulders at the cliff base. This is the landing, such as it is — a boat runs its bow up the shingle and the crew wades the last few feet through water cold enough to ache.
- **The Basking Shelf.** To the left of the shingle a broad, flat apron of rock lifts a few feet above the high-water mark, tilted toward the sun — an obvious place for a great cold-blooded thing to have hauled out and lain. Its surface is **scored with long parallel gouges**, claw-drag worn soft at the edges now, and pocked with shallow, body-sized hollows polished smooth. Barnacles and black lichen have crept back across it; a **tide-pool has formed in the deepest wallow**, thick with weed. Nothing has warmed this stone in a long while.
- **The Nest Mounds.** Beyond the shelf, above the reach of the tide, sit two or three low **mounds of heaped rotted kelp, driftwood, and shell**, each wider than a longboat — old nesting middens, slumped and grassed over at the crowns with tough salt-sedge. Dig into one and it is cold and sour to the core; no warmth of decay, no shell fragments newer than seasons old.
- **The Arch and the Stash Cave.** At the right-hand horn of the cove the sea has driven a **low arch** through a spur of rock, passable by boat only at dead low water. Just inside and above it, reached by a scramble over boulders, is a **dry sea-cave** well above the highest tide — sand floor, a natural chimney venting the damp, out of the weather and out of sight of the water. **This is where Owen keeps his goods**: oilcloth-wrapped bales and casks stacked on driftwood pallets to keep them off the floor, a coil of line, a shuttered lantern on a ledge, and a stub of tallow candle marking the spot. He mortars nothing and marks nothing outside; the cave mouth reads as shadow from below.
- **The Cracked Scute.** Near the mouth of the stash cave, half-buried in the shingle, lies the cove's one true monument: a **single shed scale the size of a cart-wheel**, curled and cracked down the middle, too big to shift. Owen uses it as his back-bearing to find the cave in poor light.

## The Scales

The cove is **scattered throughout with the shed scales of turtle dragons** — the reason the shingle grinds so strangely underfoot, for much of it is not stone at all but scale-shard worn down by the sea. Whole scales lie among the boulders and up the tideline: the largest broad as a shield or a cartwheel, the common run of them palm- to platter-sized, all of them thick, faintly domed, and heavy as slate. Fresh, they would have been dark green-black with an oil-slick sheen; here they are **weathered dull, chalked with salt, filmed with lichen and limpet**, their colors gone to the grey of everything else. Many are cracked or delaminating in layers like old horn. They drift in the wrack-line, wedge in rock crevices, crunch and shift on the Basking Shelf.

Everything about them says **absence, and for a long time**. Nothing here is fresh — no new-shed scale still bright, no scorched rock or steam-warmth, no reek of musk beyond the ordinary salt-and-rot of any northern shore, no bones newly picked, no spoor. The seabirds, the barnacled shelf, the grassed-over middens, the tide-pool in the wallow: it all reads as a place that was, for years and years, a haunt of great creatures, and then — not so recently that any one thing marks the day, but plainly — stopped being one. The turtle dragons kept this cove once. They have not come to it in a long while.

## For the DM

- **Why it's abandoned (author's eyes, not the players').** The turtle dragons' presence across the isles has grown strange and thin — a quiet the wider world hasn't noticed and Owen reads only as good luck (empty water is safe water for a smuggler). The deeper cause is Cain Toli's seizure of the Orb of the Dragon Turtle and his mastering of Oceanum Perfide (`worldbuilding/The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`, `worldbuilding/The_Dragon_Isles.md`). Owen does not know this. He knows only that a cove ships fear has, for a season or two now, been reliably empty — and a smuggler does not question a gift like that too closely. Play his ease against the party's unease.
- **What Owen will and won't say.** True to form (`characters/Owen_Black.md`), he treats the whole place as a good story and a good berth — points out the finger-stack line, jokes about the reef, waves off the scales ("dragons haven't shown in ages, don't fret"). Pressed on *why*, he genuinely doesn't know, and deflects to the work at hand.
- **What a search turns up.** The stash cave (Owen's goods). Among the scales and middens: shed scales worth real coin to the right buyer (alchemists, armorers — cf. the "dragon-scale armor" already in play, Ch. 13); a few old, clean bones; nothing living larger than crabs and nesting birds. A careful eye on the Basking Shelf reads the wear as *years* of use and *seasons* of disuse.
- **The tension to hold.** This is a lair with the owner out. The reef that hides Owen's goods also seals the party in on a falling tide; the way out closes for hours at low water. Keep the finger-stack, the falling tide, and the one cart-wheel scale in the players' minds — and leave open the question of what would happen if the thing that shed it came home while they were ashore.
- **Suggested checks (optional).** Running the reef-gap: a hard Navigation/vehicle check, easier at high water with Owen aboard, punishing without him or at the wrong tide. Reading the cove's age: a Nature/Survival check to tell "recently left" from "long gone." Finding the stash unaided: a Perception check against the shadowed cave mouth and the cracked-scute bearing.

## Read-Aloud

**Clearing the reef (as the boat lines up the marks and runs the gap):**

> The open Azure runs deep grey-green off the bow — until it doesn't. A half-mile out the water pales to a bright, sickly turquoise, and along that paleness the swell stands up and breaks in long white ranks over rock you cannot see. Owen doesn't slow. He brings a bent finger of stone — a sea-stack, leaning off the point — to sit dead in front of a pale scar of rockfall on the black cliff behind, holds the two together, and steers straight for the surf. "Trust the marks, not your eyes," he says. The white water closes on both sides, close enough to wet the rail; the channel doglegs hard left where the sea boils over a shelf; and then, between one breath and the next, the noise falls away behind you. Ahead, the swell gone flat, lies a black-walled basin of still, cold, glass-clear water, and a shore that grinds pale under the tide.

**Stepping onto the shore (the Shingle, among the scales):**

> The bank underfoot is not sand and not quite stone. It shifts and clatters as you climb it — grey shingle shot through with broken plate, some of it curved, some domed, all of it worn dull as slate. Whole ones lie among the boulders: a few the size of a dinner-platter, one, half-sunk near the cliff, broad as a cart-wheel and split down its middle. Gulls and guillemots work the ledges overhead, loud and unbothered. On the flat apron of rock to your left the stone is scored with long parallel grooves, worn soft, and pocked with smooth hollows the size of a lain body — barnacled over now, a tide-pool green in the deepest of them. Whatever shed all this, and basked here, and heaped those grassed-over mounds beyond, it has been gone a long, long while.

## Battle-Map Key

Scale: the cove is roughly **200 yards** mouth to head; a horseshoe of cliff open only through the reef-gap behind. Reef line lies ~half a mile out from the mouth. Tide governs everything — the reef-gap and the Arch open near high water and seal at low.

1. **The Teeth (reef line).** Submerged reef across the whole mouth, breaking white; impassable except at #2. ~½ mile out.
2. **The Reef-Gap & finger-stack.** The one channel through, ~two boat-lengths wide, dog-legging left over a submerged shelf. Lined up by bringing the **leaning finger-stack** to cover the **pale rockfall scar** on the cliff. High water only; shallow draft only.
3. **The Inner Water.** Flat, cold, glass-clear; kelp columns from the bottom; ~150 yards of open basin from gap to shore.
4. **The Shingle (landing).** Steep grey shingle-and-scale bank at the head of the cove; the beaching point. Storm-boulders piled at the cliff base behind it.
5. **The Basking Shelf.** Flat rock apron a few feet above high water, left of the Shingle; claw-gouged, hollow-pocked, barnacled, with a weed-choked tide-pool in the deepest wallow. The clearest "age" tell.
6. **The Nest Mounds.** Two–three longboat-wide middens of rotted kelp, driftwood, and shell above the tideline beyond the shelf; slumped, grassed over, cold to the core.
7. **The Arch.** Low sea-arch through the right-hand horn of the cove; boat-passable only at dead low water.
8. **The Stash Cave.** Dry sea-cave above the highest tide, just inside/above the Arch (#7), reached by a boulder scramble. Owen's goods: oilcloth bales, casks on driftwood pallets, coiled line, a shuttered lantern, a candle-stub marker. Mouth reads as shadow from the water.
9. **The Cracked Scute.** Cart-wheel-sized shed scale, split and half-buried in the shingle near the mouth of #8; Owen's back-bearing to find the cave in poor light.
10. **Cliff ledges & undercuts.** Basalt walls all round, wet-black and hollowed at the waterline into undercuts and **blowholes** that boom on the bigger swells; upper ledges thick with undisturbed nesting seabirds.

**Tide clock to run at the table:** the party enters near high water (#2 and #7 open). As the tide falls, both seal — the reef-gap first — closing the cove for hours. Track it; the falling tide is the trap the set-piece turns on.

## Continuity & Naming

- **Name.** **The Molt** is the cove's name (Owen's smuggler's word for it, after the shed scales). "The Scale Cove" survives only as a plain descriptor if one is ever needed.
- **Placement.** Seaward (Azure-facing) flank of the Dragon Isles, inside the outer reef; the sheltered, Jerlea-facing sides of the isles are not described here. Consistent with the isles being reef-fringed, harbor-bearing, and shunned (`The_Dragon_Isles.md`).
- **Cold-water register.** Kelp, guillemots/gulls, grey shingle, aching-cold clear water — the cool northern end of the Azure, per the gazetteer's climate note and `The_Dragon_Isles.md`. Not the coral-warm tropics of the southern isles.
- **Cross-references.** `worldbuilding/The_Dragon_Isles.md`, `worldbuilding/The_Orb_of_the_Dragon_Turtle.md`, `characters/Owen_Black.md`.
