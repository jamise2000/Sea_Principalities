## A. How the transcripts read (mechanics of the source — read first)

These are the recurring reasons a passage is hard to turn into prose. They apply across nearly every file, so they're stated once here.

1. **Speaker tags don't map to people.** The `[SPEAKER_##]:` diarization tags are per-*voice*, not per-character. One tag routinely carries the **DM narrating**, an **NPC in-character**, and **the player's own out-of-character asides**, because they're one person at the table. **The tag-to-person mapping also flips between files** (e.g., SPEAKER_00 is the DM in the alchemist-visit files but a player-narrator in `Paul_hurries_toward_the_fire`). Never assume a tag = a fixed person across transcripts.

2. **Out-of-band table chatter is baked in.** Dice, rules/edition banter, snack-and-drink logistics, phone/family interruptions, and real-world movie refs sit inline with the story. A consolidated cut-list is in section **K**.

3. **A player was reading the wrong part.** In `Jude_travels_to_Jamises_office` (~L592) a player says "I think I was accidentally reading Paul's part," so some lines tagged **Jude** are actually **Paul's**. The 3rd-Wealsun office thread's Jude/Paul split needs a second look.

4. **The DM narrates subtext at the table.** Several passages are the DM openly telling a player what their character should intuit ("Jude is actually probing you for information… trying to figure your secrets out," `Jude_tries_to_convinced_Paul` ~L42–47; "this conversation has so much subtext and foreshadowing," `Jude_Paul_confer` ~L801–805). This is authorial intent spoken aloud — useful, but not dialogue.

## C. ★ The anti-sahaugin powder (a knot of contradictions)

This plot device is described many incompatible ways. Pin one answer to each:

1. **What it is.** "Copper… and sulfur, combined in a way few know" (`visit_Alchemist` L110–113, 180) **vs** "sulfur and iron would be death to us" (same file L171–172) **vs** the Alchemist later "identified it as **copper sulfate**… used the Suel name for it" (`Paul_goes_to_fetch_formula` L136–139). Is it copper+sulfur, copper sulfate, or copper+iron — and is the recipe secret or a known named compound?
2. **Who made it originally.** The **Duke of Berghof's** southwestern mountains (`visit_Alchemist` L186–195) **vs** "the **Phyraxians/Fieraxians**" (same lines). Discoverer vs maker?
3. **When the supply was cut off.** "20 years ago" / "40 years or longer → twenty, twenty" (`visit_Alchemist` L189; `Alchemy_shop` L141–144) — and the older debrief notes had "decades" / "25 years." Settle one date.
4. **How much is left / how long it lasts.** "25 barrels" (`Jude_Paul_confer` L201–210) vs "one barrel covers the entire stretch of Monmurg" (same L236); Paul personally holds "a sample… a whole keg… three kegs… a packet… a vial" (`fetch_formula` L176–186; `hurries` L430) — keg/packet/vial all used. Stores "lasting for 20 years because we hadn't gotten any" (`visit_Alchemist` L218) vs "diminished greatly" (`Alchemy_shop` L141). How much actually exists?
5. **How long to recreate it.** "Maybe fifty days" vs "three months is what I heard" (`Jude_Paul_confer` L197–198).
6. **How long it's been in use in the strait.** "20 years plus" vs "hundreds of years" (`Alchemy_shop` L94–97).
7. **The historical eradication.** The book passage dates the sahuagin culling to **"between 427 and 430"** and names **Pocra Sententia** as the staging fortress (`Passage` L454–459). Confirm these hard dates fit the series timeline. (Also: "150 years ago… the destruction of the sahuagin," `Alchemy_shop` L353 — 427–430 CY vs "150 years ago" need reconciling against your current year.)
8. **Cain's angle.** Cain "got the lease" on the larger powder store in the mid-bay fortress and "stopped shipments for some reason" (`Passage` L423–426). Motive unstated — is halting the powder part of the dragon scheme?
