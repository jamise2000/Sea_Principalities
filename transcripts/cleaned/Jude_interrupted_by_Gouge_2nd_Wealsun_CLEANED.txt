DM: Second of Wealsun, 11:30 p.m. Jude, deep in meditation trying to recover spell slots, is interrupted by the arrival of Paul and Gouge.

JUDE: [still half in trance] Whatever it is, it can wait — I'm still trying to concentrate.

DM: You'd need until about three in the morning to get everything back. That's not happening now — Gouge is here.

GOUGE: Not quite an embrace, Jude — but you're worth more than a handshake, at least. [reaching out a hand]

JUDE: [blinking out of the trance] Wait — what? What time is it?

GOUGE: Eleven thirty. We need to get moving.

JUDE: You couldn't have waited until one? I only had a couple more hours.

GOUGE: What happened with the towers — the West Tower, the South Tower?

PAUL: Not where you came from. We talked to the captain — he's going to move on the Western Tower, but he hasn't sent the attack yet.

PAUL: The plan we worked out with him is either going along the coastline and through a cavern into the Helm, or going over the wall — levitating up — into the sanctuary.

JUDE: We'd still want to approach that from the ocean side either way. I can levitate one of us up, or — we'll see.

GOUGE: We're taking a convoy down to the cove. From there, either along the coastline and under, through the cavern, into the Helm — or over the wall.

GOUGE: Could you send the homunculus ahead to search for the cave entrance? Save us an hour of searching on foot.

PAUL: It's made of clay — if it gets damaged out there, I don't want to lose it. It can't fly, only glide, though it can climb walls. It could search the coastline, at least.

GOUGE: Fine — it searches the coastline. My Ranger and I can scout ahead in the meantime, make sure the way's clear.

PAUL: Going straight through the front door of the tower and into the keep — through the front — that's almost suicide.

GOUGE: No, no — that's not the plan. The diversion pulls their eyes to the front gate. We use that opening to take the wall from the coastline — the same spot where we'd be searching for the cave anyway.

PAUL: So while we're over there, could the homunculus fly up and check something for us? The captain described a route up the wall that leads to the inner sanctum — if we want to reach Fairwind, that's the way. Up the wall at that spot, then along to the tower, and then it cuts to the right.

GOUGE: [to Jude] Honestly, I'm not surprised I caught you mid-meditation — I figured four hours was pushing it, and I was hoping to get here a little later than I did. Any trouble on your end getting here?

JUDE: No, it was clear.

GOUGE: Here's where things stand on my end. The twenty or thirty hobgoblins who fled the tower — the ones we didn't kill — are still in the keep. They still hold the garrison and the lighthouse over there. We took the tower. My man there is keeping a signal going so our own people know to stay clear of it, and we'll be watching for the hobgoblins to try to circle back from behind.

PAUL: So, if we move earlier than planned — get into position, then have the captain send the attack on the other beacon and demonstrate at the front gate —

GOUGE: The demonstration's just to pull the guards off the side walls, so we can get up.

PAUL: Right. The captain's plan was to take the tower first, then send fifty men once we're in position, and maneuver in front with fires and a battering ram to buy time — without wasting troops. That should pull the garrison's attention to the front gate and clear some passageways for us.

GOUGE: Good. [lowering his voice, glancing toward the captain of the household guard] We should keep the rest of this between us. I don't know if Fairwind's good or turned, but I don't want to say it in front of him.

PAUL: You think Fairwind's compromised?

GOUGE: I suspect it. He may have broken the protective spell himself.

JUDE: Because of the Sahaugin? Or something else?

GOUGE: No hard evidence — but the suspicion runs high. I have my own instructions on this.

PAUL: You didn't mention you had suspicions.

GOUGE: I had them. We all should, given his rank and how convenient the timing is. We just haven't had the chance to talk it through.

JUDE: Were you sent here to assassinate him?

GOUGE: I was sent to bring him in, not kill him — that's the difference. If it were the other way, I could've done it easily enough while he was sleeping. Right now I'm just trying to figure out what actually happened.

PAUL: So — can we take him alive?

GOUGE: We're going to have to. If he's turned, that's a political disaster either way, and we need him as a prisoner — someone who can tell us how to undo whatever's broken, and re-establish the wards. First step's getting the Sahaugin away from the island.

JUDE: Why are they even here? That protection's held for centuries.

GOUGE: That's the question. He should know — the only working theory is that he had some kind of arrangement with them, and it fell apart. You wouldn't happen to know anything about merfolk operating in these waters?

PAUL: No, but the Ranger with you probably would — he knows this island. I don't swim well enough to have gone looking myself, and I've never even seen where they'd live.

GOUGE: Fair enough. Let's get moving and find out. [to Jude] Give me fifteen minutes to get my gear together — meet you at the front gate.

PAUL: We'll stay close to the coastline, use whatever cover there is. If it comes to it, the Ranger can throw up a fog bank to buy us time.

GOUGE: We haven't even seen your disguises yet.

PAUL: They're rough — hopefully you won't need them where we're going, but they're ready if you do.
