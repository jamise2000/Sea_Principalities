DM: Sometimes the greatest failures are remembered as well as the greatest victories. And so it was true for Folsom's performance of "The Brewing Storm" at the Marine barracks in Monmurg City. His admonition of the sea princes — all of them: Lord Jamis (I mean Prince Jeon), Prince Fairwind, Prince Flotsam, Prince Jetsam, and Prince Toli — would be etched in the minds of all those Marines, who at the time grumbled and seemed angry at such thoughts, particularly because of their great patriotic feelings toward the sea principality.

However, as the days and weeks rolled on, rumors of Fairwind's treachery at Helm Island, the sightings of the Sea Ghost, and the actions of the Toli reinforced Folsom's prediction. The Marines began to realize that Folsom had had insight into what was coming their way, and they grudgingly accepted the wisdom of what he had told them.

There was a storm brewing, and it was not just the Duke of Gradsul's doing — the jealousy and infighting of the sea princes added to the confusion and to the country's struggles. Soon they realized that Folsom had been correct: there was a problem outside the principalities, but there were many problems within as well, and the troubles of the nation came not just from its enemies, but from its supposed friends.

The Marines talked to one another about the song and its meaning, and in time they began to wish they could speak with Folsom and learn what else he knew. Thus began Folsom's career — and infamy — as a bard of renown.
