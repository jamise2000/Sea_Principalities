[Speaker Key: DM = Dungeon Master (narration) | JUDE = Jude's player | PAUL = Paul Revero's player]

DM: Fourth of Wealsun. Jude and Paul Revero, after a day of training in the arcane arts — Paul has actually learned the cantrip Mending — begin walking up the stairs out of the dungeon, where Jude was trying to teach him Light. Their conversation continues as Paul heads back to his room and Jude heads back to Ferdin's bar.

PAUL: Do you see—

JUDE: What do you care?

PAUL: I guess we know each other now, right? [or similar opener]

JUDE: Yeah, sometimes.

PAUL: Sometimes — mostly. I'm an elf, you ask me the same questions — but are you depressed?

JUDE: No.

PAUL: Have you ever... sagged? Like, are you tired?

JUDE: Well, yeah, sometimes.

PAUL: [studying Jude] You look morally young — maybe younger than me, and more world-weary than anyone I've ever seen.

JUDE: So that's a compliment, somehow?

PAUL: It's perfectly paced — more than anyone else I've ever met.

JUDE: And you're asking me if I'm going to end the world?

PAUL: You're focusing on the negative.

JUDE: His eyes — focusing on them, or—

PAUL: I just said you did great. I mean, you got some bruises — I hit you with a brass bowl.

JUDE: What the fuck?

PAUL: You broke like five chairs.

JUDE: I didn't break five chairs. I broke one chair.

PAUL: You used your Mending spell five separate times — you slammed like five chairs trying to cast Light.

JUDE: No, it was a brass bowl, and I smashed your knees, and you broke your face.

PAUL: You mended it.

JUDE: Yeah, sure did.

PAUL: Look, you've got this "thing," okay — and this thing is going to get you into deep shit, whether you're an assassin, a rogue, or a mage. You've got to let that go, okay?

JUDE: "The thing"? The thing?

PAUL: Yeah, you know — like, "mommy's dead, daddy never loved me—"

JUDE: You don't know that.

PAUL: Okay — he knows mommy's dead.

JUDE: I never told him that.

PAUL: He knows that — everyone knows that.

JUDE: Oh, yeah, I forgot — everyone knows the rumor mill. You wear a mask, you're "mysterious," you don't want people seeing your face — okay, that's fine, I haven't even brought any of that up until now. Now that you're my pupil, that means I'm going to poke and prod and kick you, figuratively and literally, to get you to be better than you are. If you can't handle that, maybe you should go work with Gouge instead — but I promise you, he's going to take you out on his ship and have you gut and skin living creatures as part of his idea of training.

PAUL: Yeah, I figured.

JUDE: You can still choose.

PAUL: So is the equivalent of that... blowing someone's brains out with a magic wand?

JUDE: Yeah, but I'm not teaching you that — I'm not teaching you how to blow people up. That's not what this is.

PAUL: We'll suppose that. Just — magic wand, magic wand.

JUDE: Right.

PAUL: So you're going to have me, what, hold up your toad familiar?

JUDE: No — first of all, we're going to use your fingers. I was using a wand.

PAUL: And secondly?

JUDE: Is it that easy? I'm pretty sure if you took a Toli captive, he wouldn't even make it down the hallway — you'd probably just waste him.

PAUL: Yeah.

JUDE: Okay, so that's my point.

PAUL: Isn't that what you wanted to do anyway?

JUDE: No!

PAUL: Yes, you wanted to—

JUDE: No — wasn't that what you wanted to do?

PAUL: I didn't waste him until things hit the fan.

JUDE: You wanted to waste him — yeah, you said you wanted to kill him because, you know, you figured the bad stuff hadn't happened yet, like "let's just kill him and leave him" — and then when something bad did happen, I killed him and left him.

PAUL: No — the group said we might need him, but you just decided to kill him.

JUDE: What are you talking about? I didn't kill him until the whole thing broke out — you remember the dim-witted guy who was hanging out with Gouge's group, who stopped halfway down the walkway and got trapped? It's only because of the idiocy of the guards on the other end that we didn't all get dropped into the water with that dumbass.

PAUL: I remember.

JUDE: And who saved you?

PAUL: Please — that's right, your levitation spell.

JUDE: No.

PAUL: When do I get to learn that one, huh — levitate?

JUDE: Second level. You're going to take a lot more brass bowls to the shins before you get to levitate. Can you handle it?

PAUL: I'm here, aren't I? I'm not complaining.

JUDE: True.

PAUL: You want to see me complaining?

JUDE: Oh, you mean there's more to come?

PAUL: Oh, yeah. Oh, yeah.

JUDE: All right, let's see it then.

PAUL: Well, you're not busting my shins right now — we're just walking up these damn stairs.

JUDE: So what you're saying is, when you get emotionally unstable, you're going to react badly?

PAUL: Oh, god, here we go.

JUDE: I'm telling you — some of these spells require concentration to maintain. If you're going to get all emotional—

PAUL: Do I sound like — do you hear tears?

JUDE: Yes. Yes. Yes. And yes.

PAUL: Well, clearly you've been abused as a child. There are no tears.

JUDE: You don't have to have tears to have a temper tantrum, okay.

PAUL: Well, if I was having a temper tantrum, I feel like I'd be bouncing off the walls.

JUDE: Honestly, I'm not even sure Paul Revero has tear ducts.

PAUL: Yeah. Look — look, I don't care if you cry, but I'm not seeing you cry.

JUDE: Yeah, you can't point at a face mask and say, "Oh, do you see tears?"

PAUL: I'm sitting right here — tears, let's see them.

JUDE: Turn your elf ears on.

DM: I don't know if you want to pause this or keep going — the two of you walk up the stairs. How are you doing, as you go?
