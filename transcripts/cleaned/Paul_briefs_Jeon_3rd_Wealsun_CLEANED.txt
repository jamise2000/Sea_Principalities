[Speaker Key: DM = Dungeon Master (narration, voicing Prince Jeon) | PAUL = Paul Rivera's player]

DM: Third of Wealsun, in the evening, as Paul Rivera returns to Monmurg on the corsair from the adventure on Helm Island. So, the group returns and docks at the Royal Docks — not normally where these ships dock, but for whatever reason you were given permission, most likely because you were aboard. After docking, an attendant tells you and Jude that you're both wanted for a debrief — Jude with Lord Jamis, and you with Prince Jeon. Lord Jamis doesn't have the authority to debrief you, which is part of why it's split that way — Jeon wants to hear what you observed directly from you. You're led up the private stairway, past several guards, into the palace — your home too — and to Jeon's office. When you enter, he's already there, looking concerned, studying a map of the outer islands, Songmor Bay to the north, Jerly Bay to the south.

DM (as Jeon): Paul — sit down, please.

PAUL: [sits] I heard there was some trouble on Helm Island.

DM (as Jeon): I've gotten reports that the ship we sent you on was overwhelmed.

PAUL: There were three hundred Marines on that ship — all gone. Maybe a hundred made it off.

DM (as Jeon): And it was the Sahaugin?

PAUL: Yeah, and there were also hobgoblins that had taken over the island.

DM (as Jeon): Tell me more of that.

PAUL: Fairwind is a traitor.

DM (as Jeon): How do you know this?

DM: Is Jamis in the room with you?

PAUL: [checking] No — just me and Jeon.

DM: Obviously he wants your point of view first, and in private, which is — maybe not suspicious, but it tells you he's not fully trusting everyone else right now. He wants to know what you know before he says anything more.

PAUL: I explained to him: we sailed to the island, ready in case it had been taken over. We dropped fifty Marines on one shore —

DM: Which one?

PAUL: The southern beacon. We then docked our ship in the harbor — me, Jude, and twenty other Marines got onto the dock, started scouting ahead, and suddenly came under fire from the towers above. We had to storm them. Jude and I thought about turning back, but we looked behind us and saw Sahaugin tearing into the ship, swarming it — everyone left on it was gone. I'm pretty sure it sank in the harbor and everyone aboard died. We stormed inside and got to the barracks. We met up with some Marines stationed on Helm Island who were resisting there — they knew nothing about what had happened to Fairwind.

DM (as Jeon): Those were Fairwind's men?

PAUL: Yeah.

DM (as Jeon): You said Fairwind was a traitor, though.

PAUL: Yeah, here's where it gets interesting — they didn't know anything about how the hobgoblins had gotten into the Helm, or whether any of Fairwind's own Marines were still fighting. They'd just been holed up, holding the line — under siege themselves. We linked up with them, and I made a plan: a group of us would storm the Helm by going over the wall, while the rest created a diversion. I don't know exactly what happened to them, but we snuck inside and around the Helm, to where we figured Fairwind would be.

DM (as Jeon): The inner sanctum?

PAUL: Yeah, the inner sanctum. I scouted ahead and could see and hear him — dealing.

DM (as Jeon): Dealing with whom?

PAUL: The Toli.

DM (as Jeon): The Toli?

PAUL: Yeah — he was making deals with them, standing in the same room, talking with them.

DM (as Jeon): Wait — there were Toli on the Helm at that time?

PAUL: Yep.

DM (as Jeon): How do you know they were Toli?

PAUL: I saw them, and we captured one.

DM (as Jeon): Interesting — go on. So you actually witnessed Lord Fairwind talking with them, walking around the same room, making deals. What were they discussing?

PAUL: Removing the powder from the Helm — the substance that keeps the Sahaugin away. And the Toli said something like, "We noticed you took most of it for yourself." I think Fairwind was trying to split off some for himself — that was his angle. He'd removed most of the powder from Helm Island.

DM (as Jeon): And that's how the Sahaugin were able to swarm the island.

PAUL: Right. They said he had four barrels of the stuff. So after we captured Fairwind, I put him under arrest — he made a big fuss about it, tried to claim innocence. We took one Toli alive. We moved down into a secret cave system, previously unknown, along the back coastline behind the keep. We were heading down some stairs along the side of the cave when we got spotted — we killed the Toli prisoner and dumped him over the side into the water, since he was dead weight. Fairwind, while trying to get to the front, pushed me — or got pushed — over the side. I fell into the water; I got pulled back up, but he ended up over the side and escaped. Don't know what happened to him — could be dead, could be alive. We fought our way down, killed the hobgoblins, built a raft, and sailed out of there. We managed to take four barrels with us, enough to keep the Sahaugin off.

DM (as Jeon): How many barrels were there originally?

PAUL: Twenty-five, I think, was our count — we could only take four; the rest were gone.

DM (as Jeon): Who else witnessed Fairwind speaking with the Toli agents?

PAUL: Unfortunately, nobody else — just me.

DM (as Jeon): So you're the only one with a direct, definitive witness account of this betrayal. Your companions — how much have you told them about it?

PAUL: About his treason? I told them he's a traitor, that he was making deals with the Toli — that's really it, I didn't go into specifics. But they believed me over him.

DM (as Jeon): I mean, it doesn't entirely make sense — because what he apparently told all of them was simply that he'd had his own men give up their arms and be held, in cells or whatever.

PAUL: We never found his Marines, but they basically let the hobgoblins just walk in.

DM (as Jeon): That doesn't sound very fair to him, does it — but it's your opinion that Fairwind allowed the hobgoblins to take the Helm.

PAUL: The hobgoblins were working with the Toli, we believe.

DM (as Jeon): Yes — which is strange, even though the Duke of Berghof is known to employ hobgoblin mercenaries. So there may be some involvement from him as well. And these hobgoblins were red-skinned?

PAUL: I don't know if that's how it works — I've never seen one before.

DM (as Jeon): I have — they live in the Crystalmist Mountains, which the Duke of Berghof's duchy backs up against, so he has dealings with them. They're easier to deal with than most humanoids, actually, but this is very disconcerting that they were used here.

PAUL: That's basically it. Do you have any idea how they got onto the island?

DM (as Jeon): We never found out specifically.

PAUL: We had to get out of there and report back — I didn't know if you were sending reinforcing ships our way or anything.

DM (as Jeon): We received a signal from the southern beacon — I assume that was the first squad you dropped off.

PAUL: Pretty sure, yeah — there was danger.

DM (as Jeon): They told us not to send any more ships until things were clear. I assume that's why — we got that signal just after you landed, so I assume your ship being attacked is what warned us off, but from what I'm hearing it's all right for us to proceed now.

PAUL: The Sahaugin had — one of the barrels was with us, about—

DM (as Jeon): Like you said, you don't know what happened to those Marines in the barracks. There are probably still hobgoblins on the island.

PAUL: There was this big ship — you could sometimes see it in the mist, breaking through the fog — that's where they were moving off the powder, and storing most of their gear, I guess. But that ship left.

DM (as Jeon): That was the Toli ship?

PAUL: It was huge — called the Sea Ghost. You'd heard people say Cain Toli was its captain — we think he was the captain, or at least that's what Fairwind said.

DM (as Jeon): No doubt that's where the hobgoblins came from, or at least the most likely explanation — we'd seen them send these ships back and forth to the caves. That's where they were storing the powder?

PAUL: Loading it, yeah.

DM (as Jeon): Well, I'm going to have Lord Jamis send a flotilla of troops and ships to Helm Island, and we're going to retake the smugglers' caves and recover any remaining powder. We have the area blockaded for now. I want to talk to you a bit about the companions you went with — I understand most were Marines.

PAUL: Yeah — there were some men of note. Merrick, whom Lord Jamis recommended, and two agents with me: Gouge, whom I've heard of in passing, and Jude, a wizard for hire.

DM (as Jeon): Jude — we've questioned him before, had meetings with him. What are your thoughts on these men?

PAUL: Pretty solid — good at what they do.

DM (as Jeon): Are they the only three you discussed Fairwind's treachery with?

PAUL: No, almost all of the Marines in the barracks who survived witnessed parts of it too.

DM (as Jeon): How many Marines were there with you, specifically?

PAUL: Five, I think — Merrick, Gouge, Jude, Folsom, and Tyrus. That's it.

DM (as Jeon): So two others, beyond Merrick, Gouge, and Jude — and those were just regular Marines?

PAUL: Yeah, just regular Marines.

DM (as Jeon): All right — they'll need to be debriefed too, and quietly. I'll leave that to Jamis, though they report to him anyway. Merrick, though, could be commended for this — honorably, I'd assume.

PAUL: What do you mean?

DM (as Jeon): He's obviously loyal to me. Did you choose him?

PAUL: Jamis recommended him.

DM (as Jeon): They're loyal — I don't think they'd cause trouble.

PAUL: I doubt it.

DM (as Jeon): What about Gouge? I worry about him.

PAUL: Gouge is fine — makes quick decisions under pressure. Not much of a threat unless he's threatened himself. Professional, I'd say.

DM (as Jeon): And what's your sense of this Jude character?

PAUL: He's a pretty good wizard — the job you gave him was to protect me, and he did that just fine. They're all very good fighters.

DM (as Jeon): Good. Now, Paul — I do have something I want to speak to you about, since you brought up your investigation into your past, your family history, your father. I've been thinking on some things. Please take this as a suggestion regarding your personal path forward, and how you can be most useful to your family in these times of need.

I've noticed you've taken to what could be called the ledger domain — spycraft, in a way — and I think that's quite useful. But, to be honest, I knew your father, Grayson, when we were young men, and I was very impressed by his intelligence — and your mother's, too. They were both brilliant people. He talked often about the yellow substance, and how our state would only truly be free once we could produce it ourselves. I don't know if you're aware, but that compound was given to us by the Duke of Berghof — and he's stopped supplying it. We've been running low for decades. What was left on Helm Island, even before it was taken, would only have lasted another three years. We've been desperate for it, and now that it's been removed, we have no time and no way to produce it ourselves — we simply don't have the knowledge. This is what Grayson recognized two decades ago, and he was committed to changing it. Unfortunately, the greatest alchemists all belong to Keoland —

PAUL: Or the Duke of Berghof.

DM (as Jeon): Exactly. What I'm asking is — would you be willing to dedicate yourself to figuring this out? I'm asking because I believe you're capable of it. Could you study the creation of this substance — pursue it as a personal quest, find a way to make it?

PAUL: Yeah — this would divert me from my main path, and I'd have to delve into the arcane arts. Are you asking if I'd be willing to do that?

DM (as Jeon): Most Oeridians are reluctant to go there; however, I see now that our neglect of the magical arts has led us to this point. We need to develop wizards of our own.

PAUL: Yeah, we do.

DM (as Jeon): This brings me to my second point — do you feel Jude is someone you'd be willing to study under?

PAUL: Oh, yeah — he's a good wizard, very powerful. He could teach me.

DM (as Jeon): Excellent — I've already asked Lord Jamis to propose that arrangement to Jude. From what I understand, Jude is a very transactional person, but right now we have things he wants. I think if he could teach you the magical arts and share his knowledge, it would be very advantageous for our family — and, to be honest, having a new prince of Monmurg with wizardly abilities would be advantageous in itself.

So I'm charging you: look into the arcane arts, as your father and mother did before you. I'll give you full leeway in this — do as you wish, as long as it doesn't bring harm to the city or our family's name. There are magics that are illegal in Keoland and in the Duchy of Berghof — necromancy comes to mind first — but as long as no one else knows of it, I'm not going to restrict your knowledge or where you seek it.

DM (as Jeon): And you have my full permission to use any resources in the Royal Library for your research. If there are esoteric components you need to acquire — I understand wizards have a need for such things — I'll do what I can to help. The Great Market draws wizards from around the world, and even in this difficult year, I assume we can find most of what you'll need, at least for now.

I'm going to have Jamis talk to Jude about tutoring you — I'd like to see that proceed within the next two weeks. We have months before our supply runs out entirely. There are other locations we can look into — if Fairwind did indeed betray us, he likely has a large amount of it stored somewhere on his own island.
