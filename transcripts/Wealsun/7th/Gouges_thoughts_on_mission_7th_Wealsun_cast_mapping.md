## Point of the conversation
Gouge takes stock the morning after. He wonders whether the crew was **sent to Redshore deliberately to be killed** — someone ordering Owen to take them and get rid of them — or whether Owen acted alone (which he doubts). Working it through with the DM, he lands on a reading of the pieces:
- The **Earl of Redshore is Monmurg's enemy**, so walking into his castle/power is dangerous. The men-at-arms were **hunting Owen** and reacting to the **six dead in the chart room**, so Owen is *not* clearly in league with the Earl — perhaps just tolerated.
- The **blockade** is really a **Gradsulian embargo to punish Monmurg**: merchant ships are corralled at Redshore and forced to dump cargo cheap, while Monmurg starves for goods. The **burning of Porvin** helped set it up.
- The Monmurg navy can't just smash the three men-of-war because it's being **blocked by the controlled turtle dragons** (mist/fog, sinking ships) — the amulet thread.
- **Why Owen turned on them:** Owen got **too chummy with Folsom** and spilled a "gold mine" of intel (about Cain Toli, his island, and the artifact); realizing Folsom had talked to the others and that Gouge/Merrick were **agents**, Owen tried to cover his tracks by having them killed. Their actual mission (from Lord Jamis) was simply **to find out what Owen knew** — and, thanks to Folsom, they did.
- **Gouge's conclusion:** forget the castle and the mercenaries — this is beyond them; **get the intelligence back to Lord Jamis** so those who can break the blockade can act. "Let us be the pawns that we are."
