## 1. Structure and emotional weaknesses

**S1. Nearly the whole chapter is recalled summary.** The tower walk is a strong frame, with one floor per topic. But what happens on each floor is mostly a briefing: the debrief tally, Fairwind, Keoland, food, the powder, Paul, Cain Toli, the artifact, the slave trade, the Brotherhood, the wine. The densest paragraphs are line 31 ("He stood at the window a while and let the rest of the news go by him…") and line 33 ("The powder was another matter…"). The powder paragraph alone runs about 400 words of figures and logistics. Little happens in the present beyond walking up stairs.

**S2. The chapter's real throughline arrives late.** The emotional spine is Jude's growing suspicion of the man who just gave him everything: the key held a breath too long, Jamis afraid for the first time, the Azane wine Jamis never questions. That only takes hold in the last fifth. The key motif from the opening and Jamis's grip on it (line 51: "the key that Jamis had held out across the table and not let go of at once") could be threaded through the earlier floors, so the closing "better bargain" verdict feels earned rather than summed up.

**S3. The Paul section is the most important beat for the book, and it's in the middle.** Jude's role as Paul's teacher and Jamis's spy, at once, is the premise of Part Two. It's handled well but quietly (lines 43–53), then passes. One short return to it on the roof, tied to the "better bargain" weighing, would carry it forward. The closing line half does this already.

**S4. Overlap with the new Chapter Five.** Both chapters have Jude privately sizing Paul up. Chapter Five is about hunger and conscience, and Chapter One is about patronage and spying, so they don't conflict. But neither mentions the other's angle. In particular, Chapter Five never mentions that Jude is supposed to report on Paul to Jamis. One line in Chapter Five would tie them together.

## 2. Continuity and canon

**C1. The twenty Marines did not come home (major).** Book One, Chapter 42 is clear: only the six survivors rafted home with four barrels. Gouge sent two corsairs on to reinforce "our people at the barracks." Chapter One says otherwise in three places:
- Line 21: "the twenty who had been ashore with Paul and Jude brought home alive; the thirty put off at the Southern Beacon come through with Gouge."
- Line 49: "He had seen him take command of twenty frightened Marines on the Helm's docks and bring every one of them home."
- Line 7: Gouge drinking at Ferd's with "the Marines who had come home with him."

Chapter Four has it right ("We don't know how many of them lived after we left"). Line 7 also affects:
- Book 2 Ch. 3, line 7: "where Gouge and the Marines lay snoring among the benches"
- `characters/Ferd.md`, *In Book Two*, the "night of 3 Wealsun" bullet ("Gouge and the Marines back from the Helm drink at Ferd's").

Suggested fix: make them Monmurg Marines, or simply "the men," that Gouge is drinking with. That's consistent with Book One's *Cider at the Barracks*. Reword lines 21 and 49 so the twenty and the thirty were alive at the barracks when the raft left.

**C2. Folsom's name.** Line 21 has Jude unable to recall "the bard whose name still would not come back to him." Later that same day, in Chapter Four, Jude names Folsom without effort ("Tyrus and Folsom still standing a little straighter when he spoke to them"). Either Chapter One drops the forgetfulness or Chapter Four says "the bard."

**C3. The homunculus.** Line 49 says Jude "was satisfied that the creature was Paul's own work." The new Chapter Five has him still unsure: "Whether Paul had truly made that homunculus himself, from one book and in a day, Jude still could not say. Jamis could not say either." Chapter Five also gives Jude "in a day," which is Paul's private knowledge from Chapter Two. Recommended: fix Chapter Five to match Chapter One. Chapter Three's "You did make that yourself? You are sure?" can stay as Jude probing.

**C4. "Before Cain Toli took him" (line 33) — needs author ruling.** "…the shop Lord Phranck had called on before Cain Toli took him." Nothing in canon has Cain Toli seizing Lord Phranck. `Gouge.md` ends the Phranck affair with the artifact revelation, not a capture. Was this intended, or should it be cut?

**C5. Geography on the roof (line 83) — needs author ruling.** The sun rises "out of the land behind the city," and line 29 places the city east of the tower. Helm Island is then "far out to the east, low and dark on the water," which would put it over land. Canon puts Helm at the center of the strait, which the tower's library window faces north over. Which direction does Helm lie from the headland?

**C6. Teaching in the tower (line 41).** "This was where he would teach Paul Revero, if he taught him anywhere." Under the 2026-10-24 ruling the lessons are in Paul's sitting room. The "if" covers it, and it reads as quiet irony. No change needed unless wanted.

**C7. A fix for Chapter Six's tower problem is already here.** Line 7: "Jamis had said that someone would take him to the tower tomorrow and see it furnished." Chapter Six's "Jude had spent the first part of the afternoon being walked through a tower that was now his" could become that second, official visit, with Jamis's man seeing to the furnishing. That resolves the open item logged in `transcripts/manuscript_divergences.md` without cutting it.

**Checked and consistent:** the 4 Wealsun timeline, handing off cleanly to Ch. 3 (dawn on the roof, then Ferd's, then his room); two hundred Marines lost; 25/4/21 barrels; supply cut off about forty years ago; Jamis as admiral; Zafar Azane as Lady Jamis's brother and head of the Office; the artifact treated as rumor; Kimbertus out of Greyhill; Ogmotton's ruined tower in Idee; Paul's paternal grandmother alive with some magic of her own.

## 3. Prose and style

**P1. Grammar slips:**
- Line 109: "There was a sahaugin's gills" should be "There were."
- Line 105: "Ogmotton had given him a ruin and asked nothing, just loyalty and to share in a hopeless situation" contradicts itself ("nothing" versus "loyalty") and doesn't parse.
- Line 11: "A palace could not be made as safe but at great expense" is awkward.

**P2. Repetition:** the pirates-before-the-nation idea comes up twice, at line 9 ("before there were any Sea Princes to call it a nation") and line 39 ("when the principalities were a pack of sea-wolves and not yet a nation"). "It would keep" and "let it go" also work as a repeated dismissal, which is fine once but appears several times.

**P3. Clunky phrases and capitalization:**
- "waiting for his numerous books" (line 103)
- "Inquisitor" is capitalized here (line 65) but lowercase in Chapter Four.

**Tics:** the usual offenders are almost absent: one "a great deal," one "something like," and no "particular," "solid," "exchange," "utterly" or "wrongness."

## 4. Markdown and mechanics

**M1. Remembered dialogue is in italics.** All of Jamis's recalled lines are set as *"…"* (lines 31, 33, 43, 47, 51, 65, 67, 71, 75, 87, 91, 97). The house rule reserves italics for unvoiced thought and puts speech in quotation marks. Line 33's *forty years, near enough* is italics with no quotes at all. They should become plain quoted speech. Line 53 (*Careful, then…*) and line 99 (*He may be playing them all…*) are genuine thought and are correct.

**M2. Ship names — needs house decision.** *Sea Ghost* is in italics. That's standard for ship names, but it technically breaks the "italics only for thought" rule. Book One probably does the same.

**Otherwise clean:** the `##` Part heading, one `#` chapter title, `###` scene breaks, no dice or spell levels, and only lived-in magic terms ("a small light at his fingertip," "a ward or two").

## What works

- The tower as the chapter's structure is excellent: each floor gets a topic, and the roof gets the verdict.
- The opening image of a key that stays cold "as though the iron had been kept somewhere the warmth of the city never reached" quietly foreshadows Jamis.
- Jude reading the man who owns the building, "how very little a man like that ever let you find," is sharp.
- Jamis's one moment of fear lands because Jude has never seen it before.
- The wine thread is the best thing in the chapter: a blockade-proof road that "ran in two directions," and a spymaster who has "arranged never to find out."
- The final weighing of Ogmotton's ruin against Jamis's sound tower gives the title real irony.
